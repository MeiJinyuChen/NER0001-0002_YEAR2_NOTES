
# 1. Models of Sensory Integration

## A. Visual Dominance Theory

### Core Idea

- Vision is the dominant human sense.
- In multisensory conflict, visual information overrides other senses.

### Supporting Illusions

1. **McGurk Effect**
    - Visual lip movements alter perceived speech sound.
    - “Ba-ba” heard as “Tha-tha”
2. **Ventriloquist Effect**
    - Sound location is perceived closer to visual stimulus.

### Limitation

- Cannot explain cases where non-visual senses dominate.

## B. Modality Appropriateness Hypothesis

### Core Idea

- The sensory modality most appropriate to the task dominates.

### Principles

- Vision dominates spatial tasks.
- Audition dominates temporal tasks.

### Example

**Double Flash Illusion**
- One visual flash + two auditory beeps → perception of two flashes.
- Auditory temporal precision overrides vision.

### Key Researchers

- Welch & Warren (2011)
- Shams, Kamitani & Shimojo (2000)

## C. Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) Model

### Core Idea

- Sensory cues are weighted according to their reliability.

### Reliability Formula

Reliability = 1 / σ²
- σ² = variance of cue estimate = sqrt of std.
- Lower variance = greater reliability

### Predictions of MLE

- Combined estimate lies closer to the more reliable cue.
- Integrated estimate is more precise than either cue alone.

### Supporting Evidence

**Ernst & Banks (2002)**
- VR manipulation of visual-haptic block size conflict
- Added visual noise to alter reliability
- Human perception closely matched MLE predictions

# 4. The Correspondence Problem

## Definition

How does the brain determine which sensory cues belong together?

## Principles Used

1. **Temporal proximity**
2. **Spatial proximity**
3. **Knowledge of common cause**
4. **Innate biases**

### Examples

- Mirror experiments (Helbig & Ernst, 2007)
- Bouba/Kiki effect
- Rubber Hand Illusion (Botvinick & Cohen, 1998)

# 5. Neural Basis of Multisensory Integration

## 5.1 Mechanism

### A. Traditional View

- Primary sensory cortices are unimodal.
- Integration occurs in convergence zones (e.g. PPC, STS, PMC)

### B. Updated View

- Primary cortices may also receive multisensory inputs.
- Brain may be fundamentally multisensory.

## 5.2 Plasticity and Sensory Deprivation

### Blindness and Visual Cortex Recruitment

- Congenitally blind individuals use visual cortex for non-vision tasks:
    - Braille reading
    - Echolocation

### Rapid Adult Plasticity

#### Merabet et al. (2008)

- Sighted individuals blindfolded for 5 days
- Braille training recruited visual cortex
- Effects dissapeared immediately after blindfold removal

##### Conclusion

- Primary cortices may not be truely unisensory.
- Cross-modal plasticity can occur rapidly.
- Existing pathways may be unmasked rather than newly formed.

## 5.3 Neural Evidence for Integration

### A. Super-additive Responses

- Typically in sensory convergence areas rather than primary sensory cortices

#### Example

**Superior Colliculus (Wallace & Stein, 1998)**
- Neurons respond more strongly to combined audiovisual stimuli than sum of individual responses.
- Only occurs when both cues are presented in receptive field

# 8. Synesthesia

- Especially commonly associated with familiar learned sequences such as letters and numbers (alphanumeric sequences).
- Automatic, mandatory, consistent and heritable cross-modal experiences:
	- Tasting words
	- Seeing music
	- Spatial calendars
	- Mirror-touch

## Mechanisms

|Hypothesis|Explanation|
|---|---|
|Pruning hypothesis|Excess developmental connections remain|
|Disinhibition hypothesis|Existing pathways become functionally unmasked|
