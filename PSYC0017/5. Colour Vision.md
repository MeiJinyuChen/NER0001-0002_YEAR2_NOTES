# 1. Cortical Colour Processing

## 1.1 Early Cortical Stages: V1–V3

### Primary Visual Cortex (V1)
- Colour information is not fully resolved in retina/LGN; further processing occurs in cortex.
- V1 contains specialised **colour-sensitive regions called “blobs.”**
- Blobs are:
    - Cylindrical clusters of colour-responsive neurons
    - Located within V1
    - Selectively responsive to chromatic contrast rather than luminance alone
- Functional MRI studies (e.g.,<font color="#c00000"> Hadjikhani</font> et al., 1998) show:
    - Distinct cortical responses to colour gratings compared with luminance gratings
    - <font color="#c00000">Colour-selective modulation</font> extends across **V1, V2, and V3**
### Key Function
- Extraction and refinement of colour signals
- Integration of spectral opponency into more complex perceptual coding

---

## 1.2 Higher-Order Cortical Areas

### Area V4
- Strongly implicated in:
    - <span style="background:#fff88f">Colour constancy</span> 
- V4 neurons represent perceived colour rather than simply reflected wavelength.
### Area V8
- Important for:
	- <span style="background:#fff88f">Colour consciousness</span>
	- Complex chromatic discrimination
- Involved in <span style="background:#fff88f">cerebral achromatopsia</span>
- Strongly activated by colour grating cf. luminance grating
- Activated even without colour stimulus:
  May be due to colour after-effect
### Lateral Occipital Cortex (LOC)
- Important for:
    - <span style="background:#fff88f">Colour memory</span>
    - <span style="background:#fff88f">Associating colours with object identity</span>
    - Higher-level object-colour knowledge

# 2. Colour Constancy

## Definition
**Colour constancy** = the ability to perceive an object’s colour as relatively stable despite changes in illumination.
### Example
- A red apple appears red under:
    - Sunlight
    - Shade
    - Artificial indoor lighting

Even though actual reflected wavelengths differ.

## Mechanism
The visual system corrects for illumination context by:
- Comparing surrounding wavelengths
- Using contextual scene information
- Applying prior assumptions about lighting conditions
- Higher cortical interpretation (especially <span style="background:#fff88f">V4</span>)

## Experimental Evidence
### Hansen et al. (2006)
- Demonstrated <font color="#c00000">memory and contextual influences</font> on colour perception.
- <font color="#c00000">Object knowledge affects perceived colour.</font>

### Wallisch (2017) – “Dress” phenomenon
- Individual assumptions about illumination can drastically alter colour interpretation.
- Demonstrates that colour perception depends partly on inferred lighting environment.

## Classic Study: Shipp & Zeki (1985)
### Procedure
- Varied wavelength composition of illuminating light
- Compared neuronal responses in monkey V1 and V4

### Findings

|Brain Area|Response Type|
|---|---|
|V1|Encodes actual reflected wavelength|
|V4|Encodes perceived stable colour|

### Conclusion
- **V1:** Physical stimulus representation
- **V4:** Perceptual colour constancy

# 3. Full Three-Stage Model of Colour Vision

## Stage 1: Trichromatic Stage (Retina)

### Mechanism
- Three cone types:
    - S-cones (short wavelength)
    - M-cones (medium wavelength)
    - L-cones (long wavelength)
### Principle
- Relative cone activation patterns encode wavelength.
### Theory
- **Young–Helmholtz trichromatic theory**

## Stage 2: Opponent Process Stage (Retina + LGN)
### Mechanism
![[Pasted image 20260512200702.png|274]]
Opponent coding
- Some colours seem to be mutually exclusive (e.g. you can image a reddish yellow, but not a reddish green)
- Spectrally opponent neurons encode colour along axes:

| Opponent Axis  | Comparison |
| -------------- | ---------- |
| Red vs Green   | L – M      |
| Blue vs Yellow | S - (L+M)  |
| Luminance      | L + M      |
Colour opponency → Colour after-effect
### Neural Basis
- Retinal ganglion cells
- LGN parvocellular and koniocellular pathways
### Theory
- **Hering’s opponent process theory**

## Stage 3: Cortical Stage
### Functions

| Cortical Area | Main Role                         |
| ------------- | --------------------------------- |
| V1–V3         | Detailed colour processing        |
| V4            | Colour constancy                  |
| LOC           | Colour memory/object associations |

# 4. Colour Vision Deficiencies (CVDs)
## 4.1 Monochromatism
- No spectral comparison for wavelength and luminance discrimination (between colours)

| Type                                            | Pathology             | Deficit                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rod monochromatism <br>= Complete achromatopsia | No cones. Only rod.   | No colour perception<br>Very poor visual acuity<br>Light sensitive (photophobia) |
| Cone monochromatism                             | Only 1 type of cones. | No colour discrimination                                                         |
## 4.2 Dichromatism
- Only 2 cone types
- Linked with X chromosome

| Type         | Missing Cone  | Deficit                | Approximate Prevalence |
| ------------ | ------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Protanopia   | L-cone absent | Red deficiency         | ~1% males              |
| Deuteranopia | M-cone absent | Green deficiency       | ~1.5% males            |
| Tritanopia   | S-cone absent | Blue-yellow deficiency | Very rare              |

# 5. Anomalous Trichromacy

## Definition
All three cone classes present, but one cone’s spectral sensitivity is shifted.
- ~8% of human males and 0.6% of females of Northern European ancestry
## Types

| Type          | Cone Mutation                       | Main Deficit           | Approximate prevalence |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Protanomaly   | L-cone sensitivity altered to green | Red-green impairment   | ~1% males              |
| Deuteranomaly | M-cone sensitivity altered to red   | Red-green impairment   | ~5% males              |
| Tritanomaly   | S-cone altered                      | Blue-yellow impairment | Very rare              |
### Notes
- Less severe than dichromacy
- Discrimination reduced, not absent

# 7. Cortical Colour Disorders

## 7.1 Cerebral Achromatopsia
### Definition
Loss of colour experience due to cortical damage
### Features
- World perceived in greyscale
- Retina/cones intact
- Peripheral colour mechanisms normal
- Colour imagination and memory may remain intact
### Neural Basis
- Usually linked to <span style="background:#fff88f">V8</span> damage

## 7.2 Colour Agnosia
### Definition
- Colour perception intact
- Impaired knowledge or semantic understanding of object colours
### Example
- Can see colours normally
- Cannot identify expected object colours
### Dissociation
Shows distinction between:
- Sensory perception
- Semantic knowledge
### Neural Basis
- Could be casued by LOC damage
