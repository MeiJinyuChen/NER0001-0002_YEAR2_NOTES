
1. Which of the following features of a signal can not be shown by a spectrogram?  
a. Amplitude  
b. Frequency change over time  
c. Phase of components  
d. Number of component waves  
Correct Answer: c

---

2. What is the approximate range of auditory frequencies that young adult humans can hear?  
a. 2.3Hz-600Hz  
b. 40kHz-200kHz  
c. 100Hz-4000Hz  
d. 30Hz-15kHz  
Correct Answer: d

---

3. Which of the following separates the middle and inner ear?  
a. Auditory canal  
b. Ear drum  
c. Oval window  
d. None of the above  
Correct Answer: c

---

4. Which auditory functions are assisted by the Pinna?  
a. Distinguishing sounds originating from the front/back  
b. Distinguishing sounds originating from the high/low elevations  
c. Both A & B  
d. Neither A nor B  
Correct Answer: c

---

5. Which of the following is considered the ’receptor organ’ of the auditory system?  
a. Ear drum  
b. Organ of Corti  
c. Oval window  
d. Pinna  
Correct Answer: b

---

6. Which of the following is a function of outer hair cells in the cochlea?  
a. Amplifying the movement of the basilar membrane  
b. Broadening the peak response of the basilar membrane  
c. Transducing vibrations to electrical signals  
d. All of the above  
Correct Answer: a

7. What scale best characterises mapping of frequency in the cochlea?  
a. Linear mapping  
b. Longitudinal mapping  
c. Logarithmic mapping  
d. Cartesian mapping

9. Which of the following best describes the auditory mismatch negativity (MMN)?  
a. A late positive event-related potential (ERP) component that reflects conscious detection of task-relevant sounds during attention-demanding tasks  
b. An automatic event-related potential elicited when an infrequent (“deviant”) sound violates a regular auditory pattern, even in the absence of directed attention  
c. A neural response that occurs only when individuals are explicitly instructed to discriminate between different sounds  
d. An ERP component primarily associated with motor preparation for behavioural responses to unexpected auditory stimuli  
Correct Answer: b

---

10. How does the auditory system distinguish between different sound frequencies?  
a. By encoding frequency through differential activation along a tonotopically organised axis in the cochlea and throughout the auditory pathways  
b. By encoding frequency primarily through temporal phase-locking of neuronal firing patterns across all audible frequencies  
c. By representing frequency via the overall firing rate of auditory neurons, independent of their spatial location along the cochlea  
d. By dynamically recruiting distributed neural populations without relying on a stable spatial (tonotopic) organisation  
Correct Answer: a

-----

11. What is meant by the "critical band" in auditory perception?  
a. The full range of frequencies that can be detected by the human auditory system under ideal listening conditions  
b. A range of frequencies around a given centre frequency that stimulate overlapping regions of the basilar membrane, leading to integrated processing  
c. A narrow frequency range within which sounds must fall in order to be consciously perceived  
d. A fixed frequency interval that corresponds to one octave across all parts of the cochlea  
Correct Answer: b

-----

12. Why is temporal regularity in behaviourally relevant sounds important for auditory perception?  
a. It primarily enhances the precision of frequency tuning along the basilar membrane, improving spectral resolution of incoming sounds  
b. It enables the auditory system to encode sounds solely through stimulus-driven responses, minimising the influence of prior context  
c. It ensures that perceptual grouping is based only on simultaneous acoustic features, rather than patterns unfolding over time  
d. It allows the auditory system to form predictive models of the environment, enabling efficient processing of expected input and rapid detection of deviations  
Correct Answer: d

-----

13. What does categorical perception of voice onset time (VOT) demonstrate?  
a. That gradual physical changes in VOT are perceived as abrupt shifts between discrete phoneme categories  
b. That listeners can detect all small acoustic differences in VOT with equal sensitivity across the continuum  
c. That longer VOT values are always perceived as louder or more intense than shorter ones  
d. That perception of speech sounds is based solely on continuous acoustic variation without discrete boundaries  
Correct Answer: a
