
1. Unilateral spatial neglect is best characterised as:  
a. A disorder in which patients lose access to spatial representations and relations due to sensory loss  
b. A failure to flexibly switch between spatial reference frames, caused by a general executive impairment, rather than damage to the spatial representations themselves.  
c. An inability to perceive the relationships between objects within a specific spatial frame of reference, with egocentric or allocentric frames differentially affected depending on lesion location.  
d. A deficit in constructing spatial representations that is limited to egocentric space, because all spatial reference frames must be anchored to the viewer.  
Correct Answer: c

-----

2. Hemispatial visual neglect is best described as:  
a. An inability to perceive objects that fall outside central vision, due to impaired eye movements.  
b. A general visual memory deficit that prevents patients from recalling stimuli presented on the ipsilateral side of space.  
c. A failure to attend to or represent visual information on the side of space contralateral to a brain lesion, despite intact visual processing.  
d. A loss of visual input from one half of the visual field caused by damage to primary visual cortex.  
Correct Answer: c

-----

3. In the Posner cueing paradigm, reaction times to a visual target are typically:  
a. Unaffected by cue validity, because attention cannot be shifted without eye movements  
b. Faster following invalid cues because attention is automatically drawn to unexpected locations  
c. Faster only when cues are presented peripherally, but not when they are presented centrally  
d. Faster following valid cues and slower following invalid cues, reflecting the cost and benefit of spatial attention  
Correct Answer: d
