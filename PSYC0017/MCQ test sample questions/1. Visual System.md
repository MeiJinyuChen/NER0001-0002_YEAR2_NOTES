
1. What is the approximate range of Wavelengths (λ) visible to humans?
a. 0.5 metres (m)
b. 7 micrometres (μm)
c. 380 nanometres (nm)
d. 400 millimetres (mm)
Correct Answer: c

---

2. Which part of the eye is responsible for focusing most of the light entering the eye?
a. The optic cup
b. The retina
c. The lens
d. The cornea
Correct Answer: d

---

3. What is the approximate ratio of photoreceptor cells to retinal ganglion cells?  
a. 1000:1  
b. 10:1  
c. 1:1  
d. 100:1  
Correct Answer: d

---

4. What response would you elicit by shining a white light into the surround of an ON-centre parasol cell’s receptive field?  
a. A decrease in firing rate that is slow and sustained  
b. A decrease in firing rate that is fast and transient  
c. An increase in firing rate that is slow and sustained  
d. An increase in firing rate with high spatial resolution  
Correct Answer: b

5. Which layers of the LGN receive information from parasol cells?  
a. All layers  
b. Layers 1 & 2  
c. Koniocellular  
d. Layers 3 – 6

6. Which of these is not a cell type of the lateral geniculate nucleus?

b. Microcellular

8. How do ocular dominance columns arise in the primary visual cortex (V1)?  
a. Ipsilateral and contralateral inputs are sent to separate layers of the LGN, which project to separate areas of the same layer in V1  
b. Layer 4Cα receives input from the left visual hemisphere, and layer 4Cβ receives input from the right visual hemisphere  
c. Left eye and right eye projections are combined in the LGN, but project to different layers of V1

a. Wedge stimuli excite different areas that respond to different polar angles, and ring stimuli excite areas that respond to different eccentricities  
b. Wedge stimuli excite orientation columns and ring stimuli excite ocular dominance columns

d. Wedge stimuli excite different areas that respond to different eccentricities, and ring stimuli excite areas that respond to different polar angles
