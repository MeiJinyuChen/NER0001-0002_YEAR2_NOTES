
# 1. Evidence for Dedicated Face Recognition Systems

## A. The Inversion Effect

Yin (1969):
- Upside-down faces are much harder to recognise than upright faces.

### Findings:

|Stimulus Type|Effect of Inversion|
|---|---|
|Faces|Strong impairment|
|Houses|Minimal impairment|
|Airplanes|Minimal impairment|

### Interpretation:

- Face recognition is not simply object recognition.
- Inversion disproportionately disrupts face individuation.

### Key Point:

- Suggests face-specific processing mechanisms.

## B. Inferotemporal Cortex (IT) in Monkeys

Desimone et al. (1984):
- Neurons in IT respond selectively to faces.

### Findings:

- Greater response to faces than:
    - Hands
    - Brushes
- Reduced response when:
    - Facial features are jumbled
    - Key features are occluded

### Key Point:

- Indicates specialised neural coding for faces.

## C. Fusiform Face Area (FFA)

### Location:

- Fusiform gyrus
- Part of IT

### Kanwisher et al. (1997):

- fMRI BOLD response stronger for faces than non-face objects.

### Additional Evidence:

- Inversion also reduces FFA responses
- Correct face identification produces strongest FFA activation
- Electrical stimulation of FFA distorts face perception only (Parvizi et al., 2012)

### Key Point:

- FFA is highly specialised for face perception.

# 2. Domain Specificity vs Expertise Debate

## Domain Specificity Hypothesis

### Claims:

- Face recognition is innate from birth
- Operates independently of general object recognition

## Expertise Hypothesis

### Claims:

- Face recognition uses  the same mechanism as general object recognition
- Finly tuned through extensive practive

### Evidence Comparison

| Evidence for Domain Specificity                                                                                                                                                                                        | Evidence for Expertise                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Newborn face preferences:<br>Newborns turn further to look at face-like patterns tham scrambled versions of the same images                                                                                            | Newborn preferences may reflect top-heavy pattern bias, not true face modules (Simion et al., 2002)                                                                                                |
| Developmental / Acquired prosopagnosia:<br>Can be caused by genetic heritability, and damage to inferior occipital regions and the fusiform gyrus (acquired prosopagnosia often coincides with general object agnosia) | FFA could be an area involved in the recognition of objects of expertise, rather than faces per se <br>(e.g. Greeble training activates FFA) (Gauthier et al., 1999)                               |
| Key evidence for expertise (inversion effect in Greeble training)  has issues:<br>1. Fails to replicate<br>2. Greebles activate FFA even prior to training (perhaps  because they are ''face-like'')<br>               | Other-race effect: little-to-no inversion effect for other-race faces<br>ORE can be reduced by experience → Face recognition is honed by experience                                                |
|                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Experience modifies recognition                                                                                                                                                                    |

## Prosopagnosia (Face Blindness)

### Definition:

Selective inability to recognise faces despite normal vision.

### Types:

| Type          | Cause                                                   | Properties                                  |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Acquired      | Damage to inferior occipital regions and fusiform gyrus | Often coincides with general object agnosia |
| Developmental | Present from birth, possible genetic basis              |                                             |

# 3. Holistic and Configural Processing

## Holistic Processing

- Faces processed as integrated wholes
- Whole > sum of parts

### Configural Processing

- A part of holistic processing
- Sensitive to spatial relationships:
    - Eye spacing
    - Mouth position
    - Nose placement

## Evidence:

### A. Inversion

- Feature changes preserved
- Configural changes impaired

### B. Composite Illusion

- Misaligned halves reduce illusion, due to disruption to holistic processing
- Aligned faces force holistic perception

### C. Thatcher Illusion

- Distorted features unnoticed upside-down
- Grotesque upright

# 4. Face Space Theory

- How does the brain encode identity (dimensionality to configural processing)?

## Valentine (1991):

- Faces represented in multidimensional psychological space.

### Principles:

- Average face = norm at centre
- Individual faces vary along dimensions
- Anti-faces = reverse of differences between faces and the norm
- Adaptation shifts perception within this space

### Applications:

- Explains identity coding
- Explains ORE
- Experience reshapes dimensions

# 11. Overall Summary

## Core Conclusions:

### Face recognition:

- Highly sophisticated
- Supported by specialised neural mechanisms
- Partially domain-specific
- Strongly modified by experience
- Holistic/configural in nature
- Encoded within multidimensional face space
