
# 1. Core Concept: Biased Competition Theory

## Definition

Biased Competition Theory proposes that:

- Multiple stimuli in the visual field compete for limited neural processing resources.
- Competition occurs because receptive fields (RFs), especially in higher visual areas, are large enough to contain multiple stimuli simultaneously.
- Attention provides a biasing signal that resolves this competition in favor of relevant stimuli.

## Key Principle

### Without attention:

- Stimuli suppress each other’s neural representation.
- Neural responses are reduced when multiple competing stimuli occupy the same RF.

### With attention:
- Top-down attentional signals selectively enhance the target.
- Neural response becomes similar to the preferred attended stimulus presented alone.

## Foundational Study
**Reynolds, Chelazzi & Desimone (1999)**:
- Demonstrated competition within macaque visual cortex.
- Showed attentional selection can counteract suppressive competition.

---

# 2. Competitive Interactions in Receptive Fields

## Neural Preference

A neuron may prefer:

- Stimulus A over Stimulus B

This preference is measured by:
- Higher firing rate to preferred stimulus
- Lower firing rate to non-preferred stimulus

## Competition Rule
When both stimuli appear simultaneously in one RF:
- Response is **less than the sum** of responses to each stimulus alone
- Indicates mutual suppression

## Interpretation
This means:
- Visual cortex does not represent all stimuli equally
- Stimuli compete for representation
- Competition is fundamental to selective attention


## Table: Neural Response Patterns

|Condition|Neural Response|
|---|---|
|Preferred stimulus alone|High|
|Non-preferred stimulus alone|Low|
|Both stimuli simultaneously|Intermediate/reduced|
|Preferred stimulus attended|Restored toward high|

# 3. Methodologies Used to Study Biased Competition


## A. Non-human Primate Electrophysiology

### Techniques:
- Single-cell recordings
- Multi-unit activity
- Local field potentials (LFPs)
### Advantages:
- Excellent temporal resolution
- Direct neuronal firing measurement
- High spatial precision
### Disadvantages:
- Invasive
- Limited primarily to animal models

## B. Human fMRI

### Blocked Design:
- Participants fixate centrally
- Task: count target occurrences at fixation
- Peripheral stimuli presented:
    - Sequentially
    - Simultaneously
### Logic:
- Sequential = minimal competition
- Simultaneous = competition
### Advantages:
- Whole-brain mapping
- Human participants
- Identifies large-scale attention networks
### Disadvantages:
- Poor temporal resolution
- BOLD is indirect measure of neural activity

## Key Finding:
Simultaneous presentation:
- Lower BOLD response than sequential presentation
- Evidence for competitive suppression in humans

---

# 4. Competition Increases Along the Visual Hierarchy

## Why?
Higher visual areas have:
- Larger receptive fields
- Greater convergence
- More likely multiple stimuli fall within same RF

## Hierarchical Pattern

| Visual Area                       | RF Size    | Competition Strength |
| --------------------------------- | ---------- | -------------------- |
| V1                                | Small      | Minimal              |
| V2                                | Larger     | Moderate             |
| V4                                | Large      | Strong               |
| TEO (posterior area of IT cortex) | Very large | Strongest            |

## Implication
- Competition becomes increasingly important in extrastriate cortex
- Attention is especially necessary in higher-order visual processing

---

# 5. Spatial Separation Evidence

## Prediction:
If competition occurs within RFs, then increasing stimulus separation should reduce competition.

## Experimental Findings:
### Close stimuli (0.5° separation):
- Strong competition
### Far stimuli (7° separation):
- Reduced competition in smaller RF regions
- Little reduction in very large RF areas

## Results by Area

|Area|Effect of Increased Separation|
|---|---|
|V2|Strong reduction|
|V4|Moderate reduction|
|TEO|Little/no reduction|

## Conclusion:
Competition is constrained by RF size:
- Smaller RFs = more sensitive to spacing
- Larger RFs = competition persists

# 6. Attention as a Biasing Signal

## Top-Down Bias
Attention:
- Filters irrelevant stimuli
- Selects task-relevant inputs
- Counteracts suppressive competition

## Neural Effect:
When attending preferred stimulus:
- Response approximates isolated preferred stimulus
- Competing distractor effectively ignored

## Functional Outcome:
Attention acts like:
### “Shrinking” the receptive field
- Attention reduces the effective influence of unattended parts of the receptive field
- Focuses processing resources
- Enhances attended region
- Reduces influence of distractors

---

# 7. Human fMRI Evidence for Filtering

## Findings:
Attention increases BOLD response in:
- V2/VP
- V4
- TEO
- V3A
## Pattern:
- Larger attentional effects in higher areas
- Scales with RF size
## Interpretation:
Attention:
- Improves signal-to-noise ratio
- Enhances selected inputs
- Suppresses distractor influence

# 8. Baseline Neural Bias (Expectancy Effects)

## Important Discovery: 
Bias can occur **before stimulus onset**
### Findings:
- 30–40% increased baseline firing in:
    - V2
    - V4
- Observed during expectancy periods
## Meaning:
Top-down systems pre-activate relevant cortical circuits.
### Functions:
- Preparation
- Anticipation
- Enhanced future target processing
## Additional Notes:
- Effects extend as early as LGN
- Strength of top-down bias scale with the complexity level of anticipated task

# 12. Brain Rhythms and Attention

## 12.1 Alpha and Gamma Oscillations
Two rhythms are particularly important for attention:

| Rhythm | Approximate Frequency | Main Role                                                  |
| ------ | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Alpha  | ~10 Hz                | Suppression / gating of irrelevant information             |
| Gamma  | >30 Hz                | Active processing and communication between cortical areas |
### Alpha
- Alpha power often increases for **unattended or irrelevant information**
- Associated with reduced sensory processing
- Functions as a mechanism of **active inhibition / sensory gating**
- Shows that attention can work by suppressing distractors, not only enhancing targets
### Gamma
- Associated with active perceptual processing
- Can arise from recurrent interactions between excitatory and inhibitory neurons
- Helps coordinate information transfer between cortical areas

## 12.2 Communication Through Coherence (CTC)

### Core Idea
Effective communication between two neuronal populations depends on the timing of their oscillations.
- Neuronal excitability changes across an oscillatory cycle
- Incoming input is most effective when it arrives during a high-excitability phase
- If two areas are phase-aligned, communication is facilitated
- If they are poorly aligned, the same input has less influence
### Functional Meaning
Synchrony can act as a **routing mechanism**:

- Relevant information → stronger effective communication
- Irrelevant information → weaker influence on downstream processing

## 12.3 Relation to Biased Competition
Biased Competition Theory proposes that:
- Multiple stimuli compete for representation
- Competition increases as receptive fields become larger in higher visual areas
- Attention biases this competition in favour of relevant stimuli
### Proposed Neural Mechanism
Attention may resolve competition by:
- Increasing synchronization between neuronal populations representing the attended stimulus
- Increasing their influence on higher visual areas
- Reducing the relative impact of unattended competitors
Thus, attentional bias depends not only on firing rate, but also on **temporal coordination between cortical areas**.

## 12.4 Experimental Evidence: Bosman et al. (2012)

### Experimental Design
Monkeys:
- Maintained fixation
- Viewed multiple visual stimuli
- Covertly attended one stimulus
Neural activity was recorded from:
- V1
- V4

### Main Finding
Attention selectively increased:
- **Gamma-band coherence between V1 and V4**
for the neuronal population representing the attended stimulus.
### Interpretation
- Attended input becomes better synchronized with V4
- Its spikes arrive at more effective phases of the V4 oscillatory cycle
- This gives the attended representation stronger influence on downstream processing
This supports **Communication Through Coherence** as a mechanism of attentional selection.

# 13. Unilateral Spatial Neglect

## 13.1 Core Concept and Clinical Features

### Definition
**Unilateral hemispatial neglect** is:
- A failure to attend to the side of space **contralateral to a brain lesion**
- Not primarily a sensory loss, but a deficit in **attentional representation of space**
- Most commonly associated with **right-hemisphere damage → left-sided neglect**
### Common Diagnostic Tests
- Clinical observation
- Cancellation tasks
- Line bisection
- Copying / drawing
### Neural Basis
- Strongly associated with damage to **parietal attention networks**
- Especially regions involved in spatial attention and priority mapping
- Damage to the broader attention network can produce hemispatial neglect
### Neglect vs Hemianopia

| Disorder | Main Problem |
|---|---|
| Neglect | Failure to attend to / represent one side of space |
| Hemianopia | Loss of visual input from one half of the visual field |


## 13.2 Spatial Frames of Reference

### Key Principle
Neglect does not reflect damage to one universal spatial map.
Instead:
- Multiple spatial reference frames exist
- Different frames can be selectively impaired
- Depending on lesion location and extent, neglect may affect:
    - Egocentric space
    - Allocentric space
    - Peripersonal space
    - Extrapersonal space
Thus, spatial representation in the brain is **multiple and task-dependent**.

## 13.3 Neglect Is Multimodal and Representational
Neglect is not limited to vision.
It can also occur for:
- Touch
- Audition
Neglect can also affect **internal representations**, including:
- Mental imagery
- Working memory
### Milan Duomo Example — Bisiach & Luzzatti (1978)
Patients imagined a familiar public square from one viewpoint and described only objects on one side of the imagined scene.
When imagining the same scene from the opposite viewpoint:
- Previously omitted objects could now be reported
- Objects now falling on the neglected side were omitted
### Interpretation
Neglect can affect:
- Representations of external sensory space
- Internally generated spatial representations
This supports the idea that neglect is fundamentally a disorder of **spatial representation and attention**, rather than simple sensory loss.

## 13.4 Experimental Paradigms and Mechanisms
### Posner Cuing Task
Normally:
- Valid cues → faster responses
- Invalid cues → slower responses
In hemispatial neglect:
- Patients show particular difficulty **disengaging attention from the intact side**
- If attention is first cued to the intact field, detecting a subsequent target on the neglected side becomes especially difficult

|                    | Endogenous ~                                                                                                  | Exogenous ~                                                                             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Form of cue        | Uses a **central symbolic cue**, such as an arrow                                                             | Uses a **sudden peripheral cue**, such as a flash near one possible target location     |
| Action required    | Requires the participant to **interpret the cue**                                                             | Does not require symbolic interpretation                                                |
| Attention involved | Produces **voluntary, top-down attention**                                                                    | Produces **automatic, reflexive, bottom-up attentional capture**                        |
| Example            | - Central arrow points left<br>- Participant voluntarily shifts attention to the left without moving the eyes | - A brief flash appears on the right<br>- Attention is automatically drawn to the right |

### Extinction
**Extinction** occurs when:
- A contralesional stimulus can be detected when presented alone
- But is missed when an ipsilesional stimulus is presented simultaneously
This suggests that:
- The neglected-side stimulus can still reach the nervous system
- It loses the competition for attentional representation when a competing ipsilesional stimulus is present
### Implicit Processing
- Some studies suggest neglected stimuli may still undergo unconscious processing
- The extent of this implicit processing remains debated

## 13.5 Key Theoretical Lessons
### Attention Defines Conscious Space
- Neglect shows that conscious spatial experience depends on attention, not sensory input alone
### Right-Hemisphere Dominance
- Spatial attention is strongly associated with right-hemisphere networks
- Right-sided lesions therefore commonly produce severe left neglect
### Competition
Competition is central to spatial attention at multiple levels:
- **Neuronal level:** stimuli compete within receptive fields
- **Neuropsychological level:** ipsilesional and contralesional representations compete for awareness
### Key Exam Points
- Neglect ≠ sensory loss
- Right hemisphere damage commonly causes left neglect
- Egocentric and allocentric neglect are dissociable
- Multiple spatial reference frames exist
- Neglect can occur across vision, touch, audition and mental imagery
- Posner task → impaired disengagement from intact side
- Extinction → contralesional stimulus lost mainly during simultaneous bilateral competition
