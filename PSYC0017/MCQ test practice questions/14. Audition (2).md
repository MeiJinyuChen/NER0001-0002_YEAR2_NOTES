# 14. Audition (2) — Mock MCQ

1. Auditory scene analysis concerns how the brain:
   a. Recognises faces  
   b. Segregates and identifies sound sources  
   c. Focuses light on the retina  
   d. Combines binocular images

2. A critical band is:
   a. A frequency range stimulating an overlapping cochlear region  
   b. The complete audible frequency range  
   c. A visual orientation channel  
   d. A fixed octave at every frequency

3. Two tones falling within the same critical band are more likely to:
   a. Be perfectly segregated  
   b. Produce binocular rivalry  
   c. Evoke colour constancy  
   d. Interfere and produce beating or roughness

4. Interaural time differences principally support:
   a. Face recognition  
   b. Lens accommodation  
   c. Horizontal sound localisation  
   d. Colour discrimination

5. Spatial release from masking describes improved speech perception when:
   a. Speech is converted to light  
   b. Target and masker come from different locations  
   c. Both ears receive identical mixtures  
   d. The pinnae are removed

6. Which auditory Gestalt cue corresponds most closely to visual good continuation?
   a. Smooth frequency transitions  
   b. Abrupt unrelated onsets  
   c. Opposite modulation patterns  
   d. Large temporal gaps

7. Harmonics that change with a shared frequency modulation tend to be perceived as:
   a. Unrelated visual objects  
   b. Separate sources regardless of context  
   c. A colour afterimage  
   d. One source

8. In a repeated A-B tone sequence, increasing the frequency separation tends to promote:
   a. Visual capture  
   b. Binocular suppression  
   c. Two auditory streams  
   d. One fused stream

9. Bistable auditory perception means that:
   a. Auditory objects never change  
   b. One acoustic pattern can alternate between two interpretations  
   c. Two ears always hear different words  
   d. Perception is fixed by the cochlea

10. Predictive coding proposes that the auditory system:
   a. Learns regularities and signals deviations  
   b. Passively records every sound without context  
   c. Ignores sequence probabilities  
   d. Uses no prior information

11. Mismatch negativity is typically elicited by:
   a. A constant visual grating  
   b. Voluntary eye movement  
   c. A lesion to V5  
   d. An infrequent deviant in an auditory oddball sequence

12. MMN occurs approximately:
   a. Before any sound is presented  
   b. 20–30 minutes later  
   c. 150–250 ms after a deviation  
   d. 4–6 seconds after every sound

13. MMN is especially useful clinically because it:
   a. Is a motor response  
   b. Does not require directed attention or a conscious report  
   c. Requires complex verbal instructions  
   d. Measures cochlear structure directly

14. Which method is most directly used to measure MMN?
   a. EEG or MEG  
   b. T1 MRI  
   c. Single-cell retinal recording  
   d. Method of adjustment

15. Voice onset time is physically continuous but often perceived as:
   a. A continuous colour dimension  
   b. Binocular depth  
   c. Spatial frequency  
   d. Discrete phoneme categories

16. Between birth and about 6 months, infants generally show:
   a. No speech discrimination  
   b. Adult-like categorical boundaries in every language  
   c. Broad sensitivity to native and non-native phonetic contrasts  
   d. Sensitivity only to their native language

17. By approximately 10–12 months, infants typically:
   a. Stop using auditory predictions  
   b. Specialise for native-language contrasts  
   c. Become more sensitive to every non-native contrast  
   d. Lose sensitivity to native speech

18. Reduced /r/–/l/ discrimination in Japanese learners illustrates:
   a. Long-term language-dependent perceptual shaping  
   b. Damage to outer hair cells  
   c. Visual dominance  
   d. Binocular rivalry

19. Which pairing is correct?
   a. Periphery—semantics; brainstem—colour; cortex—impedance matching  
   b. Periphery—faces; brainstem—depth; cortex—ossicular leverage  
   c. All levels perform identical computations  
   d. Periphery—frequency decomposition; brainstem—timing/spatial analysis; cortex—objects/prediction

20. Participants hear repeating tone triplets while EEG records automatic responses to a rare sequence violation, even as they watch a silent film. This is:
   a. A structural MRI lesion study  
   b. A colour-matching experiment  
   c. An oddball MMN experiment testing pre-attentive prediction  
   d. A yes/no visual threshold task

## Answers

1. b  2. a  3. d  4. c  5. b  
6. a  7. d  8. c  9. b  10. a  
11. d  12. c  13. b  14. a  15. d  
16. c  17. b  18. a  19. d  20. c
