# 12. Multisensory Integration — Mock MCQ

1. Visual-dominance theory predicts that, during sensory conflict:
   a. Audition always dominates  
   b. The least reliable cue dominates  
   c. Cues are never combined  
   d. Vision usually overrides another modality

2. In the McGurk effect, visual information about lip movements changes perceived:
   a. Motion direction  
   b. Colour  
   c. Speech sound  
   d. Object size

3. In the ventriloquist effect, perceived sound location is displaced toward:
   a. The least reliable cue  
   b. A visual event  
   c. The quieter ear  
   d. The body midline regardless of vision

4. The modality-appropriateness hypothesis predicts that:
   a. The modality most precise for the task dominates  
   b. Vision is universally dominant  
   c. Cues receive equal weights  
   d. Integration occurs only in infancy

5. In the double-flash illusion, one flash paired with two beeps is often perceived as:
   a. No flash  
   b. One silent flash  
   c. Three flashes  
   d. Two flashes

6. Shams, Kamitani and Shimojo's double-flash result is evidence against a simple:
   a. Temporal-precision account  
   b. Multisensory account  
   c. Visual-dominance account  
   d. Modality-appropriateness account

7. In maximum-likelihood estimation, cue reliability is proportional to:
   a. Mean intensity  
   b. 1/variance  
   c. Variance  
   d. Standard deviation squared plus one

8. MLE predicts that a fused estimate lies closest to:
   a. The more reliable cue  
   b. The less reliable cue  
   c. Vision in every task  
   d. The arithmetic extreme

9. Compared with either single cue, the MLE fused estimate should be:
   a. Less precise  
   b. Equally variable in every case  
   c. Unrelated to reliability  
   d. More precise

10. Ernst and Banks (2002) manipulated visual reliability in a visual-haptic size task by:
   a. Changing auditory frequency  
   b. Stimulating FFA  
   c. Adding visual noise  
   d. Lesioning V1

11. Ernst and Banks found that human cue weights were close to predictions from:
   a. Sherrington's theory  
   b. Maximum-likelihood estimation  
   c. Pure visual dominance  
   d. Early selection

12. The multisensory correspondence problem asks:
   a. Which signals have a common source and should be bound  
   b. Which cone detects red  
   c. Which eye has crossed disparity  
   d. Which LGN layer receives parasol cells

13. Which factor should increase the probability that auditory and visual events are integrated?
   a. Large temporal separation  
   b. Opposite inferred causes  
   c. Unrelated learned associations  
   d. Temporal and spatial proximity

14. Botvinick and Cohen (1998) used which paradigm to study multisensory body ownership?
   a. Thatcher illusion  
   b. Ames room  
   c. Rubber-hand illusion  
   d. Waterfall illusion

15. Wallace and Stein (1998) described superior-colliculus responses to congruent audiovisual stimuli as super-additive when:
   a. The cues fell outside the receptive field  
   b. The combined response exceeded the sum of unimodal responses  
   c. The combined response equalled the visual response  
   d. Both unimodal responses were absent

16. Merabet et al. (2008) found visual-cortex recruitment after sighted adults were blindfolded for:
   a. Five days  
   b. Five minutes  
   c. Five months  
   d. Five years

17. The rapid disappearance of Merabet et al.'s effect after blindfold removal suggests:
   a. Permanent growth of a new retina  
   b. Destruction of visual cortex  
   c. Loss of auditory processing  
   d. Unmasking of existing cross-modal pathways

18. Synesthetic experiences are typically automatic, consistent and often:
   a. Restricted to unfamiliar sequences  
   b. Caused by retinal damage  
   c. Heritable  
   d. Voluntarily invented on each trial

19. The pruning hypothesis explains synesthesia as persistence of:
   a. Eye-muscle feedback  
   b. Excess developmental connections  
   c. Absent cone photopigments  
   d. Damaged hair cells

20. A researcher varies the amount of visual noise in a visual-haptic discrimination task and fits reliability-based cue weights to forced-choice responses. Which approach is this?
   a. Psychophysical test of MLE cue integration  
   b. Structural neuropsychology  
   c. Single-cell study of V1 orientation  
   d. PET study of radioactive glucose

## Answers

1. d  2. c  3. b  4. a  5. d  
6. c  7. b  8. a  9. d  10. c  
11. b  12. a  13. d  14. c  15. b  
16. a  17. d  18. c  19. b  20. a
