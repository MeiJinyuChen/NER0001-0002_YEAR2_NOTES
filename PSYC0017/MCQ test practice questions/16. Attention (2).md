# 16. Attention (2) — Mock MCQ

1. Biased Competition Theory proposes that attention:
   a. Eliminates receptive fields  
   b. Processes every stimulus equally  
   c. Acts only after a response  
   d. Biases competition among stimuli for limited neural representation

2. When a preferred and non-preferred stimulus occupy one receptive field without attention, the neural response is typically:
   a. Higher than the preferred stimulus alone  
   b. Zero in every neuron  
   c. Intermediate because of mutual suppression  
   d. The sum of both isolated responses

3. Reynolds, Chelazzi and Desimone (1999) showed that attending the preferred stimulus makes the response resemble:
   a. The sum of all possible stimuli  
   b. The preferred stimulus presented alone  
   c. The non-preferred stimulus alone  
   d. No stimulus

4. Which method directly measured macaque neuronal firing in biased-competition studies?
   a. Single-cell electrophysiology  
   b. Structural MRI  
   c. PET only  
   d. Method of adjustment

5. In a human blocked fMRI study, lower BOLD for simultaneous than sequential peripheral stimuli indicates:
   a. Super-additivity  
   b. Colour constancy  
   c. Auditory masking  
   d. Competitive suppression

6. Why does competition increase from V1 toward TEO?
   a. All neurons become retinal rods  
   b. Higher areas have no convergence  
   c. Receptive fields become larger and include more stimuli  
   d. Receptive fields disappear

7. Which ordering best describes increasing competition?
   a. Competition is identical everywhere  
   b. V1 < V2 < V4 < TEO  
   c. TEO < V4 < V2 < V1  
   d. V4 < V1 < TEO < V2

8. Increasing stimulus separation should most strongly reduce competition in:
   a. V2, with relatively small receptive fields  
   b. TEO, with the largest receptive fields  
   c. All areas identically  
   d. The cochlea

9. Attention is sometimes described as “shrinking” the effective receptive field because it:
   a. Anatomically destroys the RF surround  
   b. Moves the retina  
   c. Stops the preferred stimulus firing  
   d. Reduces the influence of unattended RF regions

10. Expectancy can increase baseline firing in V2 and V4:
   a. Only during sleep  
   b. Only after retinal damage  
   c. Before stimulus onset  
   d. Only after a motor response

11. Major sources of top-down attentional control include FEF, IPS and:
   a. Oval window  
   b. SPL  
   c. Cochlear nucleus  
   d. FFA only

12. Alpha oscillations around 10 Hz are most associated with:
   a. Suppression or gating of irrelevant information  
   b. Active colour processing only  
   c. Cochlear amplification  
   d. Binocular fusion

13. Gamma oscillations above 30 Hz are most associated with:
   a. Complete neural silence  
   b. Lens accommodation  
   c. Rod dark adaptation  
   d. Active processing and interareal communication

14. Communication Through Coherence proposes that information transfer is enhanced when neural populations are:
   a. At opposite random phases  
   b. Unresponsive  
   c. Phase-aligned  
   d. Anatomically disconnected

15. Bosman et al. (2012) found that attention increased gamma coherence between:
   a. The two retinas only  
   b. V1 and V4 representations of the attended stimulus  
   c. The cochlea and FFA  
   d. V5 and the lens

16. Unilateral spatial neglect is primarily a failure to:
   a. Attend to or represent contralesional space  
   b. Receive retinal input from one hemifield  
   c. Hear high frequencies  
   d. Recognise colour

17. The most common severe pattern is:
   a. Right neglect after right-retinal damage  
   b. Left neglect after cochlear damage  
   c. Bilateral neglect after lens damage  
   d. Left neglect after right-hemisphere damage

18. Bisiach and Luzzatti's (1978) Milan Duomo study showed that neglect can affect:
   a. Only touch  
   b. Only eye movements  
   c. Internally generated spatial imagery  
   d. Only retinal input

19. Extinction occurs when a contralesional item is detected alone but missed:
   a. After an auditory deviant  
   b. During simultaneous ipsilesional stimulation  
   c. During every unilateral presentation  
   d. Only in complete darkness

20. A neglect patient is disproportionately slow to detect a left target after attention was cued to the intact right side. In Posner-task terms, this reflects difficulty:
   a. Disengaging from the ipsilesional side  
   b. Encoding target colour  
   c. Producing an MMN  
   d. Fusing binocular disparity

## Answers

1. d  2. c  3. b  4. a  5. d  
6. c  7. b  8. a  9. d  10. c  
11. b  12. a  13. d  14. c  15. b  
16. a  17. d  18. c  19. b  20. a
