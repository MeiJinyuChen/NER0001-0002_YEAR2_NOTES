# 6. Spatial Vision — Mock MCQ

1. In Fourier descriptions of an image, amplitude primarily determines:
   a. Spatial scale  
   b. Contrast  
   c. Edge position  
   d. Orientation

2. Phase primarily determines:
   a. Luminance contrast  
   b. Spatial alignment of edges  
   c. Colour category  
   d. Viewing distance

3. Low spatial frequencies carry information about:
   a. Broad, coarse structure  
   b. Wavelength composition  
   c. Eye dominance  
   d. Fine texture only

4. A square wave can be reconstructed by adding to its fundamental:
   a. Random phases only  
   b. Colour-opponent channels  
   c. Even harmonics of increasing amplitude  
   d. Odd harmonics of decreasing amplitude

5. Gibson's (1937) orientation-adaptation result supports the existence of:
   a. Colour-memory cells in LOC  
   b. Orientation-selective mechanisms  
   c. A single general visual neuron  
   d. Auditory critical bands

6. Hubel and Wiesel (1968) identified orientation-selective simple cells mainly in:
   a. V1  
   b. Cochlea  
   c. FFA  
   d. Superior olive

7. After adapting to a clockwise-tilted grating, a near-vertical test appears tilted away from the adaptor. This is the:
   a. Tilt aftereffect  
   b. McGurk effect  
   c. Ponzo effect  
   d. Motion aftereffect

8. Blakemore et al. (1971) used the tilt aftereffect to support:
   a. Efference-copy theory  
   b. Late selection  
   c. Population coding of orientation  
   d. Trichromatic coding

9. Why is spatial frequency commonly expressed in cycles per degree?
   a. It directly measures cortical firing  
   b. It incorporates retinal image size through visual angle  
   c. It measures wavelength in nanometres  
   d. It eliminates orientation

10. Campbell and Robson's (1968) contrast sensitivity function peaks near:
   a. 0.04 cycles/degree  
   b. 4 cycles/degree  
   c. 40 cycles/image only  
   d. 400 cycles/degree

11. Poor sensitivity at the high-spatial-frequency end of the CSF reflects a limit on:
   a. Colour constancy  
   b. Auditory pitch  
   c. Depth cue integration  
   d. Visual acuity

12. Blakemore and Campbell (1969) found that adapting to one spatial frequency mainly reduced sensitivity:
   a. Only to colours  
   b. Only in the auditory modality  
   c. Equally at all frequencies  
   d. Near the adapted frequency

13. A hybrid image appears as one identity nearby but another when viewed at a distance. Schyns and Oliva (1999) used this to show that:
   a. Spatial channels are completely independent in perception  
   b. High- and low-SF information can support different recognitions  
   c. Phase has no effect on images  
   d. Only low SF is processed

14. In Chubb et al.'s surround-suppression effect, a centre surrounded by a similar pattern appears:
   a. Higher in contrast  
   b. Lower in contrast  
   c. More saturated in colour  
   d. Closer in depth

15. The Ebbinghaus/Titchener illusion is an example of contextual influence on perceived:
   a. Pitch  
   b. Motion direction  
   c. Colour wavelength  
   d. Size

16. Compared with the fovea, peripheral vision has:
   a. No receptive fields  
   b. Better fine-detail resolution  
   c. Greater cone density and cortical magnification  
   d. Poorer high-SF and low-contrast sensitivity

17. Crowding is best defined as:
   a. Failure of colour constancy  
   b. Failure to detect an isolated peripheral target  
   c. Difficulty identifying a peripheral target surrounded by flankers  
   d. Loss of half the visual field

18. According to Bouma's principle, the interference zone around a peripheral target:
   a. Shrinks with eccentricity  
   b. Increases with eccentricity  
   c. Is constant across the retina  
   d. Exists only at the fovea

19. Which manipulation should reduce crowding?
   a. Add more nearby flankers  
   b. Move the display farther into the periphery without scaling  
   c. Reduce target-flanker spacing  
   d. Move flankers farther from the target

20. An experiment adapts observers to a 6-c/deg grating and measures detection thresholds across frequencies using randomised intensity trials. Which combination is correct?
   a. Colour constancy plus single-cell recording  
   b. Crowding plus structural MRI  
   c. Orientation adaptation plus method of limits  
   d. SF-channel adaptation plus method of constant stimuli

## Answers

1. b  2. b  3. a  4. d  5. b  
6. a  7. a  8. c  9. b  10. b  
11. d  12. d  13. b  14. b  15. d  
16. d  17. c  18. b  19. d  20. d
