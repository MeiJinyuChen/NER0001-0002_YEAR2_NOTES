# 10. Face Perception — Mock MCQ

1. Yin (1969) found that inversion impairs recognition most strongly for:
   a. All categories equally  
   b. Faces  
   c. Houses  
   d. Airplanes

2. Desimone et al. (1984) recorded face-selective responses from neurons in monkey:
   a. Inferotemporal cortex  
   b. Primary auditory cortex  
   c. Superior olive  
   d. Motor cortex

3. Which manipulation reduced the response of face-selective IT neurons?
   a. Presenting an upright intact face  
   b. Increasing auditory intensity  
   c. Adding binocular disparity  
   d. Jumbled or occluded facial features

4. Kanwisher et al. (1997) identified the fusiform face area principally using:
   a. PET glucose in the cochlea  
   b. Method of limits  
   c. fMRI BOLD responses  
   d. TMS lesions in monkeys

5. Where is the FFA located?
   a. Primary auditory cortex  
   b. Fusiform gyrus within inferotemporal cortex  
   c. Superior colliculus  
   d. Intraparietal sulcus

6. Parvizi et al. (2012) found that electrical stimulation of the FFA:
   a. Selectively distorted face perception  
   b. Abolished hearing  
   c. Improved colour constancy  
   d. Produced optic ataxia

7. The domain-specificity hypothesis claims that face recognition:
   a. Is only learned through adult expertise  
   b. Is identical to auditory streaming  
   c. Depends exclusively on V1  
   d. Uses an innate specialised mechanism

8. Simion et al. (2002) argued that newborn face preference might reflect a bias for:
   a. Crossed disparity  
   b. Moving objects  
   c. Top-heavy patterns  
   d. Low-frequency sounds

9. Gauthier et al. (1999) used training with Greebles to support the:
   a. Early-selection model  
   b. Expertise hypothesis  
   c. Opponent-process theory  
   d. Efference-copy hypothesis

10. The other-race effect is reduced by experience, suggesting that face recognition is:
   a. Modified by perceptual expertise  
   b. Entirely fixed at birth  
   c. Independent of exposure  
   d. Based only on retinal wavelength

11. Which finding supports an expertise account of inversion effects?
   a. Inversion never affects trained objects  
   b. Newborns recognise every individual face  
   c. FFA is outside the ventral stream  
   d. Dog experts show stronger inversion effects for dogs than novices

12. Prosopagnosia is:
   a. Loss of motion experience  
   b. Failure to reach accurately  
   c. A selective inability to recognise faces despite otherwise normal vision  
   d. Loss of colour experience

13. Acquired prosopagnosia is associated with damage to:
   a. Primary motor cortex  
   b. Inferior occipital regions and fusiform gyrus  
   c. Cochlear hair cells  
   d. V5 bilaterally only

14. Developmental prosopagnosia differs from acquired prosopagnosia because it:
   a. Is present from birth and may have a genetic basis  
   b. Always follows a stroke  
   c. Always includes object agnosia  
   d. Is caused by retinal cone loss

15. The composite-face illusion is strongest when face halves are:
   a. Misaligned  
   b. Presented as sounds  
   c. Reduced to one pixel  
   d. Aligned

16. Why does misaligning composite face halves reduce the illusion?
   a. It removes binocular disparity  
   b. It causes retinal rivalry  
   c. It disrupts holistic processing  
   d. It increases colour constancy

17. In the Thatcher illusion, inverted faces make grotesque local feature changes:
   a. Look like moving faces  
   b. Difficult to notice  
   c. More salient than upright changes  
   d. Impossible to encode in V1

18. Valentine's (1991) face-space theory represents the average face:
   a. At the centre of a multidimensional space  
   b. At the most distant boundary  
   c. In primary auditory cortex  
   d. As a single retinal feature

19. In face-space theory, an anti-face is:
   a. Any upside-down face  
   b. A face lacking colour  
   c. A jumbled sound  
   d. The opposite vector from an identity relative to the norm

20. A study compares BOLD activity for faces, houses and tools, then tests whether face inversion reduces the response. Which research method and target region are most appropriate?
   a. PET and AIP  
   b. Single-cell recording and lens  
   c. fMRI and FFA  
   d. EEG and cochlea

## Answers

1. b  2. a  3. d  4. c  5. b  
6. a  7. d  8. c  9. b  10. a  
11. d  12. c  13. b  14. a  15. d  
16. c  17. b  18. a  19. d  20. c
