# 8. Depth Perception — Mock MCQ

1. Helmholtz described depth perception as an inference because:
   a. Retinal images contain physical depth directly  
   b. One cone type encodes distance  
   c. Motion is never ambiguous  
   d. A 2D retinal image is compatible with multiple 3D scenes

2. Which cue is binocular and oculomotor?
   a. Occlusion  
   b. Texture gradient  
   c. Accommodation  
   d. Convergence

3. During accommodation for a near object, the ciliary muscles contract and the lens becomes:
   a. Displaced behind the retina  
   b. Flatter  
   c. Rounder  
   d. Opaque

4. An object nearer than the fixation point produces:
   a. Uncrossed disparity  
   b. Crossed disparity  
   c. Zero disparity  
   d. No retinal image

5. An object farther than the fixation point produces:
   a. Uncrossed disparity  
   b. Negative accommodation  
   c. Zero disparity  
   d. Crossed disparity

6. Points on the horopter have:
   a. Maximal uncrossed disparity  
   b. No binocular representation  
   c. Maximal crossed disparity  
   d. Zero disparity

7. Wheatstone's (1838) stereoscope showed that depth can be created by:
   a. Stimulating V5 only  
   b. Presenting appropriately different images to the two eyes  
   c. Removing all disparity  
   d. Using auditory timing

8. Julesz's (1960) random-dot stereograms demonstrate that stereoscopic depth can arise:
   a. Only after recognisable objects are matched  
   b. From disparity without identifiable monocular features  
   c. From colour constancy alone  
   d. Only through accommodation

9. Diplopia occurs when:
   a. Both images are identical  
   b. The observer closes one eye  
   c. Objects lie on the horopter  
   d. Disparity exceeds the fusional range

10. Greatly dissimilar images shown to the two eyes often produce alternating perceptual dominance called:
   a. Colour constancy  
   b. Crowding  
   c. Binocular rivalry  
   d. Motion parallax

11. A child with anisometropia or strabismus is at increased risk of:
   a. Auditory neglect  
   b. Amblyopia and reduced stereopsis  
   c. Akinetopsia  
   d. Colour agnosia

12. The finding that some stereoblind observers improve with training indicates:
   a. Plasticity can extend beyond a strict critical period  
   b. Disparity is learned only before birth  
   c. V1 has no disparity-sensitive neurons  
   d. Monocular cues cannot be trained

13. V1 “near” cells prefer _____ disparity, whereas “far” cells prefer _____ disparity.
   a. crossed; uncrossed  
   b. zero; crossed  
   c. crossed; zero  
   d. uncrossed; crossed

14. During sideways self-motion, near objects generally move across the retina:
   a. At identical speed  
   b. Only if viewed binocularly  
   c. More slowly than far objects  
   d. Faster than far objects

15. Which is not a pictorial depth cue?
   a. Motion parallax  
   b. Linear perspective  
   c. Occlusion  
   d. Atmospheric perspective

16. The interpretation of shading relies partly on the prior assumption that light comes from:
   a. Above  
   b. Below  
   c. The observer's left eye  
   d. The horopter

17. According to Emmert's law, two objects producing the same retinal size will differ in perceived physical size if they differ in:
   a. Wavelength  
   b. Motion direction  
   c. Eye dominance  
   d. Perceived distance

18. The Ames room illusion works mainly by manipulating:
   a. Colour opponency  
   b. Efference copy  
   c. Size-distance relations under restricted monocular viewing  
   d. Auditory localisation

19. When depth cues conflict, optimal cue integration predicts greatest weight for the cue with:
   a. Strongest colour  
   b. Highest variance  
   c. Highest reliability  
   d. Lowest familiarity

20. A researcher independently varies binocular disparity and texture gradient, then asks which interval looks deeper. This is best described as:
   a. Psychophysical forced-choice study of cue integration  
   b. Single-cell lesion study  
   c. Structural MRI  
   d. Dichotic listening

## Answers

1. d  2. d  3. c  4. b  5. a  
6. d  7. b  8. b  9. d  10. c  
11. b  12. a  13. a  14. d  15. a  
16. a  17. d  18. c  19. c  20. a
