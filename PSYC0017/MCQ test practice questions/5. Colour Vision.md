# 5. Colour Vision — Mock MCQ

1. Human photopic sensitivity peaks at approximately:
   a. 550 nm  
   b. 780 nm  
   c. 420 nm  
   d. 500 nm

2. Metamers are:
   a. Afterimages caused by V5  
   b. Identical wavelengths seen as different colours  
   c. Different wavelength mixtures that look the same  
   d. Colours produced only by rods

3. Why are S-cones absent from the foveal centre according to the notes?
   a. They cannot hyperpolarise  
   b. Their addition reduces acuity and short wavelengths are affected by chromatic aberration  
   c. They are required only at night  
   d. They project exclusively to MT

4. Young–Helmholtz theory proposes that colour is initially coded by:
   a. Opponent cortical columns only  
   b. Rod-cone rivalry  
   c. A single luminance channel  
   d. Three cone classes

5. Hering's theory is best supported by which neural computation?
   a. Rod output minus ganglion output  
   b. V5 direction selectivity  
   c. L+M only  
   d. L−M and S−(L+M) channels

6. Colour-sensitive “blobs” are located in:
   a. AIP  
   b. V1  
   c. MT/V5  
   d. Superior colliculus

7. Hadjikhani et al. (1998) used fMRI to show colour-selective modulation across:
   a. Retina only  
   b. V1, V2 and V3  
   c. V5 only  
   d. Hippocampus only

8. Which area is most strongly associated with colour constancy?
   a. V5  
   b. A1  
   c. LIP  
   d. V4

9. Which area is associated with colour consciousness and cerebral achromatopsia?
   a. LGN layer 1  
   b. Superior olive  
   c. V8  
   d. M1

10. The lateral occipital cortex is especially implicated in:
   a. Smooth pursuit  
   b. Colour memory and object-colour knowledge  
   c. Lens accommodation  
   d. Auditory localisation

11. Shipp and Zeki (1985) found that monkey V1 primarily encoded actual wavelength, whereas V4 primarily encoded:
   a. Eye position  
   b. Perceived stable colour  
   c. Sound frequency  
   d. Retinal disparity

12. Hansen et al. (2006) supports the conclusion that:
   a. Colour constancy occurs only in the retina  
   b. V5 creates colour afterimages  
   c. Metamers require identical spectra  
   d. Object knowledge influences perceived colour

13. Wallisch's account of “the dress” emphasises individual differences in assumptions about:
   a. Binocular disparity  
   b. Auditory timing  
   c. Object motion  
   d. Illumination

14. A person lacks L-cones. Which diagnosis is correct?
   a. Cerebral achromatopsia  
   b. Deuteranopia  
   c. Protanopia  
   d. Tritanopia

15. A person lacks M-cones. Which diagnosis is correct?
   a. Protanomaly  
   b. Deuteranopia  
   c. Tritanomaly  
   d. Rod monochromatism

16. Which is the most common anomalous trichromacy in males in the notes?
   a. Deuteranomaly  
   b. Tritanomaly  
   c. Tetrachromacy  
   d. Protanomaly

17. A patient has no cones, very poor acuity, photophobia, and no colour perception. This is:
   a. Colour agnosia  
   b. Deuteranomaly  
   c. Cone monochromatism  
   d. Rod monochromatism

18. A patient sees the world in greyscale despite intact cones and peripheral colour mechanisms. The most likely disorder is:
   a. Anomalous trichromacy  
   b. Protanopia  
   c. Cerebral achromatopsia  
   d. Colour agnosia

19. A patient discriminates colours normally but cannot state the typical colour of a banana. The most likely disorder is:
   a. Colour agnosia  
   b. Cerebral achromatopsia  
   c. Tritanopia  
   d. Rod monochromatism

20. Which method best tests whether V4 represents perceived colour rather than reflected wavelength?
   a. Measure only lens thickness  
   b. Present sounds in an oddball sequence  
   c. Record eye-muscle proprioception in darkness  
   d. Vary illumination while recording neural responses to surfaces with stable perceived colour

## Answers

1. a  2. c  3. b  4. d  5. d  
6. b  7. b  8. d  9. c  10. b  
11. b  12. d  13. d  14. c  15. b  
16. a  17. d  18. c  19. a  20. d
