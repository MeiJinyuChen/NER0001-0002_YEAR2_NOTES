# 2. Research Method — Mock MCQ

1. Which approach directly measures action potentials from individual neurons?
   a. PET  
   b. fMRI  
   c. Single-cell recording  
   d. Psychophysics

2. A monkey views moving dots while a microelectrode records spikes per second from one MT neuron. Which method is being used?
   a. Single-cell electrophysiology  
   b. Neuropsychology  
   c. EEG  
   d. Method of constant stimuli

3. Which statement correctly compares EEG and fMRI?
   a. fMRI directly measures action potentials  
   b. EEG has better temporal but poorer spatial resolution  
   c. Both measure the BOLD response  
   d. EEG has better spatial but poorer temporal resolution

4. What does fMRI measure?
   a. Single-neuron spike rate  
   b. Eye-muscle activity  
   c. A blood-oxygen-level-dependent signal  
   d. Radioactive glucose decay

5. A researcher needs millisecond timing of visually evoked population activity in humans. Which method is most suitable?
   a. Lesion analysis  
   b. Structural MRI  
   c. PET  
   d. EEG

6. A researcher wants a static image in which white matter is brighter than grey matter. Which scan should be used?
   a. T1 MRI  
   b. T2 MRI  
   c. EEG  
   d. fMRI

7. Which feature is characteristic of PET?
   a. It uses a radioactive tracer such as labelled glucose  
   b. It has excellent millisecond resolution  
   c. It directly records membrane potentials  
   d. It detects magnetic fields generated by neural populations

8. A study compares object recognition in stroke patients with different lesion sites. Which main approach is this?
   a. Single-cell physiology  
   b. Method of adjustment  
   c. Psychophysics  
   d. Neuropsychology

9. Which is a major limitation of naturally occurring lesion studies?
   a. They cannot support functional specialisation  
   b. Lesions are often not precisely localised  
   c. They cannot involve human participants  
   d. They always have poor temporal resolution

10. Which method can create a temporary functional lesion in humans with good temporal precision but poor spatial precision?
   a. PET  
   b. TMS  
   c. T1 MRI  
   d. Method of limits

11. Psychophysics primarily studies the relationship between:
   a. Brain structure and lesion size  
   b. Physical stimuli and psychological perception  
   c. Genes and behaviour  
   d. Neurotransmitters and action potentials

12. A participant answers, “Was a light present: yes or no?” What is being measured?
   a. Detection with a forced-choice task  
   b. Discrimination with a yes/no task  
   c. Detection with a yes/no task  
   d. Discrimination with a forced-choice task

13. A participant answers, “Which interval contained the grating, first or second?” What procedure is this?
   a. Neuropsychological testing  
   b. Yes/no detection  
   c. Two-alternative forced choice  
   d. Method of adjustment

14. What is the principal problem with a yes/no task?
   a. It cannot measure behaviour  
   b. It confounds sensitivity with response criterion  
   c. It cannot use visual stimuli  
   d. It always produces 75% accuracy

15. In a forced-choice psychometric function, the threshold is commonly defined at:
   a. 25% correct  
   b. 50% correct  
   c. 75% correct  
   d. 0% correct

16. Stimulus intensity is increased on successive trials until a participant changes response. Which method is this?
   a. PET  
   b. Random-dot stereography  
   c. Method of limits  
   d. Method of constant stimuli

17. A participant reports detecting a tone before it is actually audible because they expect the response to change. This is:
   a. Haemodynamic lag  
   b. Habituation error  
   c. Anticipation error  
   d. Conservative bias

18. Intensities are presented repeatedly in random order to construct a psychometric function. Which method is used?
   a. Method of adjustment  
   b. Method of constant stimuli  
   c. Staircase procedure  
   d. Single-cell recording

19. Which procedure adjusts the next stimulus from the participant's previous responses and rapidly converges on a target performance level?
   a. Structural MRI  
   b. Method of constant stimuli  
   c. Yes/no criterion procedure  
   d. Adaptive procedure

20. Why can an adaptive staircase be problematic with young children?
   a. It cannot estimate a threshold  
   b. Stimuli must always be presented simultaneously  
   c. It requires radioactive tracers  
   d. Early inconsistent responses can distort later stimulus selection

## Answers

1. c  2. a  3. b  4. c  5. d  
6. a  7. a  8. d  9. b  10. b  
11. b  12. c  13. c  14. b  15. c  
16. c  17. c  18. b  19. d  20. d
