# 7. Motion Perception — Mock MCQ

1. Which eye movement continuously tracks a moving object using visual feedback?
   a. Saccade  
   b. Smooth pursuit  
   c. Microsaccade  
   d. Vergence only

2. What is a major function of fixational drift and microsaccades?
   a. Produce binocular disparity  
   b. Create colour constancy  
   c. Focus the lens  
   d. Prevent retinal fading

3. According to Sherrington, perceptual stability during eye movement depends on comparing retinal motion with:
   a. Auditory timing  
   b. Binocular rivalry  
   c. An efference copy  
   d. Eye-muscle proprioceptive feedback

4. According to Helmholtz, perceptual stability depends on:
   a. Colour opponency  
   b. Retinal motion alone  
   c. An efference copy of the eye-movement command  
   d. Lens accommodation only

5. Attempting to move a paralysed eye makes the world appear to move. This most strongly supports:
   a. Sherrington's proprioception account  
   b. Helmholtz's efference-copy account  
   c. Hering's opponent theory  
   d. Gibson's direct perception theory

6. Addams's (1834) waterfall illusion is an example of:
   a. Size constancy  
   b. Auditory streaming  
   c. Colour agnosia  
   d. Motion aftereffect

7. After prolonged rightward motion, a stationary scene appears to move leftward because:
   a. Leftward neurons are destroyed  
   b. V1 stops responding to orientation  
   c. Rightward-selective mechanisms have adapted  
   d. The eyes converge

8. Sutherland's (1961) ratio model represents motion using:
   a. Retinal disparity  
   b. Activity ratios in opponent direction channels  
   c. A single speed neuron  
   d. Only muscle feedback

9. Bidirectional adaptation producing a unidirectional aftereffect better supports:
   a. The ratio model  
   b. Mather's distribution-shift model  
   c. The trichromatic model  
   d. Early selection

10. A Reichardt detector contains two receptive fields, a delay, and:
   a. A lens  
   b. An opponent colour blob  
   c. A semantic filter  
   d. A coincidence/AND comparator

11. Moving the receptive fields of a Reichardt detector closer together while keeping its delay fixed should tune it to:
   a. Both directions equally  
   b. Colour rather than motion  
   c. Lower speeds  
   d. Higher speeds

12. Apparent motion demonstrates that:
   a. Saccades never affect perception  
   b. Sequential stationary images can be perceived as continuous movement  
   c. Motion requires actual continuous displacement  
   d. V5 processes colour only

13. The aperture problem arises because a local receptive field viewing an edge provides ambiguous information about:
   a. The edge's true two-dimensional motion  
   b. The edge's wavelength  
   c. Which eye saw the edge  
   d. Lens curvature

14. Adelson and Movshon's (1982) intersection-of-constraints model solves the aperture problem by:
   a. Removing all component signals  
   b. Comparing sound and vision  
   c. Using only eye-muscle feedback  
   d. Combining multiple local motion constraints

15. Which comparison between V1 and MT/V5 is correct?
   a. V5 is not direction selective  
   b. V1 responds only to global motion  
   c. V1 has larger RFs and solves global motion  
   d. V5 has larger RFs and integrates global motion

16. Random-dot kinematograms are commonly used to measure:
   a. Face inversion  
   b. Global motion-coherence thresholds  
   c. Colour naming  
   d. Auditory critical bands

17. Which method would test large-scale human cortical responses to coherent versus random motion?
   a. fMRI BOLD imaging  
   b. Method of adjustment of lens curvature  
   c. T1 structural imaging only  
   d. Dichotic listening

18. Patient LM, studied by Zihl et al. (1983), had:
   a. Akinetopsia  
   b. Colour agnosia  
   c. Hemianopia  
   d. Prosopagnosia

19. Akinetopsia is most strongly associated with:
   a. Cochlear damage  
   b. FFA stimulation  
   c. Bilateral V5 damage  
   d. Unilateral V1 colour-blob damage

20. Global-motion deficits with relatively preserved global form have been reported in Williams syndrome, autism, dyslexia and amblyopia, supporting the idea of:
   a. Auditory dominance  
   b. Dorsal-stream vulnerability  
   c. Ventral-stream immunity to all disorders  
   d. Complete viewpoint invariance

## Answers

1. b  2. d  3. d  4. c  5. b  
6. d  7. c  8. b  9. b  10. d  
11. c  12. b  13. a  14. d  15. d  
16. b  17. a  18. a  19. c  20. b
