# 1. Why Motion Perception is Important

## Major Functions

|Function|Description|
|---|---|
|Object motion detection|Detect moving objects such as predators, prey, or vehicles|
|Self-motion perception|Use optic flow to understand one’s own movement|
|Figure-ground segmentation|Separate moving objects from static backgrounds|
|Control of action|Guide reaching, locomotion, pouring, tracking|
|Shape from motion|Derive 3D structure from moving points|

# 2. Self-Motion vs Object Motion

## Core Problem

Everything moves across the retina continuously because:
- Objects move
- We move
- Our eyes constantly move
Thus, the visual system must distinguish:

### Object Motion:

Movement of objects in the world.

### Self Motion:

Movement generated by:
- Eye movements
- Head movements
- Body movements

---

# 3. Eye Movements

## Types of Eye Movements

|Eye Movement Type|Characteristics|Function|
|---|---|---|
|Smooth pursuit|Continuous, feedback-controlled|Track moving objects|
|Saccades|Rapid ballistic shifts (~3/sec)|Shift gaze quickly|
|Fixational movements|Drift + microsaccades|Prevent retinal fading|

### Smooth Pursuit

- Maintains moving object on fovea
- Requires visual feedback

### Saccades

- Some sensory suppression occurs during movement
- Prevents blur

### Fixational Eye Movements

- Drift continuously
- Microsaccades refresh retinal stimulation

# 4. Discounting Eye Movements

## Problem:

Why does the world appear stable despite retinal image motion during eye movements?

## Two Theories

|Theory|Mechanism|Prediction|
|---|---|---|
|Sherrington|Compare retinal motion with eye muscle proprioception|Stability from muscle feedback|
|Helmholtz|Compare retinal motion with efference copy of motor command|Stability from internal copy|

## Experimental Evidenc

### Tracking a Moving Object

- Both theories predict stable world

### Poked Eyeball

- World appears to move
- Supports Helmholtz

### Paralysed Eye Attempt

- Attempting eye movement while eye is immobile causes perceived world motion
- Strongly supports Helmholtz

## Conclusion:

### Helmholtz’s efference copy theory is correct.

# 6. Motion Aftereffect (MAE)

## Definition:

After prolonged exposure to motion, stationary stimuli appear to move in the opposite direction.

### Example:

Waterfall illusion (Addams, 1834)

## Key Characteristics

- Adaptation reduces detector sensitivity
- Illusory motion opposite adapting direction
- Demonstrates dedicated direction-selective neurons
- Motion and position are dissociable

---

# 7. Models of MAE

## A. Ratio Model (Sutherland, 1961)

### Principle:

- Opponent pairs of neurons tuned to opposite directions
- Motion determined by relative activity ratio of neurons tuned to opposite directions

### Process:

1. Baseline = equal firing
2. Adaptation = one side fatigued
3. Static test = imbalance activity after adaption causes illusory motion to be seen

## B. Distribution Shift Model (Mather, 1980)

### Principle:

- Motion encoded by population of direction-selective neurons
- Adaptation shifts entire population response

### Advantages:

- Better explains bidirectional adaptation data

## Experimental Support:

Bidirectional adaptation produces unidirectional MAE.

### Therefore:

### Distribution Shift Model is better supported.

---

# 8. Reichardt Detectors

## Purpose:

Model how nervous systems detect motion.

## Structure:

Two receptive fields + delay + AND comparator.

## Mechanism:

1. Stimulus activates RF1
2. Delayed signal arrives
3. Stimulus reaches RF2
4. Simultaneous arrival activates comparator

## Properties

|Feature|Explanation|
|---|---|
|Direction selectivity|Reverse direction fails coincidence|
|Speed tuning|Depends on RF spacing and delay|
|Apparent motion|Sequential flashes can mimic motion|

## Importance:

Provides intuitive neural circuit for early motion detection.

# 9. Apparent Motion

## Definition:

Discrete sequential stimuli perceived as continuous motion.

### Examples:

- TV

- Computer screens

## Consequence:

Can produce motion aliasing:
- Wagon wheel illusion

# 11. The Aperture Problem

## Definition:

Local motion viewed through small receptive fields is ambiguous.

## Key Principle:

For edges, perceived motion is orthogonal to orientation.

## Consequences:

- Local detectors cannot determine true object trajectory
- Speed and direction are ambiguous

## Physical Nature:

Not merely neural limitation—general geometrical constraint.

---

# 12. Solving the Aperture Problem

## Intersection of Constraints (IOC) Model

Adelson & Movshon (1982)

### Principle:

Combine multiple local motion signals to determine true global motion.

## Process:

- Each local detector provides constraint line
- Intersection gives true motion vector

---

# 13. Motion Processing in V1

## Properties:

- Some neurons are direction-selective
- Also orientation-selective
- Respond to local/component motion only

## Limitation:

### V1 neurons suffer from aperture problem.

# 14. Area MT / V5

- Forms columinar structure of preference in motion direction

## Characteristics:

|Feature|V1|V5/MT|
|---|---|---|
|RF size|Small|Large|
|Motion type|Local/component|Global|
|Direction selectivity|Partial|Nearly universal|
|Aperture problem|Yes|Solved|
|Main pathway|Early visual cortex|Dorsal stream|

## Functions:

- Integrates motion over large areas
- Computes global motion
- Critical for motion perception

## Human Evidence:

PET/fMRI:
- Strong activation for motion
- Minimal activation for colour
- Supports modularity

---

# 15. Global Motion

## Random Dot Kinematograms (RDKs)

### Findings:

- Humans perceive coherent motion even with noise
- Threshold as low as 5–10% coherence

### Significance:

Requires large-scale integration beyond local detectors.

### V5 Role:

- Sensitive to motion coherence
- Encodes global patterns
- Greater BOLD response to coherent motion

---

# 16. Development and Dorsal Vulnerability

## Observation:

Global motion matures later than form perception, and is more vulnerable to developmental problem.

Global-motion perception (but not global form) is disrupted in:
- Williams syndrome
- Autism
- Dyslexia
- Amblyopia

## Hypothesis:

Late maturation of dorsal stream increases susceptibility.

---

# 17. Disorders of Motion Perception

## Akinetopsia (Motion Blindness)

### Cause:

Bilateral V5 lesions.

### Symptoms:

- Cannot perceive smooth motion
- Difficulty:
    - Crossing roads
    - Pouring liquids
    - Following conversations

## Classic Case:

Patient LM (Zihl et al., 1983)

## Importance:

Strong evidence for:

### Functional specialization of V5.
