

## Wave Mechanics

### Sound is a longitudinal pressure wave:

- Alternating **compressions** and **rarefactions**
- Requires a medium (air, water, solids)
- Cannot travel in a vacuum

---

## Speed of Sound in Different Media

| Medium     | Speed (m/s) |
| ---------- | ----------- |
| Air (20°C) | 343         |
| Water      | 1497        |
| Gold       | 3240        |
| Brick      | 3650        |
| Wood       | 3800–4600   |
| Glass      | 5100        |
| Steel      | 5790        |
| Aluminum   | 6420        |


### Key Principle:
- Sound travels faster in denser, more elastic materials.

# 3. Fourier Analysis and Sound Decomposition

## Fourier Principle:

All complex sounds can be decomposed into:

- Sine waves
- Different frequencies
- Different amplitudes
- Different phases

## Representations

### Spectrogram:
- x-axis = Time
- y-axis = Frequency

### Frequency Histogram:
- x-axis = Frequency

# 4. Sound Energy and Decibels
## Decibel Formula:
- **Sound level = 10 log(I/I₀) = 20 log(P/P₀) dB**
Where:
- I₀ = 10⁻¹² W/m²
- P₀ = 2 × 10⁻⁵ N/m²

---

## Important Benchmarks

| Sound Level | Meaning             |
| ----------- | ------------------- |
| 10 dB       | Barely detectable   |
| 60 dB       | Normal conversation |
| 120 dB      | Pain threshold      |

## Important Facts:
- Speaking voice moves eardrum by only **150 nm**
- Hearing threshold detects movement of **5 × 10⁻⁸ mm**
- Pain threshold ≈ **0.05 mm displacement**

# 6. Outer Ear (Pinna)

## Functions:
### 1. Funnels sound into auditory canal
### 2. Frequency-specific filter
### 3. Filters and amplifies certain frequencies
### 4. Provides localization cues, especially:
- Elevation (up/down)
- Front/back distinctions

## Key Concepts:
- Pinna shape creates unique reflection patterns
- Produces an auditory “fingerprint”
- Localization can adapt when ear shape changes, but requires relearning
## Resonance:
- Auditory canal length ≈ 25–35 mm
- Resonant frequency ≈ 2–4 kHz
- Enhances speech-relevant frequencies

---

# 7. Middle Ear

## Structures:

|Structure|Function|
|---|---|
|Tympanic membrane|Converts air pressure waves to mechanical vibration|
|Malleus|Lever transmission|
|Incus|Lever transmission|
|Stapes|Pushes on oval window|

## Main Problems Solved:
### Problem:
Air → fluid transition causes impedance mismatch
### Solution:
### Impedance Matching
1. Tympanic membrane larger than oval window (~17x area difference)
2. Ossicular lever action (~1.3x)
### Total amplification:
- ~22x pressure increase

## Protective Function:
Reduces transmission of dangerously loud sounds

# 9. Organ of Corti and Hair Cells

## Two Hair Cell Types

| Feature           | Inner Hair Cells (IHC)                          | Outer Hair Cells (OHC)                   |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Number            | ~3,500                                          | ~20,000                                  |
| Make contact with | 90–95% of spiral ganglion cells                 | 5–10% of spiral ganglion cells           |
|                   | Several spiral ganglion eurons contact each IHC | 1 spiral ganglion neuron per several OHC |
| Primary role      | Sensory transmission to brain                   | Cochlear amplification                   |


## Hair Cell Transduction:

### Process:
1. Sound moves cochlear fluid
2. Basilar membrane vibrates
3. Stereocilia bend
4. K⁺ enters cell
5. Depolarization
6. Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open
7. Neurotransmitter release
8. Auditory nerve fires

---

# 12. Auditory Nerve Coding

## Place Coding:

- Each auditory nerve fiber has a **best frequency**
- Responds most strongly to one frequency

---

## Temporal Coding:

### Phase Locking:

- Low-frequency sounds encoded by precise spike timing
