
Three main approaches
- **Physiology** → single neurons
- **Neuroimaging / Neuropsychology** → brain regions
- **Psychophysics** → behaviour

# 1. Physiology

- Single-cell recording
- Measure AP via single-cell recording with microelectrodes during presentation of stimuli
- Subject: animals (monkeys, cats, mice)
- Output = spikes rate (spikes per second) → Neural response strength

# 2. Neuroimaging / Neuropsychology
 
## 2.1 Neuroimaging

- Many approaches:
  EEG, MEG, PET, fMRI

### EEG

- Measures electrical activity (population level)
- High temporal resolution
- Low spatial resolution
- Measure VEPs (visually evoked potential) for visual stimuli

### MEG

- Similar to EEG but much more expensive
- Special room + Liquid helium
- Higher spatial resolution + Signal accuracy cf. EEG

### MRI

- Measures  static **structure**
- Based on magnetic activity associated with hydrogen atoms
- T1: White matter = brighter, Grey matter = darker
  T2: Grey matter = brighter, White matter = darker

### fMRI

- Measures BOLD signal (blood oxygenation)
- Based on haemodynamic response
- Signal occurs<span style="background:#fff88f"> 4-6 seconds</span> after the response of nerve cells
- <span style="background:#fff88f">Good spatial resolution: ~0.5 mm</span>

|Feature|EEG|fMRI|
|---|---|---|
|Temporal resolution|High|Low|
|Spatial resolution|Low|High|

### PET

- Ingest or injetced with radioactive glucose
- The most active part of the brain gives off the most radioactivity
- <span style="background:#fff88f">Half-life = 2 mins. Almost no activity in 10 mins.</span>

## 2.2 Neuropsychology

- Studies effects of brain damage (lesions)
- Humans: stroke/trauma
- Animals: experimental lesions
- Supports functional specialization
Limitation:
- Lesions are often not localized 
- Lesions may affect normal function of brain regions
- Function of damaged areas may be hard to detect under normal conditions

#### Temporary lesions

# 3. Psychophysics

### Definition

- Study of relationship between:
    - Physical stimulus
    - Psychological perception

### Principles

- Cannot measure mind directly → measure behaviour
- Requires:
    - Precise control over both stimuli and testing procedures
    - Linking hypothesis between subjective and objective phenomena

### 3. Method of ...

|                    | **Limits/Adjustment**                                                                                                                                                                       | **Constant stimuli**                                                                                                                                       | **Adaptive procedures**                                                                                                           |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Stimulus intensity | - Gadually increased/decreased (on each trial) until the participant's response changes<br>- Set by experimenter (limits) or participant (adjustment)                                       | - Random order presentation on each trial<br>- Multiple repetitions<br>- Psychometric function                                                             | - Algorithm adjusts stimulus based on responses to prior trials<br>- Rely on knowledge of perception (e.g. psychometric function) |
| Threshold          | Stimulus intensity at which responses change is taken as threshold                                                                                                                          | <span style="background:#fff88f">Tipping point (50%) = threshold</span>                                                                                    | Results in a single intensity value where a desired performance level is reached (e.g. 50% 'yes' response)                        |
| Pros               | - Relatively fast                                                                                                                                                                           | - No habituation / anticipation error<br>- More accurate = Less bias                                                                                       | - The fastest                                                                                                                     |
| Cons               | - Errors of habituation (Participant continues to give the same response for too long)<br>- Errors of anticipation (Participant anticipates a change and alters their response accordingly) | - Slower<br>- Need to test a predetermined range of stimulus intensity values                                                                              | - Too fast (e.g. when testing children → early errors locked in / disrupted by inconsistence response)                            |
