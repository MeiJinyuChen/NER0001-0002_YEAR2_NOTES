

## Application of PCR
- Generating DNA for insertion in vectors (e.g., for expressing protein, generating vectors for mouse engineering)
- DNA profiling for forensics
- Screening for genetic disorders (e.g., prenatal screening) 
- Mutating specific sites in DNA vectors (site-directed mutagenesis)
- Quantifying DNA (q-PCR, e.g., to quantify viral load)
- Editing DNA in myriad ways (e.g., fusing sequences, adding tags)

### Denaturation
- Lower salt concentration → Less stable base pairing → Favour strand separation
  Mechanism: Cations (especially Mg<sup>2+</sup> shields the negative charges on the DNA backbone, reducing the electrostatic repulsion between strands and allowing for stronger hydrogen bonding.
- Basic (high pH) conditions favour strand separation
- A-T pairs are less stable than G-C and melt first

### Extension
- Polymerase require a divalent cation, typically Mg2+, to bind DNA 
- Temperature: ~ 70 °C is typically optimal for the polymerase (~1 min / kb for Taq) 
  The annealing temperature should be lower than the extension temperature (extension temprature is optimal for polymerase activity + primer extends at the extension stage and has a higher melting temperature) 
#### Cycle
- Calculation:
  1 picogram template DNA, 40 cycles = 2<sup>40</sup> theoretical amplification => 1.1 kg DNA
- ‘Cycle efficiency’ falls between 1 (no amplification) and 2 (perfect amplification)
  1 picogram template DNA, 25 cycles with cycle efficiency of 1.8 = 1.825 * 1 pg => 2.4 ug DNA
- Both the primer concentration & dNTP concentration set an upper limit of product number regardless of cycle number

## Primer
### Primer design
- Primer length: 15-20 bp
  The value can be larger for genomic DNA and smaller for purified vector DNA (smaller possibility to get off-target similar sequences on template DNA)
- Minimum primer length n: 4<sup>n</sup> ＞ template DNA size
  The theoretical DNA length required that a DNA sequence completely complementary to the primer occurs out of random mutation should be larger than the template DNA size.
  e.g. A 16-mer oligonucleotide is the minimum primer length in a theoretical genome of 3 GB with random distribution of sequences (4<sup>16</sup> > 3 x 10<sup>9</sup>)
- Primers (typically 10 μM each) >> template in initial rounds
- Primers may self-anneal and extend prior to initial denaturation step → Always add the polymerase last
-  2 primers should have similar G-C and A-T proportions (similar melting point)
- 2 primers should not be able to bind to each other
- Should not form unwanted secondary structure (hairpin structure) within a single strand
### Primer melting temperature
Melting temperature = temperature at which 50 % annealed 
- Wallace rule
  Melting temp (°C) = 2*(A + T) + 4* (G+C) 
  Works for ~14-30mers, with one sequence filter bound, at 0.9 M NaCl
- Bolton & McCarthy method

  Works for ~14-70mers, [M<sup>+</sup>] < 0.4 M

## DNA Polymerase
### DNA polymerase I
- First PCR protocols employed DNA polymerase I purified from E. coli. 
- This polymerase has three activities: 
  1. 5’ to 3’ DNA polymerase (requires primer and template) 
  2. 3’ to 5’ exonuclease (relevant for proofreading) 
  3. 5’ to 3’ exonuclease (involved in DNA repair -- removes RNA primer)
### Klenow fragment
- The large Klenow fragment is developed and used in PCR, which lacks the subunit that supports the 5’ to 3’ exonuclease activity.
![[Pasted image 20260111194711.png|400]]
- Limitation: Klenow fragment works best at 37 °C and is denatured > 90 °C.
### Taq polymerase
- First heat-stable polymerase: Taq polymerase (from *Thermus aquaticus*)
- Stable for ~ 30 min at 95 °C
- Limitation of Taq polymerase: It has no 3’ to 5’ exonuclease activity = proofreading ability
  High error rates: Taq 1/2000, Klenow fragment 1/50000
- Can combine polymerases, e.g. Taq + Pfu (slow but possesses proofreading activity)
#### TA cloning
![[Pasted image 20260111200327.png|400]]
*Pfu produces blunt ends.*
- Taq tends to nontemplatedly add an extra A nucleotide​ to the 3' end of the newly synthesized strand. 
  PCR products amplified by Taq have single-base 3' A overhangs​ on each strand, making the ends sticky instead of blunt.
- A special linearized cloning vector called a T-vector​ is designed to have a single 3' T overhang​ on each end.
- The PCR product can efficiently anneal to the T-vector in a sticky-end ligation reaction by complementary A-T base pairing, without any treatment by restriction enzymes.

### Buffer for polymerase
e.g. Phusion polymerase are provided with different buffer options (high-fidelity buffer, GC-rich buffer)
**High-fidelity buffer**
**GC-rich buffer** 
1. Circumstance:
  - High GC content (>60–65%)
  - Strong secondary structure
  - Poor or no amplification with HF buffer
  2. Mechanism:
  - Rich in DMSO (helix destabilizer), which disrupts hydrogen bonding and reduces DNA melting temperature.

## Future use of PCR: Mutating and fusing DNA 
![[Pasted image 20260111211414.png|400]]
Primer overlaps are essential in PCR for circular template DNA
- PCR does not generate free linear fragments initially. It generates nicked circles.
- Overlaps allow primers to extend across nicks and convert partial circles into fully synthetic plasmids.
### Mutating
Panel B.
1. Add point mutation into primers. The PCR products contain the mutation.
2. The restriction enzyme Dpnl (only acts on methylated DNA) can be added following PCR to degrade genomic DNA.
### Fusing
