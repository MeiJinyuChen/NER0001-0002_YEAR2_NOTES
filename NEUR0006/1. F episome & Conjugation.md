

## Transformation
Horizontal gene transfer occurs via 3 main mechanisms. 
1. Transformation: Uptake of short fragment of naked (free) DNA from environment by transformable bacteria. The DNA is then integrated into chromosome or maintained as a plasmid.
2. Transduction: Transfer of DNA from one bacterium into another via bacteriophages (λ-phage)
3. Conjugation: Direct cell-to-cell DNA transfer mediated by a conjugative plasmid (or integrative element) using a type IV secretion/pilus system. Usually involves rolling-circle replication at an oriT site.

## Fertility factor
- The donor ability is a hereditary state imposed by a fertility factor (F). It couled be lost and regained rather easily.
-  Conjugation occured only in one direction: one cell acts as donor (carry F, designated as F<sup>+</sup> = male) and another cell acts as recipient (lack F, designated as F<sup>-</sup> = female). F<sup>+</sup> cells do not attach to other F<sup>+</sup> cells.

## F episome
F episome is a F plasmid that can both replicate independently and integrate into host chromosome, where it replicate as part of the chromosome. The integration is reversible.
- ~100 kb in length - (5% of the E. coli genome size) 
- Two origins of replication: 
  1. oriV: used when plasmid is “free” – one copy per bacterial chromosome 
  2. oriT: used for gene transfer between bacterium 
- A large (~33 kb) transfer (tra) region with 40 genes 
![[capture_20251225070318589.bmp|275]]
*IS sequences (in yellow): Transposable sequences that allows the integration of F episome via homologous recombination with the identical sequences in host chromosome. By recombination of these positions in any orientation, the F episome can jump into the host chromosome.*

## Transfer of F episome by conjugation

- **Type 4 secretion system (T4SS)**
  Complex system including enzymes and regulatory proteins required during gene transfer. Act as the transfer channel.
- **Sex pilus** 
  hairlike structure typically 2-3 µm long 
  hollow cylinder 
  8 nm in diameter with 2 nm axial hole 
  F+ cell 2-3 pili
  Pilus retraction most likely leads the cells into close proximity

### tra region
Most of the genes in the tra region are involved in DNA transfer and replication. At least 12 genes are required for modification and assembly of pilin in the sex pilus, and the stabilization of the T4SS.
![[Pasted image 20251225144459.png|475]]
The genes are arranged in loci named tra and trbB.
- traA encodes pilin 
- traI encodes relaxase: The first protein to sit at the oriT region. It makes a nick on DNA to seperate the double strand and unwinds the DNA, so the single-stranded transfer and DNA synthesis (require free -OH group) can start.
- traD encodes coupling protein ATPase (part of T4SS): Guide DNA to the transfer channel.
- traY encodes transcription factor –DNA bending
- traM interacts with DNA and numerous proteins (e.g,TraI, TraD & T4SS), so DNA can be recognised by transport proteins. <font color="#00b050">(Interact with traD and DNA at oriT. It senses the mating signal and ensure that the DNA transfer only occurs when cell-cell interaction is established)</font>
- Factors such as gene products of traN and traG & host outer-membrane components (lipopolysaccharide, OmpA protein): Important in mating pair formation and stabilization. 
  1. Prevent 2 F<sup>+</sup> cells from making contact.
  2. Make sure the transfer will not be affected by other bacterium.
### Formation of the relaxosome & rolling circle-like synthesis
Relaxosome = Relaxase (TraI) & accessory proteins (TraY, TraM, IHF)
![[capture_20251225080544203.bmp|500]]
- The relaxosome forms at the oriT of negatively supercoiled DNA. 
- Relaxase, a dimer, nicks the oriT at a site called nic. A relaxase monomer forms a covalent link with 5’ end generated during the hydrolysis of the phosphodiester bond (transesterase activity). 
- Relaxase is also unwinding the DNA (helicase activity). 
- The relaxase-bound DNA is recognized by the coupling protein (TraD) and with TraM mediating the relaxosome-transferosome contact transported through T4SS to a recipient cell. 
- Certain T4SS can also uptake DNA from the extracellular environment (Heliobacter pylori). 
- The rolling-circle replication (polymerase III) occurs concomitant in donor and recipient cell.

### Rest of conjugation
- The common duration of chromosome replication: 20 mins.
- When the F episom is “free”, the donor and recipient bacterium separate with one copy of the F episom in each cell.
- The F episome is integrated into host chromosome.

## Hfr strain
- The integration of the entire F episome into host chromosome creating an Hfr (high frequency of recombination) strain. Once integrated, the F episome no longer replicates independently. However, it rarely occurs (1 / 10<sup>-6</sup>-10<sup>-7</sup>. In most of the cases, partial transfer of F episome/chromosome makes the recipient bacteria remain F<sup>-</sup>.  Partial chromosome cannot replicate, and either is lost or recombines with recipient chromosome.
- The tra operon is still functional. When conjugation occurs, some part of chromosomal genes may also be transferred into the recipient cell, which could be of the same or different speicies to the donor cell. So the integration of F episome results in a high frequency of recombination.
- The property has been used to make the linkage maps: Map the location of genes in E.coli chromosome without using gene sequencing.
- Some times the F<sup>-</sup> cells have defence mechanisms: Using restriction enzymes to chop up foreign DNA (phages in most cases).
