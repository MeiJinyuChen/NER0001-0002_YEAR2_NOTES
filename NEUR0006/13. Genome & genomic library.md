## Choose vector

![[Pasted image 20260109141356.png]]

### Cosmid
 - Cosmid = cos + plasmid
- Enable packaging of DNA into head of lambda phage. 
- Phage head accepts DNA of ~38-50 kb
- Introduced into E. coli by transduction
- Modern vectors also contain elements for selection in mammalian cells
![[Pasted image 20260109141545.png|475]]

### Bacterial artificial chromosome
- Supports propagation of long DNA sequences up to ~350 kb 
- Based on bacterial F plasmid
- Copy number = 1 (portioning genes ensure accurate partitioning to daughter cells)
- Introduced via electroporation
- Convenient for genetic manipulation (e.g., adding point mutations, or fluorescent 
visualization markers)
- More stable than YAC (fewer DNA rearrangements/chimera formations) 
- Propagate in optimized E. coli strains, e.g., DH10B
![[Pasted image 20260109141743.png]]

### Yeast artificial chromosome
- Larger cargo capacity than BACs (up to ~3000 kb)
- Initially used by the Human Genome Project (switched to BACs) 
- Introduced into yeast by electroporation 
- Can be used to express proteins that require posttranslational modifications (in eukaryotic cells) 
- Yeast are harder to work with than bacteria (grow more slowly, lower DNA yields)
![[Pasted image 20260109142031.png]]

## Mice model

### Pros 
- Genetically similar to human 
- Biological processes mostly similar to humans 
- Easy to house 
- Short generation time 
- ### Cons 
- Limited value for understanding behavioral disorders, e.g. autism 
- Limited value for understanding neurodegenerative disease 
- Large-scale brain anatomy is strikingly different to human

### Random integration
![[Pasted image 20260109144352.png|275]]

- DNA (cloned plasmids) injected into fertilised oocytes
- Oocytes then re-implanted into foster pseudopregnant mother
- Check for presence of new DNA in germline

Limitations
- DNA often integrated as multiple copies
- Often see disappointing expression in central nervous system (can be 
improved with longer DNA fragments, e.g., using BACs)

### Homologous recombination
![[da530caff3a21dd13c0c7a021a1a8134.jpg]]
![[Pasted image 20260109150655.png]]
Chimeric mice: Only a fraction of cells are modified. Must go through multiple generations to get purified mice. Female chimeric mice are more preferred due to efficiency.
![[Pasted image 20260109150927.png]]

**Positive/negative selection**
- Positive selection: Select the integration of the transgene. Using selectable marker gene in homologous arm.
- Negative selection: Select the unwanted random integration. Using resistant gene placed outside the homologous arm, which should not be integrated. Non-homologous recombination (e.g. integration of the entire vector) that is more frequent than ideal result would lead to the survival of products.
![[54416fa1a00626ef386126b35f140baa.jpg]]
The target vector must contain regions the allow homologous recombination, a resistance gene (neomycin, positive selection marker) inserted into an exon (best to destroy also reading frame) and a thymidine kinase (negative selection marker) at the end.
The embryonic stem (ES) cells are transfected via electroporation with the linearized targeting vector.
Upon homologous recombination the thymidine kinase region is not integrated into the ES cell genome. This will generate cells resistant to neomycin that also can grow in the presence of ganciclovir, whereas random integration of the targeting vector will generate ES cells that resistant to neomycin but die in the presence of ganciclovir, because TK kinase posphorylates ganciclovir to form ganciclovir triphospate which causes chain termination and single-strand breaks upon incorporation into DNA.
This so called positive-negative selection allows to identify and enrich ES that underwent homologous recombination.

### Site-specific recombination

![[Pasted image 20260109160816.png]]Cre: recognise loxP site and catalyses recombination between them. The outcome depends on the orientation of the reaction.

1. **Cre-dependent deletion**
   Targeting knockout
2. **Cre-dependent inversion**
   Insert fragment segments between loxP sites

#### Application: Rainbow imaging
![[Pasted image 20260109161614.png]]
- Genetic Construct: A DNA construct is inserted into the neuronal genome. This construct contains genes for 3 fluorescent proteins (XFPs -- red, green, blue), each flanked by  lox sites​ .
- Cre Action:​ When Cre recombinase is expressed in a neuron, it recognizes lox sites and catalyzes the DNA recombination. The specific outcome (e.g., deletion or inversion of a gene) randomly depends on the orientation and type of lox sites used.
- Random Expression: Each neuron undergoes an independent Cre recombination. The colour expressed by a neuron is determined by the specific outcome of Cre recombination.
