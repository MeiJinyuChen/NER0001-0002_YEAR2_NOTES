### Alkaline phosphate: Calf intestinal (CIP)
- Dephosphorylates 5 and 3 end of DNA, RNA, dNTPs and proteins.
- Dimer of 2 identical subunits, each subunit contains two Zn<sup>2+</sup> and 1 Mg<sup>2+</sup>. Can be inactivated immediately by EDTA.
- Optimum pH: 8-10. 
  pH where it can be used with reduced activity: 7.5-9
- Incompletely inactivated by heat
  Can be added to the same buffer immediately after the EcoRI has been inactivated by heat -- Involving DNA precipitation and purification

- Plasmid cannot re-ligate by itself because ligaments requires a 5’-phosphate to form the bond. Only when the insert (still has its 5’-phosphates) is present can ligation occur, because it provides the missing phosphate groups.

## Ligation
### Process
1. DNA ends have to collide by chance and stay together long enough for the ligase to join them. => easier at low T˚C. 
2. Self-adenylylation of the active site lysine in the ligase using ATP or NAD+ (depending on the ligase) releases pyrophosphate (PPi).
3. Transfer of the adenylyl group to the 5-phosphorylated terminus (donor) forming a pyrophosphate bond. 
4. Nucleophilic attack of 3'-OH (acceptor) on 5'-Pi group (donor), loosing the AMP, forms phosphodiester bond.
### Ligase
![[00df53f77bf82dbf8994c2249ccac541.jpg]]
### Maxium temperature for overhangs to ligate
A-T: 2 °C. G-C: 4 °C
e.g.    CTGCA - OH
     G - OH
T+G+C+A = 2+4+4+2 = 12
For the overhangs to ligate, the temperature must ≤ 12 °C.

## Lactose operon in E.coli
![[b9084785232f2bc450bbfd39c599be91.jpg]]
![[capture_20260106161918270.bmp|650]]
- *Operon: All genes that are transcribed into the same mRNA plus any adjacent cis-acting sites involved in transcription or regulation.* 
- *Regulon: When more than one operon is under the control of a single regulatory protein, these operon are called regulon (maltose utlization).*
* *Inducer: Substance that induces enzyme synthesis.*
### Structure
- lac-Z: β-galactosidase -- Catalyses the cleavage of lactose to glucose and galactose.
- lac-Y: Lactose permease -- Inserts into the cell membrane and transports lactose across.
- lac-A: Galactoside transacetylase -- Rids the cell of toxic thiogalactosides which are also transported across.
- Lac-I: Not  part of the Lac operon and encodes the repressor molecule which regulates operon transcription by binding to the operator.
- CRP = CAP protein: Catabolic gene activation protein.
### Lac-I repressor protein
![[Pasted image 20260107003945.png|350]]
- Tetramer 
- 2 binding sites: 1 for inducer, 1 for operator
- Binding to o1 & o3 or o2 & o3
- Lac promoter typical sigma70 promoter (-10 & -35 region) 
- Bend DNA to prevent RNA polymerase & CAP bind to the promoter and the CAP binidng region.
### Situations and regulation of the lac operon
![[capture_20260106172120100.bmp]]
1. **Glucose present** 
   - cAMP level is low 
   - CAP protein does not bind 
   - RNA polymerase keeps falling off promoter site
1. **Lactose absent** 
   - No inducer (allolactose) 
   - Lac-I repressor protein is continously synthesized. Active lac-I sits on operator site and blocks the binidng of the RNA polymerase 
1. **Lactose present**
- Small amount of lactose converted in allolactose. Lac-I is inactive by the inducer
- RNA polymerase can bind to promoter region

## DNA isolation 
### Alkaline lysis
![[capture_20260107084048545.bmp]]
1. **Buffer A1**
pH: 8.0
50 mM **Tris-HCL**: pH buffer
10 mM **EDTA** : Chelates divalent cations such as Mg²⁺ to destabilise cell walls and inactivate DNA nucleases, preventing the degradation of DNA
100 μg/mL **RNase A**: Digests RNA to avoid co-purification with plasmid DNA
*Sometimes: Add lysozyme extracted from chicken egg white to weaken bacterial walls.*
2. **Buffer A2**
Basic. Blue because of pH indicator
1% **SDS**: Strong detergent which lyses cell membrane to release DNA
200 mM  **NaOH**: Denatures plasmid and chromosomal DNA. The chromosomal DNA is too large so fragile, thus it breaks apart into little fragments
3. **Buffer A3**
pH 5.1. Extremely fastly neutralises the solution
2.8 M **KAc**: Generates fast neutralisation. Every base rehybridises. The plasmid DNA is intact again. 
But the chromosal DNA fragments are too many and too distant, so they fail to find completely complementary strand and are mixed with proteins surrouded by SDS. The mixture turns into white and fluffy stuff.  → The KAc reacts with SDS to form KDS → The whole KDS molecule becomes insoluble, dragging all proteins and chomosomal DNA into ppt.

### Spin column-based nucleic acid purification
λ-pUC19 DNA is purified using spin column-based nucleic acid purification.
A way to get very pure DNA/RNA.
![[capture_20260107093850305.bmp]]
1. **Under high-salt conditions**
The chaotropic salt takes away all hydrate shell of DNA. The DNA/RNA backbone binds to silicahydroxyl groups as a replacement of H<sub>2</sub>O.
DNA/RNA bound to silica membrane under high-salt conditions while contaminants were washed away.
2. **Under low-salt conditions**
H<sub>2</sub>O is going between and removes DNA/RNA from the silica membrane.
DNA/RNA is eluted.

## Calculation
### How much antibiotic should be added into culture
How much antibiotic do we have to add to our medium? Ampicillin stock is 10 mg/mL. You have a 3 ml culture. How much ampicillin must you add.
- The common working concentration of ampicilin in liquid culture is 100 µg/mL.
- 3 mL * 100 μg/mL / 10 mg/mL = 0.03 mL = 30 μL
### How much CIP is required to dephosphorylate DNA
1 unit of CIP will dephosphorylate ~1 pmol (= 10<sup>-12</sup> mol) of 5' phosphorylated termini at 37˚C within 30 
minutes. You have 1  µg of a 3 kb plasmid and the CIP has a concentration of  0.2 u / µL. How much CIP do you need to add?
- The molecular weight of a base pair = 660 Da (g/mol)
- The total number of plasmid = mass of plasmid / (mass per bp * number of bps) = 1 μg / (3000 * 660 g / mol) = 5.05 * 10<sup>-13</sup> mol = 0.505 pmol
- The total number of 5' -Pi termini = 0.505 * 2 = 1.01 pmol
- Unit number of CIP required = 1 u/pmol * 1.01 pmol = 1.01 u
- Volume of CIP required = 1.01 u / 0.2u / μL = 5.05 μL
### Efficiency of colony formation
Let’s assume PstI cuts 1 µg with an efficiency of 99%, and the transformation efficiency of competent bacteria used is 10<sup>6</sup> cfu / μg. How much colonies of bacteria with uncut plasmid will form?
10<sup>6</sup> cfu / μg * 1 μg * (1-99%) = 10<sup>4</sup>
