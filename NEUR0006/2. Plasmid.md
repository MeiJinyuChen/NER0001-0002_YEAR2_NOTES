

## Plasmids in gene technology
- Copy number under relaxed control (replication not linked to chromosome → more plasmids)
- Small molecular weight (efficiency of replication & transformation is inversely proportional the plasmid size) 
- One recognition site per restriction enzyme in the multiple cloning site (so the vector is cut once rather than many fragments)

## Incompability 
A number of different incompatibility (Inc) groups of plasmids exist. 
Only plasmids belonging to different Inc groups can co-exist in the same bacterium.
- Plamids in the same Inc group share the replication mechanism (same ori or the same replication control proteins), so they compete for the replication factors, and one of the plasmids will be lost over time. Only plasmids in different Inc group can maintain in the same cell stably.

#### Iterons & RepA (resistance plasmids)
- Iterons: Short, repeated DNA sequences in oriV Binding site for initiator protein (RepA). 
- RepA: Initiates and inhibits its own transcription. The initiator protein requires to start the replication of the plasmid.
- Handcuffing: When the copy number is high, RepA binds to iterons on 2 plasmids at the same time  and links the 2 oriV regions together. The oriV regions are constrained, so the replication is inhibited.
- Negative control:
  Low plasmid copy → fewer iterons overall → less handcuffing → replication proceeds
  High plasmid copy → many iterons → more Rep-bound ori + more ori-ori coupling → replicaation constrained

## Plasmids curation
Agents (e.g. EtBr, glycerol) can disrupt plasmid replication by stabilizing or curing plasmids from cells

EtBr mechanism:
1. Wedge between DNA base pairs and reduce negative supercoiling of plasmids. This inihibits DNA replication (especially for plasmids that rely on supercoiling to initiate replication)
2. Plasmids are smaller than chromosome, and are more sensitive to topological changes
3. Plasmids replication is blocked, but chromosomal replication continuous. After a few generations, daughter cells lost the plasmids

## Plasmid amplification

![[capture_20251227082257774.bmp|375]]
1. Culture grown in <sup>14</sup>C medium (pyramidine, <sup>14</sup>C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>5</sub>N) to label all cellular DNA (2% of which is plasmid). 
2. Cells are washed and <sup>3</sup>H medium is added.  <sup>3</sup>H/<sup>14</sup>C ratio reflects the replication rate of chromosomal DNA
3. After one division control (Ctrl.), chloramphenicol (Cm, 175 µg/ml) = protein synthesis inhibitor is added. Chromosomal DNA completes current round of replication and ceases after addition of chloramphenicol. Plasmid DNA (ColE1) continued to replicate, increasing its rate and copy number.
   Mechanism: Chromosomal DNA replication requires newly synthesised initiator protein, while plasmid replication only requires pre-existing host RNA polymerase. 
4. 3000 copies per cell of ColE1 accumulate under this condition. 125-fold increase over the normal 24 copies per cell 
5. Rifampicin (broad-spectrum antibiotics) stops replication of ColE1.
