### Agarose concentration
![[194ef1aa24bff71223b93440b91bf1e4.jpg]]
![[c600986b695c5b45035110de82e875dc.jpg|300]]![[7fc4f1d362d47746d001ba6684dab559.jpg|400]]
- Assume 100 mL of agarose solution is needed to separate DNA fragments that are 1 kb long. How much agarose is needed?—1.00%, 1 gram (concentration = x gram of agarose gel/100 mL of solution)
- Lower agarose concentration→Larger gel pores & less resistance→DNA fragments run faster. Suitable for larger DNA fragments(larger size range)
- The yield of recovered DNA from the gel is higher if the agarose concentration is smaller.
- Amount of agarose solution required: Depends on the gel chamber. As thin as possible, as long as it covers the wells that contain DNA inside→More fluorescence can be observed.
- Volume of buffer required: as thin as possible as long as it covers the agarose gel (more is waste)
- At which voltage should the electrophoresis be performed? — Compromise between the best voltage for the smallest and the largest fragments / what to observe and what to separate.
  The problem of making gel electrophoresis really slow:very wide band bulking together that cannot be separated. (For small fragments, the drag by electricity is less than diffusion→Very wide bands→Unable to observe)
  Very fast: long and thin bands (Large fragments have to squeeze themselves through gel pores→They become very long and smear).

Lower buffer capacity: Buffer pH changes quickly under current due to  electrolysis of water at electrodes

- Tris: Temperature sensitivity. pH drops substantially in room temperature (if not stored in fridge)→Experiment fails
- EDTA: Chelates ions (especially Mg<sup>2+</sup>) that destroy DNA

## Intercalating agents
Stains used in gel electrophoresis. e.g. Ethidium bromide (EtBr), GelRed.  It intercalates between DNA base pairs and makes DNA visible under UV.
- The intercalation is proportional to length. The longer the DNA fragment is, the more intercalated it is, so the more fluorescence there is.
- Intercalated DNA becomes longer and stiffer so migrates more slowly.

## Gel loading buffer
3 purposes of gel loading buffer:
1. Increase density (sucrose, glycerol, Ficoll) of sample (=>evenly distributed in well)
2. Aid loading by adding colour
3. Add mobility dyes (Bromphenol Blue (dark band at the front), Xylene cyanol FF/Orange Green (light band at the back)) to monitor how fast does the gel run
