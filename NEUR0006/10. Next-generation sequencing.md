##  Pyrosequencing & 454 genome sequencing
### Pyrosequencing
![[76c29259fe1ffe1920d6e8b963b9cf72.jpg]]
![[b0f3a8eade76a040274970b1bd3ace3f.jpg]]
e.g. Light intensity readout produced by AA is twice as A

dATP needs to be replaced by dATPαS: dATP but not dATPαS can serve as substrate for luciferase resulting in erroneous light signals not linked to the incorporation of a deoxyadenosine triphosphate.

DNA synthesis: a dNTP is attached to the 3’ end of the growing DNA strand. The two phosphates are released as pyrophosphate (PPi).
#### Mechanism
1. ATP sulfurylase converts PPi and APS (adenosine 5’-phosphosulfate) to ATP.
2. The newly synthesized ATP drives the luciferase to converts luciferin into oxyluciferin.  
   The light produced by the reaction is proportional to the amount of synthesized ATP and thereby proportional to the amount of nucleotides added to DNA.
3. The light is detected by a CCD camera and seen as a peak in the Pyrogram.
4. The 4 dNTPs are added one at a time in each flow cycle, with apyrase degradation / washing in between.
#### Material
**Solid phase**
DNA polymerase, ATP sulfurylase, luciferase
**Liquid phase**
3 enzymes + apyrase
- Apyrase hydrolyses ATP and unincorporated dNTP, thus switches off the light production.

### 454 Genome sequencing
The 454 sequencer cycles between the 4 dNTPs to build up the sequence. About 300-700 nt of sequence can be read, which is shorter and less accurate compared to the average read length obtained with the Sanger methods.

#### Process
1. DNA is sheared (300-800 bp) and ends are “blunted”.
2. Two different adapters are added to each end of fragment. 
   One adapter provides the primer binding site, and the other is complementary to oligonucleotide on sequencing bead. 
   The ratio of beads to DNA molecules is controlled so that most beads get only a single DNA attached to them.
3. Emulsion PCR: 
   Oil is added to the beads and an emulsion is created. PCR is then performed, with each aqueous droplet forming its own micro-reactor. Each bead ends up coated with about a million identical copies of the original DNA.
4. The sequencing process is done by pyrosequencing.
#### Advantage
- Very quick. Within 24 hours.
- No cloning (use emulsion PCR instead): Fast, no bias towards particular clones
- Average substitution error rate = 10<sup>-3</sup>~10<sup>-4</sup>, higher than Sanger sequencing (but still low for next generation sequencing so far).
#### Disadvantage
##### Errors
- In vitro amplification: Higher bacground error rate due to the use of enzymes.
- Bead preparation: a fraction of the beads end up carrying copies of multiple different sequences, which becomes nonsense products.
- InDels (insertion/deletion) errors: A large fraction of errors are inaccurate readout of homopolymer lengths (single base-pair deletions or insertions) due to signal noise issue (e.g. the weakening of luciferase activity, the light from preveous cycles overlaps with the current cycle). e.g. AAAA being mis-read as AAA.
- Strong light signal in one well of picotiter plate give incorrect reading in neighboring plate
- The accuracy of reading reduces when its position becomes later in sequence: Due to the loss of enzyme or reduction of enzyme activity.
- Phasing: Not all DNA copies on one bead incorporate bases in perfect synchrony. Some strands fall out of phase overtime → Some DNA strand misses a base or adds an extra base → Loss of sychrony/phase results in echo and adds noise to signal.

Traditional Sanger sequencing clone DNA fragments inside living E.coli to amplify them (in vivo amplification). The 454 genome sequencing uses emulsion PCR instead (in vitro amplification).

|              | In vivo amplification                              | In vitro amplification                    |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Advantage    | /                                                  | Fast<br>No bias towards particular clones |
| Disadvantage | Slow<br>Introduces bias during the cloning process | Higher bacground error rate               |

## Illumina sequencing
![[4470bef5461c539de7da9d167735ee17.jpg]]
### Library generation
1. Prepare genomic DNA sample
   Randomly fragment genomic DNA and ligate 2 different adapters to both ends of the fragment.
2. Attach DNA to surface
   2 populations of immobilized oligonucleotides complementary to the adapter ends are prepared on the glass plate surface.
3. Bridge amplification
   Add unlabled nucleotides and enzyme to initiate solid-phase bridge amplification.
4. Denature the double-stranded DNA fragments. The cycle repeats.
![[4028b00d5d9004bbc17dd35a016c407a.jpg]]
### Mechanism of sequencing
1. 4 types of dNTPs are attached with different coloured fluorescent tags.
2. New bases are added one at a time on the primed strand. 
3. Fuorescent tags block the 3’-OH and next base can only be added after tag is removed. They are also called fluorescent-labelled reversible terminators.
   The cycle repeats 50~100 times.
![[Pasted image 20260108185106.png]]
### Detection

### Advantage
1. No cloning: Fast, no bias towards particular clones
2. Low cost
### Disadvantage
1. High error rate: 10<sup>-2</sup>~10<sup>-3</sup>
2. Short read length (initially 26 nt, meanwhile 100 nt)
3. Reading of later position in sequence less accurate dues to phasing (bi-directional phasing)
4. Simultaneous detection of four different fluorescent dyes with similar emission spectra contributes to error
