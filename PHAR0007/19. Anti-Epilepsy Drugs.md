
# 1. Concept of Epilepsy

## 1.1 Definition
- Epilepsy = chronic neurological disorder characterised by recurrent seizures
- Caused by sudden excessive synchronous neuronal discharge in cerebral cortex
- Clinical manifestations:
	- Seizures
	  A short, sudden event involving a change in a person's awareness of their state.
	- Convulsion
	  One of physical manifestation of a seizure, involving episodes of excessive, abnormal muscle contractions.
    
## 1.2 Classification of Seizures

| Type                         | Features                | Key Mechanism                       | First-line Drugs           |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Focal (partial)**          | Localised onset         | Local hyperexcitability             | Carbamazepine, Lamotrigine |
| **Generalised tonic-clonic** | Bilateral convulsions   | Widespread cortical discharge       | Valproate                  |
| **Absence seizures**         | Brief loss of awareness | Thalamocortical T-type Ca²⁺ rhythms | Ethosuximide               |
| **Status epilepticus**       | Continuous seizures     | Failure of seizure termination      | Lorazepam                  |

# 2. Cellular Basis of Seizures

## 2.1 Paroxysmal Depolarising Shift (PDS)
### Mechanism:
- Sustained depolarisation via:
    - ↑ Na⁺ influx
    - ↑ Ca²⁺ influx
- Followed by:
    - Ca²⁺-dependent K⁺ channel activation
      → A prolonged afterhyperpolarisation
      → PDS terminated
### Transition to seizure:
- Failure of Ca²⁺-activated K⁺ current
- Causes a burst of AP in cell
  → Propagates hyperexcitability to surrounding tissue
## 2.2 Epileptogenesis
### Three major mechanisms:
1. **Intrinsic neuronal changes**
    - ↑ Na⁺ currents
    - ↑ Ca²⁺ currents
    - ↓ K⁺ currents
2. **Synaptic imbalance**
    - ↑ EPSPs (excessive activation of NMDARs)
    - ↓ IPSPs (loss of GABA-mediated inhibition)
3. **Circuit reorganisation**
    - Changes in circuitry prompted by loss of normal input
      e.g. Hippocampal sclerosis

# 3. Treatment


## 3.1 Treatment Strategies
1. Decrease excitation
	- Decrease Na<sub>v</sub> / Ca<sub>v</sub> activity
	- Decrease efficacy of excitatory synapses
2. Increase inhibition
	- Increase K<sup>+</sup> channel activity
	- Increase efficacy of inhibitory synapses
