
# 1. Mechanism of Contraction

## 1.1 Ca²⁺-Dependent Contraction Pathway

<img src="assets/Pasted%20image%2020260418062938.png" width="525" alt="Pasted image 20260418062938">

1. ↑ intracellular **Ca²⁺**
2. Ca²⁺ binds **CaM (calmodulin)**
3. → Ca²⁺-CaM activates **MLCK (myosin light chain kinase)**
4. → phosphorylates **MLC** into **MLC-P**
   → ATPase activity
   → Allows conformation change in myosin head
5. → **Actin-myosin cross-bridge formation**
   → Contraction
6. Relaxation occurs via MLC phosphatase (MLCP)

	- Potentiate depolarisation
- **G<sub>q</sub>-linked GPCR**
	- Potentiate depolarisation
- **Gap Junctions**
	- Single-unit cells (e.g. pacemaker cells)

# 2. Mechanism of Relaxation

## 2.1 Passive Relaxation

<img src="assets/Pasted%20image%2020260418080256.png" alt="Pasted image 20260418080256">

- Occurs spontaneously after contraction
- Ca²⁺ removed by:
    - **PMCA** (plasmalemma Ca²⁺-ATPase)
    - **SERCA** (sarcolemma Ca²⁺-ATPase)
	    - IP<sub>3</sub> mediated
	- **NCX** (Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger)
		- High<font color="#000000"> [Ca²⁺]</font>  mediated
- High<font color="#000000"> [Ca²⁺]</font>  → ↑ **B<sub>K</sub> channel** opening
- Depolarisation → ↑ **K<sub>V</sub> channel** opening
- B<sub>K</sub> channel / K<sub>V</sub> channel → ↓ **VGCC** opening
- Hyperpolarisation

# Example Question

## 1. “Describe the cellular mechanisms underlying smooth muscle contraction and explain how pharmacological agents can enhance this process.”

Smooth muscle contraction is primarily regulated by intracellular Ca²⁺ concentration and myosin light chain phosphorylation. Increased intracellular Ca²⁺, mainly through voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels (VGCCs) and IP₃-mediated sarcoplasmic reticulum release, binds calmodulin to form a Ca²⁺-CaM complex. This activates myosin light chain kinase (MLCK), which phosphorylates myosin light chains, enabling actin-myosin cross-bridge formation and contraction.

Gq-coupled receptor agonists such as <font color="#c00000">noradrenaline (α1), acetylcholine (M3), angiotensin II (AT1), vasopressin (V1), and histamine (H1)</font> strongly promote contraction through PLC activation. PLC converts PIP₂ to IP₃ and DAG. IP₃ releases Ca²⁺ from intracellular stores, while DAG activates PKC, which inhibits K⁺ channels, causing depolarisation and further Ca²⁺ influx through VGCCs.

Contraction can also be strengthened independently of Ca²⁺ concentration through Ca²⁺ sensitisation. G12/13 activation stimulates Rho kinase, which inhibits myosin light chain phosphatase (MLCP), prolonging myosin phosphorylation and enhancing force generation.

Pharmacologically, α1 agonists such as phenylephrine are used for vasoconstriction in hypotension and nasal decongestion. Oxytocin stimulates uterine contraction during labour, while bethanechol promotes bladder and GI smooth muscle contraction.

Therapeutic strengths include effective restoration of vascular tone or organ motility. Limitations include excessive vasoconstriction, hypertension, reflex cardiovascular responses, or tissue-specific adverse effects.

Overall, smooth muscle contraction involves integrated receptor signalling, membrane depolarisation, Ca²⁺ mobilisation, and MLCP regulation, all of which are important pharmacological targets.

## 2. “Compare cAMP- and cGMP-mediated smooth muscle relaxation pathways.”

Smooth muscle relaxation can be actively promoted through cyclic nucleotide signalling, primarily via cAMP and cGMP pathways, both of which reduce contractile activity but through distinct mechanisms.

The cAMP pathway is typically activated by Gs-coupled receptors such as β2-adrenoceptors or prostacyclin receptors. Gs stimulates adenylyl cyclase, increasing intracellular cAMP and activating protein kinase A (PKA). PKA reduces <font color="#c00000">MLCK activity</font>, lowers Ca²⁺ sensitivity, increases SERCA-mediated Ca²⁺ reuptake, and opens K⁺ channels such as KATP, causing membrane hyperpolarisation. This reduces VGCC opening and lowers intracellular Ca²⁺. Clinically, β2 agonists such as salbutamol are used in asthma and premature labour.

The cGMP pathway is primarily activated by nitric oxide (NO), which diffuses into smooth muscle cells and stimulates soluble guanylate cyclase. This increases cGMP and activates protein kinase G (PKG). PKG promotes relaxation by enhancing Ca²⁺ extrusion (<font color="#c00000">PMCA</font>), increasing SERCA activity, <font color="#c00000">reducing PLC signalling</font>, opening BKCa channels, decreasing <font color="#c00000">VGCC</font> activity, and <font color="#c00000">activating MLCP</font>.

NO is produced by endothelial cells in response to Gq-linked agonists such as acetylcholine. Clinically, NO donors such as glyceryl trinitrate (GTN) are used in angina and severe hypertension, while PDE5 inhibitors such as sildenafil enhance cGMP signalling.

Both pathways reduce intracellular Ca²⁺ and myosin phosphorylation, but cGMP has particularly strong vascular importance through endothelial regulation.

Overall, cAMP and cGMP pathways are central to smooth muscle pharmacology and represent major therapeutic mechanisms for bronchodilation, vasodilation, and organ relaxation.

## 3. “Discuss pharmacological strategies used to relax smooth muscle, including therapeutic strengths and limitations.”

Pharmacological smooth muscle relaxation is essential in treating diseases such as hypertension, asthma, angina, erectile dysfunction, and urinary obstruction. Multiple strategies target intracellular Ca²⁺, membrane potential, or cyclic nucleotide signalling.

β2-adrenoceptor agonists such as <font color="#c00000">salbutamol</font> and salmeterol activate Gs-coupled receptors, increasing cAMP and promoting<font color="#c00000"> bronchodilation or uterine relaxation</font>. These drugs are highly effective in asthma and premature labour but may cause <font color="#c00000">tremor, reflex tachycardia, and hypokalaemia</font>.

α1 antagonists such as prazosin and <font color="#c00000">tamsulosin</font> reduce Gq-mediated contraction, lowering vascular or prostatic smooth muscle tone. They are valuable in <font color="#c00000">hypertension </font>and <font color="#c00000">BPH</font> but may cause <font color="#c00000">postural hypotension</font>.

L-type Ca²⁺ channel blockers such as <font color="#c00000">nifedipine</font> directly reduce Ca²⁺ entry and are widely used in <font color="#c00000">hypertension</font>. K<sub>ATP</sub> channel openers such as <font color="#c00000">minoxidil</font> hyperpolarise smooth muscle, reducing VGCC activity.

NO donors such as <font color="#c00000">glyceryl trinitrate</font> activate cGMP-mediated relaxation and are highly effective for <font color="#c00000">angina</font>, though <font color="#c00000">tolerance and headache</font> limit chronic use. <font color="#c00000">PDE inhibitors</font> such as <font color="#c00000">sildenafil</font> prolong cGMP action and are useful in erectile dysfunction and pulmonary hypertension.

Each strategy offers specific tissue targeting, but adverse effects depend on receptor distribution and systemic exposure. Common limitations include cardiovascular instability, tolerance, or reflex compensatory responses.

Overall, smooth muscle relaxants represent a highly important therapeutic category, with mechanism-based drug selection essential for maximising efficacy while minimising adverse effects.
