

# **1. Concept**

- **Excitatory (Glutamate):**
    - Opens **cation channels (Na⁺, Ca²⁺)** → depolarisation → EPSP
- **Inhibitory (GABA, Glycine):**
    - Opens **Cl⁻ channels or K⁺ channels** → hyperpolarisation → IPSP

- Glutamate → Na⁺/Ca²⁺ influx
- GABA → Cl⁻ influx

1. Precursor uptake
2. Synthesis
3. Vesicular storage
4. Action potential arrival
5. Ca²⁺ influx
6. Vesicle exocytosis
7. Receptor binding

# Example Question



## 1. “Compare glutamate and GABA neurotransmission, including synthesis, receptor pharmacology, and therapeutic significance.”

Glutamate and GABA are the principal excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmitters in the mammalian CNS, respectively, and maintain essential neural network balance.

Glutamate is synthesised primarily from α-ketoglutarate via aminotransferase or from glutamine via glutaminase in presynaptic neurons. Following vesicular packaging by VGLUT, glutamate is released and acts on ionotropic receptors (AMPA, kainate, NMDA) or metabotropic glutamate receptors (mGluRs). AMPA and kainate receptors mediate rapid Na⁺-dependent depolarisation, while NMDA receptors additionally permit Ca²⁺ influx, are voltage-dependent due to Mg²⁺ block, and require glycine co-activation. NMDA receptors are particularly important in synaptic plasticity, memory, and excitotoxicity.

GABA is synthesised from glutamate via glutamic acid decarboxylase (GAD) and packaged via VIAAT. GABA_A receptors are pentameric ligand-gated Cl⁻ channels producing rapid inhibition, while GABA_B receptors are Gi/o-coupled GPCRs that reduce neurotransmitter release and enhance K⁺ efflux.

Therapeutically, glutamate receptors are less commonly targeted directly due to widespread excitatory importance, though NMDA antagonism is relevant in anaesthesia and neuroprotection research. In contrast, GABA_A receptors are major pharmacological targets for benzodiazepines, barbiturates, alcohol, and anaesthetics. Benzodiazepines act as positive allosteric modulators, enhancing inhibitory tone for anxiety, epilepsy, and sedation.

Overall, glutamate promotes excitation and plasticity, whereas GABA provides inhibitory control. Pharmacological manipulation of GABAergic signalling has broad therapeutic value but carries risks of dependence, tolerance, and CNS depression.


## 2. “Explain the pharmacology and clinical importance of GABA_A receptor modulators.”

GABA_A receptors are pentameric ligand-gated chloride channels that mediate fast inhibitory neurotransmission in the CNS. Activation increases Cl⁻ influx, hyperpolarising neurons and reducing excitability.

GABA_A receptors are major targets for several clinically important CNS depressants. <font color="#c00000">Benzodiazepines</font>, such as<font color="#c00000"> diazepam and midazolam</font>, act as positive allosteric modulators (PAMs), increasing the frequency of channel opening in response to endogenous GABA. These drugs are widely used for <font color="#c00000">acute anxiety, insomnia, epilepsy, muscle relaxation, and procedural sedation</font>.

<font color="#c00000">Barbiturates</font> also positively modulate GABA_A receptors but <font color="#c00000">prolong channel opening duration, producing stronger CNS depression</font>. While effective as <font color="#c00000">anaesthetics and anticonvulsants</font>, they have <font color="#c00000">lower therapeutic indices and greater risk of respiratory depression</font> than benzodiazepines.

Other modulators include intravenous and inhalational anaesthetics, neurosteroids, and alcohol. Because these drugs enhance inhibitory signalling rather than directly activating receptors, they generally preserve physiological control at therapeutic doses.

Strengths of GABA_A modulators include <font color="#c00000">rapid onset, strong anxiolytic and anticonvulsant efficacy</font>, and <font color="#c00000">broad utility </font>across psychiatry, neurology, and anaesthesia. However, major limitations include <font color="#c00000">sedation, cognitive impairment, tolerance, physical dependence, withdrawal syndromes, and respiratory suppression at high doses</font>.

Benzodiazepines are generally safer than barbiturates due to wider therapeutic margins, but long-term use remains problematic.

Overall, GABA_A receptor modulation represents one of the most therapeutically important mechanisms in neuropharmacology, though careful balance between efficacy and CNS toxicity is essential.


## 3. “Describe the NMDA receptor and explain its physiological and pharmacological significance.”

The NMDA receptor is a specialised ionotropic glutamate receptor that plays a central role in excitatory neurotransmission, synaptic plasticity, and neurotoxicity.

Unlike AMPA receptors, NMDA receptors require both glutamate binding and glycine co-activation, and their ion channel is blocked by Mg²⁺ at resting membrane potentials. Depolarisation removes this Mg²⁺ block, allowing Na⁺ and significant Ca²⁺ influx. This voltage-dependent gating makes NMDA receptors critical coincidence detectors in learning and memory processes such as long-term potentiation.

Physiologically, NMDA receptor-mediated Ca²⁺ entry activates intracellular signalling pathways involved in synaptic strengthening, development, and plasticity. However, excessive activation can produce excitotoxicity through Ca²⁺ overload, contributing to stroke, epilepsy, neurodegeneration, and traumatic brain injury.

Pharmacologically, NMDA receptor antagonists include AP5 (research), ketamine, and certain anaesthetic agents. Ketamine produces dissociative anaesthesia and has emerging antidepressant uses due to modulation of glutamatergic circuits. However, NMDA blockade may cause psychotomimetic effects and cognitive disturbance.

NMDA receptor dysfunction is also implicated in schizophrenia and developmental neurological disorders.

Thus, NMDA receptors are physiologically essential but therapeutically challenging targets due to their central role in both normal cognition and pathological overexcitation. Understanding NMDA pharmacology is highly important in modern neuroscience and clinical therapeutics.
