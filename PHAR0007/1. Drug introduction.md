## 1. Drug Target and Mechanisms

### A. Specific Binding Target
#### **Types**
- Receptors (e.g. agonists/antagonists on β-adrenoceptors)
- Enzymes (e.g. MAO, cholinesterases)
- Transporters (cardiac glycosides acting on Na⁺/K⁺ pump)
- Ion channels (local anaesthetics acting on Na⁺ channels)
- DNA (anticancer drugs)
#### **Example**
- **Local anaesthetics (e.g. lidocaine)**
    - Target: voltage-gated Na⁺ channels
    - Mechanism: block Na⁺ influx → prevent action potentials
    - Use: pain control
#### **Speficity**
- High specificity → lower side effects
- Off-target binding → toxicity

### **B. Non-specific drug action**
- No identifiable binding sites
- Generally acts at **higher concentrations**
- Common mechanism: disrupt membrane/ion channel function
#### **Example**
- **General anaesthetic (e.g. ether, N<sub>2</sub>)**
	- Less affected by changes in chemical structure
	- A wide variety of molecules can produce general anaesthesia
    - High concentration required → Works at >20 mM
    - Mechanism: alters membrane fluidity
#### **Strength**
- Broad effect
#### **Limitation**
- Poor specificity → higher toxicity

## 2. Selective Toxicity

### Concept
- Off-target binding: drugs bind to sites in addition to their main site of action, which may casue unwanted or toxic effects
- → High affinity to **pathogen/diseased tissue** + Minimise damage to normal cells

### **How selectivity is achieved**
#### **1. Pharmacodynamics (Drug Power)**
- Increase **affinity for desired site of action**
- Example:
    - **Cardioselective β1-blockers (e.g. atenolol)** 
	    - Selective MAO inhibitor
	    - Preferentially act on heart
- Key concepts
	- **Affinity (binding)**
	  → Ability of a drug to bind to a receptor
	  → Determined by receptor–drug interaction strength
	- **Efficacy (activation)**
	  → Ability of a drug to activate the receptor and produce a response
	  → Determines maximum effect (Emax)
	  → High efficacy = full agonists / Low efficacy = partial agonists
	- **Potency**
	  → Amount of drug needed to produce a given effect
	  → Usually measured by **EC50** (dose for 50% effect)
	  → Strongly affected by affinity + Slightly modulated by efficacy
#### **2. Pharmacokinetics (Drug Motion)**
- Increase concentration of drug at desired site of action ( = deliver drug to specific site)
- Example:
    - Inhaled bronchodilators
    - local anaesthetics
    - Eye drops
- Drug movement:
	1. Absorption
	2. Distribution
	3. Metabolism
	4. Excretion
