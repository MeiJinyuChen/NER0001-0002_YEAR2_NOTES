## CRISPR

### CRISPR in vivo

![[capture_20251208113118349.bmp]]
**Phase I: Formation of adaptive immune response**
1. Bacteria identifies the foreign phage DNA as an invader.
2. cas1-cas2 protein dimer captures a short segment (~30bp) of the phage DNA (protospacer) as spacer.
3. cas1-cas2 integrates the spacer into the CRISPR locus of the bacterial chromosome. A permanent, genetic memory of the infection is created.

![[capture_20251208113144313.bmp]]
![[capture_20251208113214697.bmp]]
**Phase II: Upon viral invasion**
1. CRISPR locus is transcribed as a long precursor RNA (pre-crRNA) from upstream promoter.
2. pre-crRNA is processed by cas proteins & tracrRNA to form short crRNAs.
3. A crRNA and a tracrRNA combine to form a gRNA.
   - gRNA: Directs the cas9 nuclease to any sequence that is complementary to the crRNA's spacer sequence.
   gRNA and cas9 nuclease combine to form a CRISPR complex.
   - CRISPR complex: Seek out and cleave DNA of the same virus if it infects the cell again.

### Phase III: Interference
1. cas9 finds a sequence that 1) matches the spacer and 2) has the PAM. 
2. Once it finds the sequence, it unwinds DNA and make a double-strand break in 3~4bp upstream of PAM, disrupting DNA of virus.
   PAM (protospacer adjacent motif):  Short DNA sequence (2–6 bp) located immediately adjacent to the protospacer in the invading DNA. Typical PAM sequence: -NGG-, where N could be any nucleotide.

## Prime editing

![[capture_20251208123209941.bmp]]
### Structure of PE:pegRNA complex
1. Revserse transcriptase and cas nickase combine to form prime editor(PE).
2. Prime editor and prime editing guide RNA (peg RNA) combine to form PE:pegRNA complex.
   pegRNA: 
   (1) Scaffold: binds Cas9.
   (2) 3' extension: 
   - prime binding sequence (PBS): a short sequence (≈10–15 nt) complementary to the DNA downstream of the nick, enabling the RT to bind and initiate DNA synthesis.
   - reverse transcription template (RTT): contains the DNA sequence that will replace the original DNA (e.g. base change, insertion, deletion)
   (3) 5' extension:
   - spacer region: guides Cas9 to target.
   
![[capture_20251208123651682.bmp]]
### Process
1. PE binds and nicks genomic DNA
2. PBS hybridizes to nicked genomic DNA
3. Reverse transcriptase copies the edit into a 3' DNA flap
4. The edit may permanently incorporate into the genome or revert to WT sequence by 50% : 50% chance.

 primer design: 
 1. 2 strands should have similar G-C and A-T proportions (similar melting point)
 2. 2 strands should not bind to each other
 3. Should not form unwanted secondary structure (hairpin structure) within a single strand
 4. Should not be too short (less specific as longer primers). Normal length: 18-25 nt

Sanger sequencing
![[capture_20251205004327926.bmp]]
Smaller fragments travel farther than larger fragments in gel electrophoresis.

Sanger sequencing uses radiolabeled or fluorescently labeled dideoxynucleoside triphosphate (ddNTP) analogues to terminate DNA synthesis at A, T, G, or C. The resulting DNA fragments are then separated by size using gel electrophoresis. Longer DNA fragments (toward the top of the gel) contain sequence information about the 3′ end of the coding strand.

The 3′ to 5′ sequence of the coding strand can be read off the gel from top to bottom: CTAAACTTGACG.

Therefore, the sequence of the DNA template strand is its complement: 5′⁢−GATTTGAACTGC−⁢3′ .
