## DNA damage

### Internal factors (spontaneous)
- Base losses (e.g. depurination / depyrimidination) - most frequent
- DNA replication (e.g. polymerase errors, topoisomerase nick not sealed properly) 
- Cellular metabolic by-products (e.g. oxidation) 
- Base modifications (e.g. deamination / alkylation)  
- Repeat “slippage” (e.g. expansion, or decrease) rNTPs instead of dNTPs

### External factors (environmental)
- Alkylating agents - disrupt base pairing and backbone (e.g. nitrosamines in tocacco) 
- Intercalating agents - insert between stacked bases and distort helix (e.g. EtBr) 
- Environmental toxins (e.g. heavy metals, Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAHs)) 
- Ionising, UV radiation – thymine (pyrimidine) dimers 
- Dietary DNA-Damaging Factors (e.g. heterocyclic amines, nitrosamines, aflatoxin, acetaldehyde)

## Repair mechanisms

![[capture_20251207233022367.bmp|500]]

Same principle and different enzymes between prokaryotes and eukaryotes:
- Mismatch repair (MMR)
- Nucleotide excision repair (NER)
- Base excision repair (BER)
- Homology directed repair (HDR)

Different principles and different enzymes:
- Direct reversal
- Translesion DNA synthesis (TLS)

Absent in most of the prokaryotes:
- Non-homologous end joining (NHEJ)

## Mismatch repair (MMR)

<span style="background:#fff88f">Base mismatch occured during DNA replication </span>
Related proteins detect the mismatch, remove the incorrectly paired nucleotide, and insert the correct nucleotide in its place, using the complementary strand as a template.

![[Pasted image 20251207215838.png]]
Base-change substitution 

99% of errors occured in replication is corrected by MMR.
Can also corrected by DNA polymerase proofreading (3' to 5' exonuclease) activity:
1. Mismatch recognition 
2. Polymerase pauses 
3. Mismatched 3′ end shifts from catalytic site to exonuclease 
4. Polymerase cleaves nucleotide(s) mismatch 
5. 3’ end is transferred back to the polymerase site 
6. Replication resumes

**MMR in prokaryotes:**

![[c634976b09f78ef6699c63433730dc56.jpg]]


1. Base pair mismatch create a distorsion in the DNA helix.
2. MutS recognises and sinks to the distorsion place and forms a complex with MutL.
3. MutS-MutL scans DNA bidirectionally, forming a loop.
4. MutS-MutL finds the nearst GATC site and recruits MutH.<span style="background:#fff88f"> MutH</span> cleaves the newly synthesised unmethylated GATC sequence.
5. <span style="background:#fff88f"> Helicase II</span> and <span style="background:#fff88f">Pol I</span> exonuclease unwinds and degrade the newly synthesised DNA strand past the mismatch.
6. Pol III fills the gap. Ligase seals the DNA.

❗**No hemimethylation mechanism in eukaryotes.**
Eukaryotic MMR equivalents recognise: 
1. Temporary Nicks in between Okazaki fragments 
2. Misjoined rNTPs 
3. Associate with sliding clamp (marks newly synthesized strand)
   Sliding clamp works as a beacon. It has signals that indicates where the strand has been newly synthesised.

## Base excision repair (BER)

<span style="background:#fff88f">Small, non-helix-distorting base damage (e.g. damage to a single base caused by chemical modification)</span>

![[Pasted image 20251207225646.png|350]]
1. <span style="background:#fff88f">DNA glycosylase</span> recognises and binds damaged base 
2. It flips base out of the DNA helix 
3. It cleaves sugar-base glycosidic bond (abasic nucleotide) 
4. <span style="background:#fff88f">AP endonuclease</span> cleaves nucleotide phosphodiester bond 
5. a) Short patch – Polymerase synthesises cleaved nucleotide 
   b) Long patch – Nucleases trim / modify ends, Polymerase synthesises cleaved nucleotide stretch (≤~12) 
6. Ligase seals the gap

------

## Nucleotide excision repair (NER)

<span style="background:#fff88f">Bulky, helix-distorting, large DNA lesions. e.g.</span>
<span style="background:#fff88f">1. Thymine dimers (UV light damage)</span>
<span style="background:#fff88f">2. Bulky cheimcal addcuts (benzopyrene from tobacco)</span>
<span style="background:#fff88f">3. Interstrand crosslinks</span>
<span style="background:#fff88f">4. Other pyramidine dimers</span>

4×UVR proteins – UvrA-D
![[fea6dc039674cdda20fe8700546fa88c.jpg]]

1. 2xUvrA + 1xUvrB complex scans genome 
2. Complex recognises and binds distortion 
3. UvrA exits complex 
4. UvrB “melts” into lesion - locally unwinds helix 
5. UvrB recruits UvrC (endonuclease) 
6. UvrC makes 2x precise cuts of 12-13bp length
7. UvrD (helicase) removes the cut 
8. <span style="background:#fff88f">DNA pol I </span> fills gap and Ligase seals gap

## Direct reversal
- Simplest repair mechanism 
- Recognises specific modifications and reverses them without DNA excision, resynthesis, or ligation 
- No need for DNA template 
- No need for ATP

## Translesion DNA synthesis (TLS)
<span style="background:#fff88f">Damage tolerance mechanism - enables replication to bypass damage and continue </span>
Repair by low fidelity DNA polymerases (Y-family), without proofreading activity

2 models.
1. **Polymerase-switching model** 
- High fidelity pol stalls and switches with TLS pol 
- TLS pol replicates across damage  
- High fidelity pol switches back
![[49bd0d0b18b54565c9d15050845dda59.jpg|425]]

2. **Gap-filling model**
- High fidelity pol skips ahead of lesion 
- Gap is filled by TLS pol
![[89b857807ffcfedd175153ba51954be2.jpg|425]]

## Non-homologous end joining (NHEJ)
<span style="background:#fff88f">dsDNA breakage</span>

- Major repair pathway in eukaryotes, but not universal in prokaryotes (use a more accurate system, because their tiny and simple genome cannot afford errors)
- Always available during cell cycle
- Error prone: quick fix but easily induce errors

![[capture_20251207231737593.bmp]]


![[capture_20251207231858717.bmp|350]]

1. Ku70/Ku80 recognises and binds ds break 
2. Ku70/80 recruits protein kinases (DNA-PKcs, autophosphorylated once the DNA ends are aligned) 
3. Formation of DNA-PK complexes (1 per DNA end)
4. 2 DNA-PK complexes aligns and stabilises ends (brings 2 broken ends together)
5. Nucleases / polymerases trim / fill ends 
6. Blunt ends or micro-overhangs are formed 
7. Ligase seals the break

DNA-PK complex = Ku70/Ku80 + DNA-PKcs

## Homology directed repair (HDR)
- Occurs only during <span style="background:#fff88f">S/G2 phase</span> (when homologous DNA template, i.e. sister chromatid is nearby)
- Needs template DNA 
- Highly accurate

![[capture_20251207232604873.bmp|400]]
### Pre-synapsis 
- MRN complex identifies the DSB 
  RAD50: holds broken DNA ends together 
  MRE11: nuclease - initiates DNA end resection 
  NBS1: recruits and activates ATM 
- ATM signals cell cycle arrest and recruits and coordinates repair proteins 
- End resection: <span style="background:#fff88f">MRN complex + BRCA1</span> initiate DNA trimming - ss 3’ overhangs 
- <span style="background:#fff88f">RPA</span> coats and stabilises ssDNA overhangs 
- <span style="background:#fff88f">BRCA1, BRCA2 and RAD52</span> help replace RPA with <span style="background:#fff88f">RAD51</span> 
- <span style="background:#fff88f">RAD51 presynaptic nucleofilament</span> is formed on ssDNA


![[capture_20251207232806005.bmp|400]]
### Synapsis 
- RAD54 travels with RAD51 - remodels chromatin for homologous DNA access 
- <font color="#c00000">RAD51 nucleofilament searches for and invades the homologous DNA</font> 
- D-loop (displacement loop) is formed and stabilised by RAD54 
- <span style="background:#fff88f">RAD52</span> keeps the second DNA end stable for later pairing 
### Post-synapsis 
- <font color="#c00000">RAD54 removes RAD51 after invasion and frees 3′ end for synthesis </font>
- DNA polymerase elongates invading 3′ end, with homologous DNA as template 
- Extended fragment returns and pairs with original broken DNA end 
- Remaining gaps filled by DNA polymerase and sealed with ligase

## Check point


![[99310fbf936fa6ff1ac2c47168098baa.jpg|350]]![[16d011fb169ce7b73f9600720abc96df.jpg|350]]

### 1. Restriction point
= The “all or nothing” moment in G1 when the cell decides to either go through the entire cell cycle or remain in G1.
1. The extracellular environment must be favorable
2. The cell must have enough nutrients and resources to duplicate its chromosomes and centrosome and to double the size of all other cellular components

### 2.  G1/S checkpoint
Check for DNA damage

### 3. G2/M checkpoint
 1. The chromosomes must be completely duplicated 
 2. The centrosome must also be duplicated
 3. DNA must not be damaged.

### 4. metaphase/anaphase checkpoint
This checkpoint controls the separation of sister chromatids at the metaphase plate and, in doing so, controls the transition from metaphase to anaphase. 
1. All of the kinetochores must be bound to kinetochore microtubules 
2. All of the sister chromatids must be properly lined up and bi-oriented at the metaphase plate.
