## TFII system (eukaryote)

### Consensus sequence in eukaryotes
- Core promoter elements: Minimal set of elements required for transcription initiation, close to Transcription Start Site (TSS).
- BRE- TFIIB Recognition Element
- TATA box (TATAAA)
- INR - Initiator Element 
- MTE - Motif Ten Element
- DPE - Downstream Promoter Element 
- DCE - Downstream Core Element 
Optional regulatory promoter elements: Modulate gene expression, more distantly located. e.g.  Enhancers, silencers, insulators.
<font color="#00b050">Most eukaryotic genes do not possess a TATA box in their promoter. The TATA box is present in ~20% of eukaryotic genes and is involved in the tight regulation of genes that respond to rapidly changing conditions. Constitutively expressed genes, such as those encoding housekeeping proteins, do not have a TATA box and are expressed at a constant rate.</font>
![[8242c10a55f0fad1190224910ede2a2a.jpg|700]]
TFIID is composed of TBP (TATA binding protein) and TAF (TBP-associate factors)
Process:
  1. TBP (TFIID) binds TATA box, causing distortion 
  2. TAF (TFIID) binds to INR and MTE/DPE/DCE
  3. TFIIA stabilises TBP-DNA interaction 
  4. TFIIB binds BRE – positions and orients RNA Pol II to TSS
  5. TFIIF associates with RNA Pol II and binds TSS 
  6. TFIIE recruits TFIIH (helicase activity)
  7. TFIIH unwinds DNA

  ![[capture_20251205121014033.bmp|400]]
### Prenitiation complex in eukaryotes
RNA Pol II holoenzyme = 
1. RNA Pol II (multi-subunit) 
2. General transcription factors (TFIIA - H) 
3. Mediator complex - required by most promoters (But not always necessary. Not core elements) : Mediates response to activators, repressors, and co-factors
4. Nucleosome modifiers and remodelers 
5. Transcriptional activators
<font color="#00b050"> The last component to complete the pre-initiation complex (PIC) is a large protein complex called Mediator. Once the PIC is fully assembled, the RNA polymerase II tail is phosphorylated. The PIC is transversed to initiation-open complex, and RNA pol II can “escape” the PIC in a process called polymerase escape. Transcription elongation begins.</font>

# Transcription Process


## 1. Transcription initiation

1. Pre-initiation-closed complex: Polymerase holoenzyme in position on promoter 
2. Transcription bubble: dsDNA unwinds ~14bp (-11 to +2) at TSS. ~8bp of DNA-RNA hybrid is formed
3. <span style="background:#fff88f">Abortive synthesis</span> = frequent, repeated failure of polymerase to start RNA synthesis. Release and degradation of short 2-9bp RNAs.
   The transcription elements hold RNA pol II tightly, preventing it from elongation. RNA pol II can add a few nucleotides, but cannot advance until it overcomes the stability of the initial complex. The failure of elongation happens several times.
- Preinitiation-closed complex: One in which the dsDNA has not yet separated into two ssDNA strands.
- Initiation-open complex:   Upon synthesis of 9-11bp, the RNA pol II undergoes a conformational change, closing around DNA and establishing a firm, stable association. RNA pol II opens the dsDNA into single strands. The region formed is the transcription bubble, and the elongation succeesfully starts. 
  Prokaryotes: spontaneous – No energy needed
  Eukaryotes: ATP dependent 
4. Promoter clearance: Transcription factors leave promoter. This is now the elongation complex

![[capture_20251205175724567.bmp|300]]

<font color="#00b050">Conformational change of σ factor: The nascent mRNA transcript then exits the complex through an exit channel. Initially, sigma factor blocks the RNA exit channel. When the transcript reaches about 10 nucleotides in length, the force of the continued polymerization pushes the transcript against sigma factor. With no room to grow, the transcript is pushed off the DNA template and subsequently out the side of the complex by its own continued polymerization. After being pushed by a few more of these aborted transcripts, σ factor is eventually pushed off of the complex, allowing the transcript to exit through the exit channel. Transcription elongation then proceeds unencumbered.</font>

## 2. Transcription elongation

- DNA strands separate
  Template binds RNA polymerase at the active site 
  Non-template is held apart by “lid”, “zipper” and “rudder” regions
- rNTPs enter the active site and pair in a processive manner = 1bp opens, 1bp is formed 
- Transcription bubble size remains constant 
- RNA polymerase adds in quick succession 1000’s of bases (20-50 / sec)

## 3. Transcription termination in prokaryotes

Terminator sequences  signal RNA polymerase to cease transcription. 
Two prokaryotic models: 
1. Intrinsic 
2. Rho-dependent
![[Pasted image 20251205155845.png]]

### Intrinsic termination
DNA sequence that ends transcription in absence of other factors 
Features: 
- An inverted C-G rich repeat that forms an RNA hairpin-loop 
- A string of 8-10 A bases that pairs with the transcribed poly-U in the transcription bubble (a weak, unstable structure)
![[ccbd716b8e260fc3d9571dd34d54ea29.jpg]]

### Rho dependent termination
Dependent on Rho (helicase). Used by E.Coli and several prokaryotes.

Features: 
- Rho: Ring shaped hexameric ATP dependent helicase 
- Rho utilisation site = rut site: Act as binding site for Rho protein and activate it  
- Rho “hops” on elongating transcripts and translocates along them 
- Induces termination through <span style="background:#fff88f">ATP hydrolysis</span> by: 
  1. Unwinding and destabilizing the DNA-RNA hybrid (RNA detaches)
  2. Inducing RNA polymerase conformational changes (RNA pol dissociates from DNA)

## 4. Transcription termination in eukaryotes
Not governed by a single mechanism. 2 mechanisms coorperating together: allosteric weakening, Torpedo-driven disassociation.

### Allosteric weakening
![[ebd3b7b5d1ff68fb92792a6816c01fe5.jpg]]

**Process**
1. Upon transcribing the transcription termination signal (poly-A signal), the C-terminal domain (CTD) of RNA pol II is phosphorylated. The conformational change causes the RNA pol II to gradually lose ability to elongate and slows down.
2. RNA pol II loses some elongation factors and starts recruiting some termination factors such as <span style="background:#fff88f">CPSF and CSTF</span> to CTD.
3. CPSF & CSTF 1) weaken the bonding between RNA pol II and DNA; 2) bind to and cleave poly-A signal, separating RNA transcript from RNA pol II.
4. RNA transcript is polyadenylated. 


### Torpedo-driven disassociation
![[capture_20251205172416187.bmp]]
Any small bits of uncapped transcript remnants on RNA pol II (above the cleaving site, where RNA pol II is bound to DNA) is degraded by RNAse. RNA-DNA is disrupted, and RNA pol II dissociates from DNA.
