## Nuclear protein transport

- Receptors: importin (soluble cytosolic proteins, karyopherin family) 
- Channel: nuclear pore 
- The Ran-GTPase drives directional transport through nuclear pores
- Nucleoporins have FG repeats (phenylalanine-glycine repeats) that serve as binding site for importins and exportins

## Mitochondrial transport

<img src="assets/capture_20251208225310173.bmp" alt="capture_20251208225310173">

<img src="assets/capture_20251208225338953.bmp" alt="capture_20251208225338953">

- <font color="#00b050">hsp 70: part of import ATPase</font>

**Steps**
1. The protein is kept unfolded by the binding and hydrolysis of cytosolic <font color="#c00000">hsp70 chaperones</font> (an ATPase).
2. The signal sequence is recognised. The protein is bound by the receptor part of the <font color="#c00000">TOM complex</font>.
3. The protein is handed to the channel part of TOM complex and <font color="#c00000">inserted to the membrane</font>.
4. <font color="#c00000">TIM complex</font> recognises the signal sequence.
5. The cthsp70 at peptide tail out side the mitochondrion is<font color="#c00000"> removed by ATP hydrolysis</font>.
6. The signal sequence is cleaved by a <font color="#c00000">protease</font>.
7. The peptide head is transfered to the mitochondrial matrix by <font color="#c00000">mitochondrial hsp70 chaperones</font>. 2 possible mechanisms (probably both):
   - mthsp70 actively pull the protein into the matrix
   - mthsp70 prevent it from slipping back into the channel
   ❗Translocation across the inner membrane requires the electrochemical gradient.
1. Protein is refolded inside the matrix by folding protein (<font color="#c00000">hsp60</font>). This process is ATP-required.

#### Steps

1. The signal sequence is on the N-terminus (first of protein synthesised). It is immediately recognised and bound by <font color="#c00000">SRP</font>.
2. Once the protein is bound by SRP, it stops translation.
3. Once SRP binds to<font color="#c00000"> SRPR</font>:
   (1)  SRP and SRPR dissociate from the protein, and the signal sequence binds to the <font color="#c00000">sec61</font> <font color="#c00000">complex</font>. Both are powered by GTP hydrolysis.
   (2) Translation starts again. Ribosome sits on sec 61.

### N-linked glycosylation in ER

N = asparigine, at <span style="background:#fff88f">N-X-S or N-X-T</span>, where X can be any amino acid except for proline.
1. <font color="#c00000">Oligosaccharyl transferase</font> (ER-membrane protein that sits beside sec61) recognises asparigine in the growing polypeptide chain.
2. It interacts with ribosome and glycosylate (transfer sugars from the lipid-linked oligosaccharide to) the asparigine side chain.

### ER-associated degration

<img src="assets/capture_20251209114634152.bmp" alt="capture_20251209114634152">

1. Chaperone catches the misfolded protein
2. Disulfide isomerase: disulfide bonds are broken → protein begins to unfold
3. Lectin: recognises and binds to sugar
4. <font color="#c00000">E3 ubiquitin ligase</font>: adds ubiquitin chain → marks proteins for degradation by proteasome
5. <font color="#c00000">AAA-ATPase</font>: uses ATP to transport protein out of ER
6. <font color="#c00000">N-glycanase</font>: removes sugar from the protein
7. Polyubiquitin chain guides the protein to the proteasome
