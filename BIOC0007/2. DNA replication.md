## Process of replication

![[capture_20251202230933924.bmp]]

### Initiation
1. Ori/s is recognized by initiator proteins that open-up the helix 
2. Initiator proteins recruit DNA helicase 
3. DNA helicase unwinds the helix to expose ssDNA 
4. Binding proteins coat the ssDNA (stabilize and protect) 
5. Primase is recruited by helicase 
6. Primase synthesises a ~10bp RNA primer
![[capture_20251207214835839.bmp|475]]

### Elongation
1. Sliding clamp is recruited 
2. Clamp loader is associated with DNA polymerase 
3. Holoenzyme is associated with the sliding clamp 
4. Replication machinery moves along the DNA, simultaneously, adding nucleotides to the 3' end of the growing DNA strand 
5. RNA primers are removed and replaced with DNA 
6. DNA ligase seals nicks
![[capture_20251207214944357.bmp|550]]


### Termination
1. Two replication forks meet and fuse, when they reach the end 
2. Topoisomerases resolve any supercoiling occurring during the final stages 
3. Replication complexes are disassembled 
4. Two daughter DNA strands are formed
![[Pasted image 20251207215104.png|450]]

## Origins of replication

dsDNA at ORI is easily pulled apart because it is riched in A-T base pairing.
Once replication starts, origins are "fired" and cannot be used again until the next cell cycle, preventing re-replication

![[capture_20251207202834449.bmp|275]]
- DNA unwinds to form 2x replication forks that move in opposite directions. 
- Replication moves from ori bidirectionally , forming a replication bubble

## Initiator proteins

Key features: 
➢ Identify and bind Ori(s) 
➢ Recruit helicase loading protein(s) 
➢ Recruit helicase(s) 
➢ Ori(s) - licenced and open(s)

### Prokaryote - DnaA

![[capture_20251207203532751.bmp|450]]
1. DnaA·ATP binds to 9mer (7×9bp DnaA boxes)
2. DnaA·ATP interacts with 13-mer (AT-rich DNA elements) and opens the dsDNA strands at 13-mer, forming  2 replication forks.
3. DnaC helicase loader and DnaB helicase are recuited and located on the replication forks at 13-mer.

### Eukaryotes

![[a1db726a6edcca11eeec7d40cda5ff39.jpg]]

1. ORC binds to ori.
2. Cdc6 binds to ORC, creating a docking platform.
3. ORC-Cdc6 complex recuits Cdt1, which brings MCM2-7 helicase along.
4. ORC-Cdc6 complex use ATP hydrolysis to open the ring of MCM2-7, and load it onto ori.
5. The second MCM2-7 is recruited and loaded in the same way.

❗<span style="background:#fff88f">2 helicase molecules bind to ori at G1 phase, but are not activated until S phase.</span>

## Binding proteins

ssDNA strands are unstable and fragile, or forming unwanted secondary structure. RPA immediately binds to, stabilize and protect ssDNA, keeping it straight and accessible for enzymes like DNA polymerase.

Key features: 
➢ Stabilise ssDNA 
➢ Protect from nucleases 
➢ Prevent helix re-association 
➢ Prevent formation of secondary structures 
➢ Help with recruitment of other proteins

**Prokaryotes: Single Stranded Binding protein (SSB)** 
**Eukaryotes: Replication factor A (RFA)**

![[capture_20251207211116080.bmp]]

## Primase

Key features: 
➢ It is an RNA polymerase 
➢ It adds an ~10bp RNA primer in 5’ - 3’ direction for DNA polymerase to bind 
➢ RNA primer is degraded by: DNA polymerase I with its 5′ → 3′ exonuclease activity in prokaryotes RNase H and FEN1 in eukaryotes
![[f0db8de8a19775791ae502860771dbbb.jpg|475]]


----

## Topoisomerase

**Prokaryotes: Topo I, Gyrase (type II)** 
**Eukaryotes: Topo I,  Topo II** 

Key features: 
➢ Act on dsDNA, ahead of helicase 
➢ Transiently break and reseal DNA to relieve torsional stress (create negative supercoil to neutralise the positive supercoil produced during replication)
➢Topoisomerase:
1. Topo I: ssDNA cut; no ATP required (directly use the distortional stress of DNA)
2. Topo II: dsDNA cut; use ATP
![[capture_20251207212022201.bmp|300]]

## Sliding clamp

Key features:
➢ Highly stable ring-shaped complex 
➢ Highly conserved 
➢ Binds and keeps polymerase tethered to DNA 
➢ Remains associated until replication ends

**Prokaryotes: β sliding clamp**
**Eukaryotes: proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA)**

![[capture_20251207213538097.bmp|450]]

-----

## Clamp loader

Key features:
➢ 5-subunit ring structure 
➢ Opens clamp and loads it onto DNA, so the clamp can load DNA polymerase <span style="background:#fff88f">(Does not load DNA polymerase directly)</span>
➢ ATP hydrolysis binds clamp on DNA and releases loader 
➢ Loader removes clamp when replication finishes (Firstly removes clamp, and then remove DNA polymerase. The loader cannot attach to the clamp and the DNA polymerase at the same time)

**Prokaryotes: τ/γ complex loader**
**Eukaryotes: Replication factor C (RFC)**

## DNA polymerase

**Prokaryotes**
E. Coli has at least five DNA polymerases, Pol I-V
![[e53766d010763d0863057f9925b0123f.jpg]]

**Eukaryotes**
Eukaryotes have typically >15
![[08cf2cfd960b082b503aeeec910c033b.jpg]]

### DNA polymerase structure
<font color="#00b050">DNA replication is extremely accurate (mistake occurs 1/10 million nucleotides)</font>

![[capture_20251207212903534.bmp|325]]

Palm (most important, where the polymerase activity takes place): 
➢ <span style="background:#fff88f">Most highly conserved </span>
➢ Rigid 
➢ Composed of a β sheet core, flanked by α helices 
➢ <span style="background:#fff88f">Catalyses phosphodiester bond formation</span> 
➢ <span style="background:#fff88f">Proofreading (3' to 5' exonuclease) activity</span>

Fingers: 
➢ Composed of α helices and loops 
➢ Flexible 
➢ <span style="background:#fff88f">Enclose, bind and align complementary incoming dNTPs</span> 
➢ <span style="background:#fff88f">Bring dNTPs in contact with the palm </span>
➢ Protect ssDNA

Thumb:
➢ Composed of α helices 
➢ Rigid 
➢ <span style="background:#fff88f">Stabilises Polymerase/DNA association </span>
➢ Ensures polymerase processivity


### Process of DNA polymerisation
➢ ssDNA winds through the fingers and guided towards the palm 
➢ Growing dsDNA fits into the palm 
➢ Fingers scan and help position incoming dNTP 
➢ Correct dNTP is aligned 
➢ Fingers close around the base-paired dNTP 
➢ Palm catalyses the base-paired dNTP phosphodiester bond 
➢ Palm performs proofreading 
➢ Thumb stabilises the elongated dsDNA:Polymerase complex 
➢ Attachment of paired dNTP to the primer leads to opening of fingers 
➢ Next dNTP addition - cycle begins again

## DNA polymerase holoenzyme

It is crucial to: 
▪ Keep DNA ss as little as possible 
▪ Couple leading + lagging strand synthesis
Therefore:
▪ Multiple polymerases function at the same time (Prokaryotic: DNA polymerase III; Eukaryotic: DNA polymerases δ (lagging) and ε (leading))

Key features:
➢ 3x core pol (the third pol is used to start the next Okazaki fragment on the lagging strand while another pol on lagging strand is still finishing the previous one)
➢ 2-3x clamps (one more for the lagging strand)
➢ 1x loader

## Ligase

Several for both prokaryotes and eukaryotes.

Key features: 
➢ Identifies and binds DNA nick (3′-OH and 5′-P ends) 
➢ <span style="background:#fff88f">Requires ATP in eukaryotes (ATP → AMP + PPi) / NAD⁺ in bacteria</span>
➢ Covalently joins adjacent nucleotides 
➢ Seals ss and ds breaks 
➢ Repairs DNA after RNA primer removal 
➢ Seal nicks between Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand
