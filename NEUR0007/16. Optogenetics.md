
# I. Definition and Core Components of Optogenetics

## A. Definition (Slide: “Controlling neurones with light”)

Optogenetics consists of two essential components:

### 1. Genetic targeting

- Specific neurones are targeted with genes encoding:
    - Light-sensitive ion channels
    - Ion pumps
    - GPCRs

### 2. Optical stimulation

- Light is delivered to neurones expressing these proteins
- Typically uses **blue light**

---

## Integrated Principle:

Optogenetics enables:

### **Control of neurones using light via genetically encoded actuators**


---

## D. Molecular Mechanism (Slide: 470 nm)

### Sequence:

1. Light absorption (≈470 nm)
2. Conformational change in protein
3. Channel gating
4. Ion flow (e.g. Na⁺)
5. Membrane depolarisation

---

### **Part 1: Answers to Core Learning Objectives**

#### **1. How ChR2 Was Discovered, Characterised and Developed for Use in Neurones**

- **Discovery:** Channelrhodopsin-2 (ChR2) was originally discovered in the green alga _Chlamydomonas reinhardtii_. In its native organism, it serves as a "visual" protein that regulates phototaxis, allowing the algae to move toward or away from an ambient light source.
    
- **Characterisation:** Biophysical characterisation revealed that ChR2 is a directly light-gated **ionotropic** cation channel. Upon absorbing blue light ($\sim$470 nm), the channel undergoes a conformational shift that opens its core pore, allowing a non-selective influx of cations ($Na^+$, $H^+$, $K^+$, and $Ca^{2+}$) down their natural electrochemical gradients.
    
- **Development for Neurones:** Neuroscientists engineered the genetic sequence of ChR2 to express it in mammalian neurons. Because it functions as a single-protein unit containing both the light sensor and the ion pore, introducing its gene into a neuron makes that cell immediately responsive to light. Delivering brief flashes of blue light triggers rapid, non-selective cation influx, producing a strong inward current that causes immediate membrane depolarization. This drives the cell past its action potential threshold, enabling sub-millisecond optical excitation.
    

---

#### **2. Selective Activation and Inhibition of Neurones by Location, Cell Type, Projection Target, or a Combination**

- **By Location:** Achieved by stereotaxically microinjecting a viral vector (such as an Adeno-Associated Virus, AAV) carrying the opsin gene directly into an anatomically restricted brain nucleus, combined with localized implantation of a cranial optic fiber.
    
- **By Cell Type:** Exploits specific cell-type genetic promoters. The opsin gene can be packaged inside a Cre-dependent vector cassette (such as a Double-floxed Inverted Open-reading frame, **DIO**, or **FLEX** switch) where the gene is initially inverted and inactive. When this virus is injected into a transgenic mouse line expressing _Cre recombinase_ under a cell-specific promoter (e.g., _Vgat-Cre_ for GABAergic neurons or _Vglut2-Cre_ for glutamatergic neurons), the Cre enzyme flips the inverted opsin into the correct sense orientation, restricting expression to that specific cell type.
    
- **By Projection Target:** Targeted by delivering light specifically to axon terminals (**terminal photostimulation**). For a pathway running from Region A to Region B, the virus is injected into the cell bodies of Region A. The translated opsin protein traffics down the long-range axons to the terminals in Region B. Placing an optic fiber exclusively over Region B allows the investigator to control only the axon terminals that project to that target area.
    
- **Combination Targeting:** Combining a cell-type specific transgenic line (_Cre_ mouse), a local Cre-dependent viral injection (_DIO-opsin_), and target-restricted optic fiber placement allows an investigator to manipulate a single cellular phenotype projecting to one specific downstream zone.
    

---

#### **3. Behavioral Roles of Cell Types and Circuit-Level Mapping**

- **Linking Circuit to Behaviour:** Optogenetics allows investigators to manipulate distinct, genetically specified pathways in awake, behaving animals to determine the necessity and sufficiency of specific circuits in real time. For example, optic fibers implanted bilaterally can deliver light to excite or inhibit a target population during an active behavioral essay, establishing a direct causal link between cellular firing and systemic behavioral outputs.
    
- **Circuit-Level Mapping (CRACM):** Channelrhodopsin-Assisted Circuit Mapping (CRACM) is used to map functional synaptic connections within complex networks.
    
    1. A virus carrying ChR2 is injected into an upstream source area, and the opsin translates and fills the entire axonal arborization running to a distant target region.
        
    2. Acute brain slices of the target region are prepared. These slices contain the dendrites and cell bodies of local target neurons, intermingled with the severed ChR2-expressing axon terminals originating from the distant source area.
        
    3. Whole-cell patch-clamp recordings are performed from selected target cells under voltage-clamp configuration while flashing blue light across the slice.
        
    4. Because the severed axon terminals retain functional ChR2 channels, light depolarization drives local transmitter release. Recording a post-synaptic current (EPSC/IPSC) provides immediate biophysical proof of a functional synaptic connection onto that specific cell type.
        

---

#### **4. Current Limitations of and Challenges for the Field of Optogenetics**

- **Light Attenuation and Scattering:** Photons scatter and attenuate significantly as they pass through dense, lipid-rich brain tissue. This limits the depth of effective light penetration, requiring invasive, rigid glass optic fibers to be implanted directly into deep structures, which can cause local tissue inflammation and structural tract damage.
    
- **Over-expression and Photocurrent Desensitization:** High viral titers can drive excessive expression levels of microbial opsins, occasionally leading to cellular toxicity, altered baseline membrane properties, or distorted vesicular docking kinetics. Furthermore, continuous or high-frequency light stimulation can induce desensitization of the channel pore, decreasing the amplitude of successive photocurrents.
    
- **Non-Physiological Synchrony:** Optogenetic stimulation forces an entire population of ChR2-expressing neurons to fire action potentials simultaneously in perfect synchrony. This artificial synchronization bypasses the natural, sparse, and temporally staggered firing patterns that these circuits use during normal endogenous computation.
    

---

#### **5. Discovery, Development, and Modification of New Optogenetic Actuators**

- **Discovery:** New actuators are identified by screening genomes of diverse microbial organisms, such as specialized bacteria, archaea, and marine algae found in extreme environments.
    
- **Development for Improved Properties:** Newly discovered opsins are engineered via site-directed mutagenesis to optimize three core biophysical properties:
    
    1. _Spectral Red-Shifting:_ Developing opsins sensitive to longer wavelengths of light (red light). Red light scatters less in lipid tissue, allowing deeper penetration and enabling non-invasive transcranial stimulation or independent dual-color control when combined with a blue-shifted opsin.
        
    2. _Altered Ion Selectivity:_ Engineering the amino acid residues lining the channel pore to shift selectivity (e.g., converting a cation-selective ChR2 into an anion-selective chloride channel, such as iChLOCC, to achieve light-gated inhibition).
        
    3. _Modified Kinetics:_ Altering the step-function kinetics to create either ultra-fast opsins (capable of tracking high-frequency firing up to 100 Hz without spike failure) or step-function opsins (with stabilized open states that keep channels open for minutes following a brief pulse of light, eliminating the need for continuous illumination).

### **Part 2: Answers to Handout Example Questions**

#### **Q1: Explain how ChR2 was discovered and subsequently developed. Contrast its properties to those of another optogenetic actuator.**

##### **Discovery and Development**

Channelrhodopsin-2 (ChR2) was identified as a light-sensitive visual protein in the green alga _Chlamydomonas reinhardtii_. It was subsequently developed for neurobiology by cloning its gene and inserting it into viral expression vectors (such as AAVs) under the control of cell-type-specific promoters. Because ChR2 is a single, self-contained macromolecule containing both the light-absorbing chromophore and an intrinsic ion channel pore, it requires no external helper proteins. When expressed in mammalian neurons, it embeds directly into the plasma membrane, rendering the cell responsive to light.

##### **Contrast of Properties: ChR2 vs. Halorhodopsin (NpHR)**

|**Biophysical Property**|**Channelrhodopsin-2 (ChR2)**|**Halorhodopsin (NpHR)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Biological Source**|Green Algae (_Chlamydomonas reinhardtii_)|Halophilic Archaea (_Natronomonas pharaonis_)|
|**Protein Classification**|Light-gated **ionotropic channel**|Light-driven **ionotropic ion pump**|
|**Optimal Wavelength**|**Blue Light** ($\sim$470 nm)|**Yellow Light** ($\sim$589 nm)|
|**Ion Species Directed**|Cations ($Na^+$, $Ca^{2+}$, $H^+$, $K^+$)|Anions ($Cl^-$)|
|**Direction of Flux**|Passive influx down electrochemical gradient|Active transport (1 $Cl^-$ ion per photon) into cytosol|
|**Net Membrane Effect**|Rapid **Depolarization**|Immediate **Hyperpolarization**|
|**Functional Application**|Optical **Excitation** (drives action potentials)|Optical **Inhibition** (suppresses action potentials)|

#### **Q2: Explain how newly discovered novel optogenetic actuators might be modified to improve their experimental usefulness.**

When a novel optogenetic actuator is discovered in nature, its wild-type properties are often suboptimal for precise circuit interrogation. Investigators use genetic engineering and site-directed mutagenesis to systematically modify the protein's primary structure to maximize its experimental utility across three main axes:

##### **1. Tuning Kinetic Properties (Speed and Open-State Stability)**

- **Ultra-Fast Kinetics:** To drive high-frequency spike trains without structural failure, mutations can be introduced into the channel's gating domain to accelerate its deactivation time constant ($\tau_{\text{off}}$). This prevents current plateaus and allows the channel to precisely track high-frequency laser pulses (up to 100 Hz).
    
- **Step-Function Stabilisation:** Conversely, the channel can be modified to slow its closing kinetics. By stabilizing the open conformation, a brief millisecond pulse of light opens the pore, and it remains open for minutes until turned off by a different wavelength of light. This allows long-term modulation of network excitability while minimizing tissue heating caused by continuous laser illumination.
    

##### **2. Red-Shifting the Absorption Spectrum (Deeper, Less Invasive Penetration)**

- Wild-type opsins often respond best to blue or green light, which scatter heavily in brain tissue. By substituting amino acid residues within the retinal-binding pocket, engineers can shift the protein's peak sensitivity toward **red light ($\sim$635–650 nm)**. Red light penetrates tissue more deeply with less scattering, enabling non-invasive transcranial stimulation of deep structures and allowing independent control of two distinct cell types when paired with a blue-sensitive opsin.
    

##### **3. Modifying Ion Pore Selectivity**

- To transform a newly discovered excitatory channel into an inhibitory tool, mutations can be introduced into the central pore's selectivity filter. Replacing negatively charged amino acids with positively charged residues switches the pore from a cation-selective channel to an **anion-selective channel**. Upon light illumination, this engineered channel drives a selective influx of chloride ions ($Cl^-$), causing membrane hyperpolarization and providing an alternative pathway for optical inhibition.

### **Part 3: Linkage to Experimental Methods (Exam Focus)**

To evaluate an optogenetic circuit mechanism or verify synaptic parameters within problem-solving exam questions, apply the following electrophysiological protocols and logical frameworks:

#### **1. Differentiating Monosynaptic vs. Polysynaptic Projections in CRACM**

- **The Problem:** Flashing blue light over a target brain slice during a CRACM experiment evokes a clear post-synaptic current. How do you prove that this response represents a direct **monosynaptic** connection from the ChR2-expressing terminals, rather than an indirect **polysynaptic** network effect (where ChR2 terminals excite a local intermediate neuron, which then synapses onto your patched cell)?
    
- **The Method:** Apply a pharmacological cocktail of **Tetrodotoxin (TTX)** and **4-Aminopyridine (4-AP)** to the recording bath.
    
- **The Experimental Logic:** 1. **TTX** blocks voltage-gated $Na^+$ channels, completely abolishing action potential propagation throughout the slice and shutting down all polysynaptic network relays.
    
    2. Under TTX alone, normal action potential-evoked transmitter release is blocked. However, because ChR2 is a directly light-gated ion channel, a strong pulse of blue light can still depolarize the immediate terminal membrane enough to open local voltage-gated calcium channels and trigger vesicle fusion, provided voltage-gated $K^+$ channels do not instantly repolarize the terminal.
    
    3. Adding **4-AP** blocks these voltage-gated $K^+$ channels, prolonging the light-induced depolarization at the terminal and rescuing vesicle release.
    
    4. **The Diagnostic Proof:** If the light-evoked post-synaptic current **persists in the presence of TTX + 4-AP**, the connection is definitively proven to be **monosynaptic**. If the response is completely abolished by TTX and cannot be rescued by 4-AP, the original current was **polysynaptic**.
    

#### **2. Mapping Synaptic Subcellular Topography via Laser-Scanning Photostimulation**

- **The Method:** Combine whole-cell voltage-clamp recordings of a principal cell with automated, grid-based laser-scanning photostimulation across a brain slice containing ChR2-expressing projection axons.
    
- **The Experimental Logic:** By systematically flashing a finely focused blue laser beam across an array of coordinates covering the cell's dendritic arbor, investigators can map exactly where functional synapses are located.
    
    - If flashing light at a specific grid coordinate yields a fast, time-locked post-synaptic current, it marks a functional synaptic site at that point on the dendrite.
        
    - Plotting the peak current amplitudes across the coordinate grid generates a high-resolution map of functional input density, revealing whether the projection pathway preferentially targets the proximal dendrites, distal apical tufts, or the soma of the target cell population.
