
# **I. The Physical Basis of Ion Permeation**

### **1. The Hydration Barrier**
- **Problem:** Ions in solution are surrounded by a **hydration shell** (water molecules attracted by the ion's charge). Crossing the hydrophobic lipid bilayer requires the ion to shed this shell, which is energetically unfavorable.
- **Solution:** Ion channels are integral membrane proteins that provide an **hydrophilic aqueous pore**, allowing hydrated ions to cross the membrane down their electrochemical gradient without entering the non-polar lipid environment.
    
### **2. The Selectivity Filter**
- **Mechanism:** Channels distinguish between ions (e.g., $Na^+$ vs. $K^+$) using a specialized region called the **selectivity filter**. They discriminate among possible permeant ions on the basis of ionic charge, ionic diameter and weak electrostatic interaction of the ion with charged amino-acid residues that line the wall of the channel.
- **Energetics:** In a $K^+$ channel, carbonyl oxygens from the protein backbone line the filter. They are spaced precisely to mimic the hydration shell of a $K^+$ ion. A $Na^+$ ion is too small to coordinate perfectly with these oxygens; therefore, the energetic cost of desolvation is not recovered, and $Na^+$ cannot pass.

# **II. Molecular Architecture and Activity**

### **1. Subunit Composition and Stoichiometry**
Ion channels are multimeric proteins. The number of subunits or domains determines the "fold" of the channel:
- **Tetrameric (4 subunits):** Typical of Voltage-Gated $K^+$ ($K_v$) channels. 6TM (S1-S6).
- **Pseudotetrameric:** Voltage-Gated $Na^+$ ($Na_v$) and $Ca^{2+}$ ($Ca_v$) channels consist of one massive $\alpha$-subunit with four internal repeats (domains I-IV). 6TM (S1-S6).
	- **S1–S4:** Form the **Voltage-Sensor Domain (VSD)**.
		- S4: Main voltage sensor. Each is characterised by 4 positive charges (arginine or lysine).
		- High sensitivity of channel gating: Results from the transport of 16 charges in totle across the membrane.
    - **S5–S6 + P-loop:** Form the **Pore Domain**.
	    - S6: Form intracellular activation gate.
### 2. Resting state (hyperpolarisation)
- S4 positively charged residues are electrostatitically pulled inward by the negative charge of intracellular environment
- S6 intracellular gate closed
- No ion conduction
### 3. Channel opening (depolarisation)
#### Process
- Intracellular environment becomes more positive
- Outward electrostatic force on S4 positive charges develops
  → S4 segments rotate by 180° as they moves outward
- S4 is mechanically linked to S4-S5 lilnker.
- The tilting motion of S4 and S5 induces a concerted movement of S4-S5 linker and S6, allowing ion conduction.
### **2. Inactivation ("Ball and Chain" Model)**
- **N-type Inactivation:** A fast inactivation mechanism where the N-terminus of the protein (the "ball") physically plugs the open pore from the cytoplasmic side.
- Can be abolished by Shaker mutant (removal of "ball and chain" domain).
#### Process
- N-terminal intracellular region acts as tethered “ball”
  A flexible linker acts as "chain"
- After channel opening, this ball rapidly swings into intracellular pore entrance
- Physically occludes ion conduction pathway

# **IV. Experimental Methods in Molecular Characterization**

### **1. Site-Directed Mutagenesis**
- **Method:** Transfect clonal cell lines with site-directed mutant genes by mixing cultured cells and cDNA together.
- **Purpose:** To identify which amino acids are critical for function.
- **Example:** Mutating the positively charged residues in S4 reduces the voltage sensitivity of the channel.

### **2. Heterologous Expression Systems**
- **Systems:** _Xenopus_ oocytes or HEK293 cells.
- **Logic:** By injecting mRNA for a specific channel into a cell that doesn't normally have it, you can isolate and study the properties of that single protein using electrophysiology (e.g. patch-clamping).

### **3. Chimeragenesis (Pore Transplantation)**
- **Method:** Swapping the P-loop region between two different channels (e.g., a TEA-sensitive channel and a TEA-insensitive channel).
- **Conclusion:** If the sensitivity moves with the P-loop, it proves the P-loop forms the drug-binding site and the pore.

# **V. Key Formulas and Constants**

1. **Probability of Opening ($P_{open}$):**
    $$P_{open} = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(\frac{-z_g F (V_m - V_{1/2})}{RT}\right)}$$
    
    _(Where $V_{1/2}$ is the voltage at which 50% of channels are open)._
    
1. **Single Channel Conductance ($g$):**
    $$g = \frac{i}{V_m - E_{rev}}$$
    
2. **Macroscopic Current ($I$):**
    $$I = N \cdot g \cdot P_{open} \cdot (V_m - E_{rev})$$
    
    _(This connects the molecular properties $g$ and $P_{open}$ to the whole-cell current measured in your exam traces).

### **Part 1: Learning Objectives**

#### **1. What are ion channels and their main functional features?**
Ion channels are large, integral membrane proteins that form aqueous pores across the lipid bilayer.
- **Passive Transport:** They allow ions to flow down their electrochemical gradients (they do not require ATP directly).
- **Selectivity:** They can distinguish between ion species (e.g., $K^{+}$ vs $Na^{+}$) based on charge and size.
- **Gating:** They transition between open and closed states in response to specific stimuli (voltage, ligands, or mechanical stress).
- **High Conductance:** They support very high rates of ion flow (up to $10^8$ ions/second), far exceeding the speed of transporters.
    
#### **2. Techniques and discoveries demonstrating the existence of ion channels.**
- **Gramicidin:** Early evidence showed that small peptides could form pores in artificial membranes, allowing discrete current flow.
- **Patch Clamp Technique (Neher & Sakmann):** The breakthrough that allowed the recording of current through a **single** ion channel, proving that channels open and close in an "all-or-none" quantized fashion.
- **Molecular Cloning:** Identified the primary amino acid sequences, showing that channels are complex proteins with specific membrane-spanning domains.
- **X-ray Crystallography (MacKinnon):** Provided the 3D atomic structure of the KcsA $K^{+}$ channel, revealing the physical nature of the selectivity filter.
    
#### **3. Structure: How channels look, span the membrane, and their functional domains.**
Most voltage-gated channels follow a tetrameric or pseudo-tetrameric symmetry:
- **Alpha-Subunit:** The main pore-forming protein. In $Na^{+}$ and $Ca^{2+}$ channels, this is one large protein with four repeats. In $K^{+}$ channels, four separate subunits combine.
- **Pore Domain (S5-P loop-S6):** The P-loop sits between the S5 and S6 transmembrane segments, forming the selectivity filter.
- **Voltage Sensor Domain (VSD):** Segments S1 through S4. The **S4 segment** contains positively charged residues (Arginine/Lysine) and acts as the primary sensor for membrane potential changes.
    
#### **4. Variety of ion channels and diverse signaling.**
Different neurons express different combinations of channel subtypes. For example, $K^{+}$ channels have the highest diversity (e.g., delayed rectifiers, A-type, $Ca^{2+}$-activated). The specific "cocktail" of channels determines if a neuron fires rapidly, slowly, or in bursts, allowing for specialized signaling across different brain regions.

### **Part 2: Example Questions**

#### **Q1: Describe the experimental evidence that led to our understanding of the existence and main functional features of ion channels.**
Evidence includes the **Patch Clamp technique**, which demonstrated that currents are not continuous but occur in discrete steps (quantized). **Single-channel recordings** showed that ion flow is passive and extremely fast, ruling out carrier proteins. Furthermore, **pore transplantation (chimeragenesis)** experiments proved that swapping the P-loop between channels changed their selectivity and pharmacology (e.g., TEA sensitivity), confirming the pore's location.

#### **Q2: What is meant by ion channel "gating" and "gating currents"? What is the structural correlate of gating?**
- **Gating:** The process of switching between open, closed, and inactivated states.
- **Gating Currents:** Minute currents caused by the movement of the charged **S4 segments** within the membrane field _before_ the pore actually opens.
- **Structural Correlate:** The **S4 segment**. In response to depolarization, the positive charges in S4 move outward, which pulls the **S4-S5 linker**. This mechanical pull moves the S6 segments to widen the intracellular gate of the pore.

#### **Q3: What is meant by "inactivation"? Which molecular mechanism is responsible for it, and how was it experimentally demonstrated?**
- **Inactivation:** A state where the channel pore is physically blocked even while the stimulus (e.g., depolarization) is still present.
- **Molecular Mechanism:** The **"Ball and Chain" model (N-type inactivation)**. The N-terminus of the channel protein acts as a tethered "ball" that swings into the open pore to block it.
- **Experimental Demonstration:** 1. **Proteolysis:** Treating the inside of the membrane with enzymes (pronase) that "clip" the N-terminus abolished inactivation.
    2. **Synthetic Peptide:** Adding a synthetic version of the N-terminal "ball" back to a clipped channel restored inactivation._
