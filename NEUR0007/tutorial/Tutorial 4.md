## Introduction

The chemical synapse is the central junction for information transfer and plasticity in the mammalian nervous system, where electrical signals are transduced into chemical messages. Presynaptic action potentials trigger a $Ca^{2+}$ influx, driving the SNARE-mediated release of neurotransmitters into the synaptic cleft. These molecules bind to postsynaptic receptors, modulating membrane potentials or initiating intracellular cascades. This process forms the biological basis of behavior and consciousness. Understanding these mechanisms requires isolating the influence of specific components, such as ion channels and receptors, on synaptic responses. The following four methodologies have contributed most to understanding the mechanisms of particular chemical synapses.

## **1. Pharmacological Block**

Pharmacological block uses target-specific ligands to isolate consequent synaptic events, identifying the specific ion channels responsible for observed currents and characterizing their conductance and kinetics. 
#### Key Discoveries:
- In the characterization of potassium channels, Ruppersberg et al. used tetraethylammonium (TEA) and dendrotoxin (DTX) as diagnostic tools. The high sensitivity of RCK1 versus the complete insensitivity of RCK4 allowed for the identification of heteromultimeric RCK1,4 channels, which exhibited intermediate sensitivity.
- Takahashi et al. employed this method to determine how presynaptic $Ca^{2+}$ currents are modulated by glutamate receptors at mammalian synapses. They utilized $\omega$-agatoxin IVA to identify that these currents are predominantly mediated by P/Q-type $Ca^{2+}$ channels, as the specific blocker abolished $96\%$ of the current. Furthermore, they used the agonist L-AP4 to identify mGluR-mediated suppression of $Ca^{2+}$ influx, confirming through combined application with the $Na^{+}$ channel blocker TTX that this effect resulted from decreased $Ca^{2+}$ influx rather than increased $K^{+}$ conductance. 
- Additionally, Tsien et al. used CNQX in blocking AMPA receptors to isolate NMDA-mediated currents, which is crucial in verifying the successful knockout of NMDA in CA1 region in CA1-KO mice.

## **2. Gene Knockout**

Gene knockout methodologies disrupts the gene expression of the synaptic component, and allow researchers to investigate the function of it by comparing experimental models with control groups.  It can be classified as eith conventional or conditional gene knockout. To generate a conventional knockout, researchers use electroporation to deliver a targeting vector into embryonic stem cells, where homology arms facilitate homologous recombination to replace the target gene with a marker. However, the target gene is knocked out in all cells, which results in neonatal lethality or developmental compensations. In contrast, conditional gene knockout uses Cre-loxP system and causes gene deletion within restricted brain regions or specific cell types. This system utilizes "floxed" target genes flanked by loxP sequences; regional deletion occurs only when the organism is crossed with a line expressing Cre recombinase under a tissue-specific promoter.
#### Key Discoveries:
- Tsien et al. (1996) utilized this to create the CA1-KO mouse, deleting the essential NMDAR1 subunit exclusively in hippocampal CA1 pyramidal cells. The resulting mice were viable and displayed normal nonspatial learning, yet they exhibited a total lack of NMDAR-mediated currents and long-term potentiation in the CA1. Behavioral deficits in the Morris water maze confirmed that NMDAR-dependent plasticity in this specific region is essential for the formation of spatial memory.

## **3. Heterologous Expression**

The biophysical characterization of specific synaptic components is often obscured by the heterogeneity of native neurons. Heterologous expression involves introducing foreign cDNA or cRNA of a certain synaptic component into a "blank-canvas" host, such as HeLa cells or _Xenopus_ oocytes, to study the properties and functions it in isolation. 
#### Key Discoveries:
- Ruppersberg et al. expressed RCK1 and RCK4 independently and in combination to reveal that their co-expression produces RCK1,4 heteromultimers with distinct hybrid kinetics, conductance and pharmacological properties. This finding suggested that the combinatorial assembly of a limited number of ion channel subunits may contribute to functional diversity in the mammalian brain.

## **4. Paired Recordings**

Paired recordings involve the simultaneous electrophysiological recordings of presynaptic and postsynaptic terminals, allowing researchers to directly correlate a presynaptic trigger with its resulting postsynaptic response. Due to the small size of most mammalian terminals, this technique often utilizes giant synapses such as the Calyx of Held in the rat brainstem.
#### Key Discoveries:
- By recording the presynaptic $Ca^{2+}$ current and the excitatory postsynaptic current (EPSC) concurrently, Takahashi et al. proved that mGluR agonists inhibit transmitter release by suppressing high-voltage-activated P/Q-type $Ca^{2+}$ conductances. Crucially, by comparing the $Ca^{2+}$-EPSC relationship during mGluR activation to the relationship observed during a simple reduction of external $Ca^{2+}$ concentration, they established that presynaptic inhibition is accounted for by the suppression of $Ca^{2+}$ influx rather than effects on the downstream exocytotic machinery.
