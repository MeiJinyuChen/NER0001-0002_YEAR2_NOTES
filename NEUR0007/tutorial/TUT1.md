
## Heterologous Expression Models

HeLa cells & Xenopus oocytes were transfected with:
1. RCK1 alone
2. RCK4 alone
3. RCK1 + RCK4 together

---

## I. Pharmacological sensitivity to TEA and DTX

Purpose:  
To determine whether coexpression of RCK1 and RCK4 produces channels with distinct pharmacological properties.

### Process

1. Transfect HeLa cells with plasmids containing cDNAs encoding RCK1 or RCK4.
2. Whole-cell patch clamp recordings were performed in transfected HeLa cells.
Voltage protocol:
- holding potential ≈ −80 mV
- depolarizing step to 0 mV
Currents recorded before with the application, and after the washout of:
- TEA (tetraethylammonium)
- DTX (dendrotoxin)

### Observation

|           | Observation                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| RCK1      | - sustained noninactivating outward current<br>- strongly blocked by 10mM TEA                                                                                            |
| RCK4      | - transient outward current<br>- almost insensitive to 10 mM TEA<br>- unaffected by 100 mM TEA                                                                           |
| RCK1+RCK4 | transient current component<br>- transient component:<br>    - half-blocked by 10 mM TEA<br>    - almost completely blocked by 100 mM TEA<br>    - reduced by 100 nM DTX |
Similar pharmacological differences were observed in oocytes.

### Main conclusion

Coexpression generates channels with:
- RCK4-like transient kinetics  
    BUT
- RCK1-like pharmacology
→ Suggests:
- Heteromultimeric assembly
- rather than independent homomeric populations.

## II. Gating and conductance properties

- Cell-attached macro patches on Xenopus laevis oocytes
- TEA (10mM) to block non-activating currents
To evaluate whether these properties or the formation of the heteromultimeric channels depend on the expression system:
- Compared whole-cell currents recorded in Hela-cells transfected with RCK4 and with RCK1 + RCK4 cRNA.
  → Differences in saturation of the peak-transient currents were consistent in the 2 types of transfected Hela cells.

### 1. Instantaneous Voltage Stimuli (a)

#### Pocess

- Membrane held at –80 mV
- Voltage stepped from –50 mV to +80 mV in 10 mV increments

| Group       | the peak of the transient current saturats at | Inactivation during the test pulse |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| RCK1 + RCK4 | + 20 mV                                       | Little inactivation                |
| RCK4        | No saturation up to +40 mV                    | Begin to inactivate                |

#### Interpretation

Heteromers display:
- hybrid gating behavior
- not identical to either parent channel

### 2. Tail Current Experiment (b)

#### Process

- Prepulse to 0 mV to fully activate channels
- Step membrane to different test voltages (–80 to +60 mV)
- Measure instantaneous current immediately after voltage change

#### Measure

- Open-channel I–V relationship → Intrinsic conductance properties
- Driving force independent of activation kinetics

#### Purpose

To determine whether differences between channels are due to:
- gating/inactivation differences  
    or
- intrinsic permeation/conductance differences

## III. Peak current-voltage relationship

### Purpose

To compare voltage-dependent activation properties.

### Method

Peak transient current amplitudes measured at:
- different test voltages.

### Observation

#### RCK1

- current saturates above +20 mV

#### RCK4

- no saturation up to +40 mV

#### RCK1,4

- intermediate behavior

### Interpretation

Differences reflect:
- distinct gating mechanisms,
- altered voltage dependence,
- heteromeric channel behavior.

## IV. Steady-state Inactivation Behaviour

- Cell-attached current recordings
- In bath solution containing 10 mM TEA
- HeLa cells
- Responses to test pulses to 0 mV from different prepulse potentials

#### Process

##### First depolarizing pulse (conditioning pulse)

- Voltage stepped to **0 mV**
- Duration: **5 seconds**
- Purpose:
    - Opens channels
    - Drives transient channels into **inactivated state**

##### Recovery interval

- Membrane returned to **–80 mV**
- Variable recovery duration:
    - Short to long intervals
- Purpose:
    - Allows channels to recover from inactivation

### Observation

RCK1,4: 
- Inactivation curves slightly steeper than RCK4
- Recover more than 2 times faster from inactivation caused by depolarizing pulse than RCK4

### Interpretation

Heteromerization alters kinetic properties.
