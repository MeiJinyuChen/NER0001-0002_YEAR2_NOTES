

## **I. Temporal Classification of Synaptic Plasticity**

Synaptic changes span multiple distinct timescales, each serving a unique role in network computation and information storage:
- **(Very) Short-Term Plasticity:** Operates across a timescale of **milliseconds to seconds**. Classic examples include **Paired-Pulse Facilitation (PPF)** and **Paired-Pulse Depression (PPD)**.
- **Short-Term Plasticity:** Persists over a timescale of **minutes**. This includes phenomena such as **Post-Tetanic Potentiation (PTP)**, **Frequency-Dependent Depression**, and **Short-Term Potentiation (STP)**.
- **Long-Term Plasticity:** Persists over **hours, days, weeks, or years**. This includes **Long-Term Potentiation (LTP)** and **Long-Term Depression (LTD)**, which provide the structural basis for persistent memory retention.

## II. Paired-Pulse Facilitation/Depression
![[Pasted image 20260516232527.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260516232717.png]]

When two presynaptic action potentials invade a terminal in rapid succession, the secondary response ($EPSC_2$) deviates from the baseline initial response ($EPSC_1$). The direction of this change serves as a diagnostic for baseline presynaptic characteristics:
### **1. Paired-Pulse Facilitation (PPF)**
- **Locus and Cause:** Facilitation is entirely **presynaptic** and is characteristic of synapses with a **low initial release probability ($p$)**.
- **Biophysical Mechanism:** The first action potential triggers minimal vesicle release but drives a massive influx of intracellular calcium ($Ca^{2+}$). Before the terminal clearance mechanisms can fully sequester or pump out this $Ca^{2+}$, a second action potential arrives. The **residual calcium** left from the first pulse sums with the fresh calcium influx triggered by the second pulse. This elevated local concentration acts on the low-affinity vesicular sensor, dramatically boosting the probability of release ($p$) for the remaining vesicles, causing $EPSC_2 > EPSC_1$.
### **2. Paired-Pulse Depression (PPD)**
- **Locus and Cause:** Depression is driven by a **presynaptic** mechanism characteristic of synapses with a **high initial release probability ($p$)**.
- **Biophysical Mechanism:** Because the baseline probability of release is high, the initial action potential triggers the exocytosis of a substantial fraction of the **Readily Releasable Pool (RRP)** of vesicles. When the second action potential arrives shortly after, it encounters a cleared and depleted active zone. Despite the presence of residual calcium, the physical depletion of available, primed vesicles limits further exocytosis, causing $EPSC_2 < EPSC_1$.

## III. Experimental Evidence for Paired-Pulse Facilitation
![[Pasted image 20260517152142.png]]

### 1. Inject fluorescent Ca²⁺ indicators into large terminals
e.g. Fura-2, Fluo-4
#### Process
- Establish a whole-cell patch clamp filled with internal pipette solution containing a fluorescent Ca²⁺ indicators, on presynaptic terminal of large synapse model (e.g. Calyx of Held, Squid giant synapse)
- Allow dye to diffuse throughout terminal
- Optionally establish postsynaptic patch clamp simultaneously
- Deliver two closely spaced (paired) presynaptic stimuli
	- Presynaptic depolarisation opens VGCCs
	- Ca²⁺ influx binds fluorescent dye
- Measure presynaptic Ca²⁺ transients  = fluorescent changes using: 
	- Fast CCD camera
	- Photodiode
	- Two-photon microscopy
- Simultaneously record postsynaptic EPSCs if required
- Compare:
    - Ca²⁺ fluorescence signals
    - Paired-pulse ratio
    - Residual Ca²⁺ levels
#### Result
##### First pulse:
- Rapid Ca²⁺ rise from VGCC opening
- Partial clearance before second pulse
##### Second pulse:
- Additional Ca²⁺ influx occurs on top of residual Ca²⁺ from first pulse 
  = Elevated baseline Ca²⁺
- Higher peak residual Ca²⁺
- Larger EPSC/EPSP
- PPR > 1 (facilitation)

### 2. Ca<sup>2+</sup> Buffers
1. BAPTA = fast Ca²⁺ buffer - inhibits both responses
2. EGTA = slow Ca²⁺ buffer - inhibits only second responses
#### BAPTA
- Rapid Ca²⁺ binding kinetics
- Chelates Ca²⁺ almost immediately near channels
- Suppresses local Ca²⁺ microdomains
- Both immediate and residual Ca²⁺ signals are disrupted.
##### Observation:
- Both first and second EPSC greatly reduced
- PPF abolished or severely diminished
##### Interpretation:
- Ca²⁺ is essential for both release events
- Facilitation depends on rapid Ca²⁺ accumulation
#### EGTA
- Slower Ca²⁺ binding kinetics
- Does not strongly affect very local nanodomain Ca²⁺ near release machinery
- Preferentially buffers slower residual Ca²⁺
##### Observation:
- First EPSC relatively preserved
- Second EPSC selectively reduced
- PPR decreases toward 1
##### Interpretation:
Residual Ca²⁺, not first-release Ca²⁺, drives facilitation

### 3. Postsynaptic Receptor Desensitisation 
(Alternative Cause of Depression)
#### Process
- Measure paired pulses
- Apply receptor desensitisation blockers (depending on receptor type)
  e.g. Cyclothiazide prevents AMPA receptor desensitisation
#### Observation
- First response normal
- Second response depressed
- Depression reduced when desensitisation blocked
#### Interpretation:
Postsynaptic receptors become transiently less responsive after first pulse.
This mechanism is postsynaptic, not presynaptic.

### 4. Metabotropic Autoreceptor / Heteroreceptor Activation
(Alternative Cause of Depression)
e.g. 
- Presynaptic mGluRs
  → MCPG blocks mGluRs
- GABA<sub>B</sub> receptors
- Adenosine A1 receptors
#### Method
- Paired-pulse stimulation
- Apply autoreceptor/heteroreceptor antagonist
#### Observation
##### Without antagonist
- First stimulus releases transmitter
- Activates inhibitory metabotropic receptors
- Second release probability reduced
- PPR < 1 (depression)
##### With antagonist
- Depression reduced
- PPR increases
#### Interpretation
Feedback inhibition suppresses transmitter release on pulse 2.

### **IV. Linkage to Experimental Methods (Exam Focus)**

In an exam case study, you must demonstrate how mathematical models and electrophysiological configurations are utilized to distinguish between presynaptic and postsynaptic expressions of plasticity.

#### **1. Paired-Cell Recording and Unitary Amplitude Histograms**

To track the biological mechanisms underlying short-term variations, researchers perform hundreds of sequential recordings of interconnected neuronal pairs. They map the exact amplitude distribution of the first responses versus the second responses on a frequency histogram.

- **Isolating Presynaptic Changes ($\Delta p$ or $\Delta n$):**
    
    - **Statistical Profile:** If the modulation in synaptic strength is driven by a presynaptic alteraton (such as residual calcium boosting $p$, or vesicle depletion lowering $p$), the **peak heights (frequency of events)** across the amplitude histogram change dramatically.
        
    - **Failure Rates:** Modulating $p$ alters the mathematical rate of complete **synaptic failures** ($P_0 = (1-p)^n$). If a protocol decreases failures while increasing the overall frequency of larger multi-quantal peaks without shifting their position, the locus of expression is conclusively proven to be presynaptic ($\Delta p$).
        
- **Isolating Postsynaptic Changes ($\Delta q$):**
    
    - **Statistical Profile:** If the alteration is postsynaptic (such as a change in the average number of functional receptors or single-channel conductance at each synapse), the parameter modified is quantal size ($q$).
        
    - **Histogram Peak Shifting:** Because every single quantum now generates a larger or smaller current, the **physical positions of the peaks on the x-axis shift horizontally**. The baseline failure rate ($P_0$) remains perfectly static because the presynaptic release probability was unaltered, providing definitive diagnostic proof of a postsynaptic change ($\Delta q$).
        

#### **2. Structural and Analytical Limitations: The Reality of Quantal Analysis**

While theoretical models depict perfectly separated, clear multi-modal peaks corresponding to $1q, 2q, \dots$ values, the slides note that raw experimental data (e.g., historical data from Redman, 1990) reveals highly overlapping distributions. This blurriness arises due to:

- **Dendritic Filtering:** Postsynaptic currents generated at distant dendritic spines must travel through cables with inherent electrical resistance and capacitance, which filters, dampens, and distorts the wave before it reaches the somatic recording electrode.
    
- **Intrinsically High Biological Variance:** Subtle variations in vesicle diameters, baseline transmitter loads, and structural receptor cluster sizes across independent active zones introduce noise that spreads individual peaks into continuous, overlapping curves.
