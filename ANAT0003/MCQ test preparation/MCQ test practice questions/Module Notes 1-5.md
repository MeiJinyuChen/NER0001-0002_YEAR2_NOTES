8. **a** — Astrocytes provide energetic support.

10. **a** — The notes list interactions with microglia and oligodendrocytes.  
11. **b** — Microglia are yolk-sac-derived, not bone-marrow-derived.  
12. **a** — Their precursors enter the CNS early in development.  
13. **b** — Ramified = surveillant.  
14. **c** — Amoeboid = phagocytic.  
15. **b** — Activation changes ramified microglia to amoeboid microglia.  
16. **a** — Programmed cell death is listed.  
17. **a** — Microglia prune synapses.  
18. **a** — Microglia support neuronal plasticity.  
19. **a** — Inflammation is a listed dysfunction outcome.  
20. **a** — Amyloid plaques are a listed dysfunction outcome.  
21. **a** — Excessive synaptic pruning is the relevant outcome.  
22. **c** — Müller glia are in the retina.  
23. **b** — Amoeboid microglia are phagocytic.  
24. **b** — Bergmann glia are in the cerebellum.  
25. **b** — Phagocytosis is the amoeboid microglial stage.  
26. **d** — Direct muscle innervation is a motor-neuron role.  
27. **a** — Both are linked to neuronal/synaptic plasticity in the notes.  
28. **a** — Glutamate clearance is an astrocytic synaptic-regulation role.  
29. **a** — Ramified refers to the surveillant state.  
30. **a** — The developmental source is the yolk sac.  
31. **b** — Neuropil comprises dendrites, axons and synapses; white matter is axon-rich and commonly myelinated.  
32. **b** — Dendrites receive and sum inputs; the soma supports biosynthesis and integration.  
33. **b** — Axon-hillock action potentials are all-or-none; transport can be anterograde or retrograde.  
34. **c** — Glutamatergic excitatory synapses generally have a thick PSD and are common on dendritic spines.

## Cross-lesson high-yield checks

- **CNS vs PNS wording:** tracts are in the CNS; nerves are in the PNS. Do not confuse this with the ANS “PNS” abbreviation for *parasympathetic nervous system* in the Lesson 5 notes.
- **Two major crossings:** lateral CST crosses in caudal medulla; DCML crosses in upper closed medulla; spinothalamic crosses early in the spinal cord.
- **Three fluids/barriers:** choroid plexus makes most CSF and contributes to the blood–CSF barrier; arachnoid granulations absorb CSF into dural sinuses; BBB includes endothelial tight junctions, astrocytes, pericytes and basement membrane.
- **ANS exception worth memorising:** sympathetic postganglionic fibres usually release noradrenaline, but those to sweat glands release ACh.
- **Functional pairing:** sexual intercourse requires contributions from both sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions.
