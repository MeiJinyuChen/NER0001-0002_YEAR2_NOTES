## Lesson 6 — Cranial Nerves

1. Which statement about the cranial nerves is correct?

   a. There are 12 unpaired cranial nerves, all of which attach to the spinal cord.  
   b. There are 12 bilaterally paired cranial nerves; CN I and II attach to the forebrain, whereas the remaining nerves attach to the brainstem.

   d. Cranial nerves are numbered by the size of their peripheral territory.  
   e. Only sensory cranial nerves attach to the brainstem.


2. <span style="background:#fff88f">Which pairing of functional category and example is correct?</span>

   a. General visceral efferent — facial-expression muscles derived from pharyngeal arches.  
   b. Special visceral afferent — voluntary skeletal-muscle movement.  
   c. General somatic afferent — endocrine output to viscera.  
   d. Special somatic afferent — vision, audition, olfaction or vestibular sensation.  
   e. Special visceral efferent — taste.

   c. CN I and II relay first through the nucleus solitarius.  
   d. CN VIII carries only taste and parasympathetic fibres.

   e. Oculomotor nucleus — midbrain at superior-colliculus level; trochlear nucleus — midbrain at inferior-colliculus level; abducens nucleus — caudal pons.

6. <span style="background:#fff88f">Which statement about the trigeminal nerve (CN V) is correct?</span>

   a. It is a purely sensory nerve for the anterior tongue.  
   b. It supplies muscles of facial expression from the facial nucleus.

### Answers and review points — Lesson 6

1. **b** — CN I/II attach to forebrain; the remainder attach to brainstem.  
2. **d** — SSA conveys the named special senses; SVE drives pharyngeal-arch muscles.  
3. **a** — I, II and VIII are solely special sensory afferent nerves.  
4. **e** — These three locations are a key brainstem-localisation set.  
5. **c** — III supplies all extraocular muscles except SO (IV) and LR (VI), and mediates pupillary constriction.  
6. **b** — CN V is mixed: head GSA and mastication SVE.  
7. **d** — This is the exceptional CNS site of primary sensory somata.  
8. **a** — Principal = touch; spinal trigeminal = pain.  
9. **e** — Supratrigeminal circuitry supplies automatic masticatory rhythm.  
10. **c** — Demyelinated fibres can transmit ephaptically after vascular compression.  
11. **b** — CN VII has separate motor and nervus-intermedius roots at cerebellopontine angle.  
12. **d** — VII carries anterior-tongue taste and the listed parasympathetic functions.  
13. **a** — Bilateral upper-face corticobulbar input explains relative upper-face sparing in supranuclear lesions.  
14. **e** — IX and X share all four listed categories.  
15. **c** — Nucleus solitarius integrates taste and visceral sensory feedback.  
16. **b** — CN IX links posterior-tongue taste, carotid sensing and parotid secretion.  
17. **d** — Nucleus ambiguus supplies SVE; dorsal motor nucleus supplies widespread vagal GVE.  
18. **a** — Nucleus-solitarius connections recruit raphe and locus-coeruleus neuromodulation.  
19. **e** — The spinal accessory course explains head/shoulder motor function.  
20. **c** — CN XII is the motor nerve of the tongue.

11. **e** — PPT/LDT ACh projections omit spinal cord and cerebellum.  
12. **c** — SNc and VTA are rostral-projecting dopamine nuclei, not RF.  
13. **b** — ARAS activation promotes arousal.  
14. **d** — PPT/parabrachial glutamate promotes arousal; parafacial GABA promotes sleep.  
15. **a** — CPGs automate patterned actions under higher control.  
16. **e** — MLR drives locomotor/postural reticulospinal circuitry.  
17. **c** — Inspiration and expiration are separated into dorsal and ventral respiratory nuclei.  
18. **b** — Pontine output coordinates S2–S4 parasympathetic bladder emptying.  
19. **d** — MRN recruits spinal inhibitory interneurons through 5-HT.  
20. **a** — Disinhibition of PAG permits recruitment of MRN-mediated analgesia.
