8. **c** — Phase locking declines with frequency and is strongest below about 3 kHz.
9. **b** — Cochlear implants bypass normal mechanical transduction to stimulate auditory nerve fibres.
10. **a** — VCN is fast/precise and projects to superior olive; DCN has complex responses and projects to inferior colliculus.
11. **d** — ITDs are most useful at low frequencies; ILDs at high frequencies.
12. **d** — The LSO combines ipsilateral excitation with contralateral inhibition to extract ILDs.
13. **e** — IC is an obligatory ascending integration station, including MSO and LSO information.
14. **a** — Ventral MGN is the primary-auditory transmission division.
15. **e** — Human primary auditory cortex lies in Heschl’s gyri.
16. **c** — Core is primary-thalamic and tonotopic; parabelt has no thalamic input and is non-tonotopic.
17. **d** — The highlighted lateralisation is pitch direction on the right and sound duration on the left.
18. **c** — LOC modulates auditory-nerve sensitivity and MOC cochlear amplification.

### Answers and review points

1. **c** — Neurodegeneration is progressive neuronal dysfunction/death with worsening clinical deficits.
2. **d** — AD and PD become increasingly common with age.
3. **e** — AD is defined pathologically by amyloid plaques and neurofibrillary tangles.
4. **e** — MCI has relatively preserved independence; dementia compromises independent daily function.
5. **c** — APOE-4 is a notable less-penetrant genetic risk factor for old-age AD.
6. **b** — Aβ derives from APP.
7. **d** — Only the β-secretase-first APP processing pathway produces Aβ.
8. **c** — Aβ42 is particularly aggregation-prone.
9. **e** — The slide describes temporal/prefrontal onset, cortical spread and later subcortical involvement.
10. **c** — Seeding after inoculation with AD brain extracts supports a possible prion-like mechanism.
11. **b** — Tau stabilises axonal microtubules.
12. **d** — Hyperphosphorylated tau disengages from microtubules and accumulates from monomers to oligomers to tangles.
13. **a** — Tau begins in entorhinal cortex, reaches hippocampus/limbic areas, then neocortex.
14. **a** — Plaques precede symptoms and can occur without symptoms; tau progression tracks clinical progression better.
15. **d** — The lecture lists tau PET, CSF phosphorylated tau and Aβ42 as prodromal biomarkers.
16. **a** — PD classically includes rest tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, hunched posture and soft speech.
17. **b** — SN dopamine cells are lost while VTA is relatively preserved early in motor PD.
18. **e** — Alpha-synuclein aggregates form oligomers, fibrils and large aggregates including Lewy bodies.
19. **a** — Reduced dopamine promotes D2/indirect no-go activity and suppresses movement.
20. **b** — Levodopa is the motor-treatment standard but has limited duration and important side effects.
