
**Relationship between Aβ plaques and Tau in the pathology of Alzheimer's disease**
- Both Aβ plaques and Tau pathology contribute to Alzheimer’s disease, but Tau pathology alone is not sufficient to spread efficiently through the brain.
- Emerging consensus:
	- Current evidence suggests that Aβ plaques enhance Tau pathology by helping Tau tangles spread from one neuron to another through synaptic connections.

# **1. Definition & Overview**

## **1.1 What is Neurodegenerative Disease**
- Progressive loss of:
    - Neuronal function
    - Neuronal viability (cell death)
- Leads to **progressively worsening clinical symptoms**:
    - Motor dysfunction
    - Cognitive decline
    - Sensory deficits
## **1.2 Major Neurodegenerative Diseases**
- Alzheimer’s disease (AD)
- Parkinson’s disease (PD)
- Huntington’s disease
- Frontotemporal dementia
- Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)
- Multiple sclerosis
- Progressive supranuclear palsy

-----

# **2. Core Principles of Neurodegeneration**

## **2.1 Key Mechanisms**
- Misfolded protein accumulation → **cellular toxicity**
- Neurodegeneration is:
    - **Progressive**
    - **Anatomically specific**
## **2.2 Structure–Function Relationship**
- Clinical symptoms depend on **which brain regions are affected**
## **2.3 General Features**
- Age = **primary risk factor**
- Most diseases:
    - Not curable
    - Some have symptomatic treatments (e.g. PD)

# **3. Alzheimer’s Disease (AD)**

## **3.1 Core Symptoms**
- Memory loss
- Disorientation
- Communication deficits
- Emotional dysregulation
- Loss of motivation/movement
## **3.2 Core Pathology**
- Two hallmark features:
    1. **Amyloid plaques (Aβ)**
	    - Extracellular accumulation of misfolded Aβ protein
    2. **Neurofibrillary tangles (Tau)** 
	    - Intracellular accumulation of abnormal Tau protein
## **3.3 Clinical Progression 
### Stages
1. **Preclinical**
    - Very mild lost in episodic memory
    - No obvious brain pathology
2. **Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI)**
    - Mild, accelerated impairment in ≥1 cognitive domain
    - Daily function preserved
3. **Dementia**
    - Severe impairment
    - Loss of independence
## **3.4 Risk Factors & Causes**
- **Age** (major risk; ~70+)
- **AD genes**
    - Rare ( ~5%) + highly penetrant + dominantly inherited → Early-onset
	    - Produce more **Aβ42** by affect either:
	    1.  **γ-secretase (Presenilin 1 & 2: significant part of γ-secretase)** = an enzyme that is responsible of cleaving APP
	    2. OR changes in APP (Amyloid Precursor Protein) cleavage → tendancy to be cleaved into Aβ42
    - Less penetrant → Increased risk of old-age AD
	    - e.g.  APOE-4 → impair Aβ clearance → promote Aβ aggregation
- **Health conditions**
    - Diabetes
    - Hypertension
    - Stroke history
    
- <font color="#245bdb"> Non-amyloidogenic pathway:</font>
  <font color="#245bdb">APP → (cleaved by α-secretase) → C83 fragment →  (cleaved by γ-secretase) → non-toxic fragments</font>.
- <font color="#245bdb">Amyloidogenic pathway:</font>
  <font color="#245bdb">APP → (cleaved by β-secretase) → C99 fragment →  (cleaved by γ-secretase) → Aβ peptides (including Aβ 42)</font>.
- γ-secretase cleaves the C99 fragment of APP to produce Aβ peptides of different lengths, mainly Aβ40 and Aβ42. Aβ42 is more prone to aggregation because its extra two amino acids increase its hydrophobicity, making it more likely to form amyloid plaques.

## **3.5 Late-stage Pathology**
- Extensive neuronal death
- Brain atrophy (especially limbic & association cortex)
- Leads to:
    - Cognitive failure
    - Motor impairment

# **4. Amyloid β (Aβ) Pathway**

## **4.1 Origin**
- Derived from **Amyloid Precursor Protein (APP)**
## **4.2 Processing Pathways**
- APP cleaved into an intra- and extra-cellular portion
- Two pathways:
    - α-secretase pathway (non-amyloidogenic  = safe)
    - **β-secretase pathway → produces Aβ**
## **4.3 Key Properties**
- Suspected to be acting as a prion protein (can act as a catalyst for further misfolding)
  <font color="#245bdb">A prion protein is a misfolded protein that can induce normal proteins of the same type to also misfold and become abnormal</font>.
- Aβ exists different lengths
	- Aβ42: especially prone to aggregation and form plaques
- Forms:
    - **Aβ monomers → Aβ oligomers → protofibrils → amyloid fibrils→ amyloid plaques**
## **4.4 Functional Notes**
- Normal role unclear:
    - Released at synapses
    - Shows circadian change in levels
    - Cleared via glymphatic system
## **4.5 Toxic Effects**
- Plaques + soluble Aβ:
    - Synaptic dysfunction
    - Neurotoxicity
## 4.6 Spread of Aβ
- Temporal + mPFC → Whole neocortex + Thalamus → Other subcortical regions (brain stem, cerebellum)

# **5. Tau Pathology**

- Not so characteristically unique to AD. Can be seen in and other diseases such as demantia.
- Also found in non-symptomatic brains → Necessary but not sufficient for AD
## **5.1 Normal Function**
- Tau (micro-tubule associated protein Tau):
    - Bind and stabilises microtubules (especially in axons)
## **5.2 Pathological Changes**
- Hyperphosphorylation of microtubules:
    - Microtubule destabilised → Release soluble Tau monomers → Tau oligomers accumulates in cytosol → **Neurofibrillary tangles** (cause cell death → aggregate in dead cell)
- Not known genetic factor
## 5.4 Spread of Tau 
- Entorhinal cortex + Hippocampus → Limbic areas (temporal + mPFC) → Whole neocortex
	- Hippocampus → early memory lost
- Mechanism:
    - Likely **synaptic transmission**

# **6. Evidence from Animal Models**

## 6.1 Aβ Models 
- APP mutation → ↑ Aβ
- Findings:
    - Hippocampal plaques → impaired place cells
    - Spatial memory deficits
## 6.2 Tau Models 
- Mutant Tau (P301L)
- Findings:
    - Hippocampal shrinkage
    - Disrupted place cell activity
## 6.3 Cognitive Impairment 
- Early AD:
    - Impaired **spatial memory/imagination**
- Linked to:
    - Entorhinal cortex + hippocampus dysfunction

# **8. Parkinson’s Disease (PD)**

## **8.1 Clinical Features**
### **Motor Symptoms**
- Rest tremor
- Rigidity
- Bradykinesia
- Postural instability
- Soft speech
## **8.2 Core Pathology**
- Loss of **dopaminergic neurons** in:
    - Substantia nigra (SNpc)
- Presence of:
    - Abnormal aggregation of **α-synuclein (classifically present as Lewy bodies)**
## 8.3 α-Synuclein 
### Native function
- Not known
- Cytosolic protein
- Possibly involved in intracellular trafficking, synaptic function
### **Aggregation Process**
- α-synuclein monomers → oligomers → fibrils → Lewy bodies
### **Possible Causes of Misfolding**
- Overproduction
- Mutations
- Impaired protein clearance
## 8.4 Spread Mechanism 
- Prion-like propagation:
    - Between neurons
- Evidence:
    - Injected into olfactory bulb → spread to connected areas
    - dorsal motor nucleus of vagus nerve → (via vagus nerve) → almost all viscera, including ENS
## 8.5 Clinical Progression
![[Pasted image 20260412165155.png]]
### **Stages**
1. **Prodromal**
    - Non-motor symptoms
2. **Early**
    - Motor symptoms appear
3. **Late**
    - Severe motor dysfunction
    - Dementia
## 8.6 Anatomical Progression 
1. Medulla & pons → prodromal symptoms
2. Midbrain (SNpc) → motor symptoms
3. Cortex (temporal/limbic) → dementia
	Dopaminergic cells also lost in VTA

# **9. Dopamine & Basal Ganglia**

## **9.1 Pathways**

- **Direct (D1)** → facilitates movement
- **Indirect (D2)** → inhibits unwanted movement
## **9.2 PD Mechanism**
- ↓ Dopamine → ↑ indirect pathway
- Result:
    - **Reduced motor cortex activity**
    - **Movement inhibition**

---

# **10. Therapies for PD**
## **10.1 L-DOPA**
- Dopamine precursor
- Most effective treatment
### **Limitations**
- Dyskinesia
- Compulsive behavious (e.g. gambling, risk taking, sexual compulsions)
- Time limited response
## **10.2 Deep Brain Stimulation (STN)**
- Excellent improvement of motor control
### **Limitations**
- Surgical risks (non-zero morbidity & mortality rate)
- Time limited efficacy

**Common Themes**
- Protein misfolding:
    - AD → Aβ + Tau
    - PD → α-synuclein
- Prion-like spread
- Age-related
- Progressive neuronal loss


**Key Differences**

| Feature       | Alzheimer’s        | Parkinson’s   |
| ------------- | ------------------ | ------------- |
| Main symptom  | Cognitive          | Motor         |
| Key region    | Cortex/hippocampus | Basal ganglia |
| Protein       | Aβ + Tau           | α-synuclein   |
| Early deficit | Memory             | Movement      |
