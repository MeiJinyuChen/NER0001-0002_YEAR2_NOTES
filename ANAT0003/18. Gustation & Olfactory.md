
# 1. Chemical sensing, qualia & language

## 1.1 Concepts
- Tastants = chemical molecules detected by taste receptors
- Odourants = chemical molecules detected by olfactory receptors
- Flavour = integrated percept of:
	- Taste (gustation)
	- Smell (olfaction)
	- Other oral sensations
## 1.2 Qualia Problem
- Language is imprecise and metaphorical
- Subjective sensory experience (**qualia**) limits classification
- Attempts (Henning, Amoore, etc.) to categorise odours are:
	- Incomplete
	- Not universally agreed
## 1.3 Chemical Composition of Odours
- Real odours = complex chemical mixtures.

# 2. Gustation (Taste)

## **2.1 Taste Bud Structure**
![[Pasted image 20260412101403.png]]
- Each taste bud contains:
    - **20–40** taste cells
- Features:
    - Tight junctions at taste pore → protection
    - **Turnover ~10 days**
    - Maintained by basal stem cells

## **2.2 Basic Taste Modalities**

### **Five widely recognised tastes**
1. Salty
2. Sour
3. Bitter
4. Sweet
5. Umami (savoury)

## 2.3 Taste transduction mechanisms
- **Salty** → Na⁺ channels (ENaC)
- **Sour** → H⁺ (proton-sensitive channels)
- **Sweet / Umami / Bitter** → GPCRs:
    - Sweet: <font color="#245bdb">T1R2 + T1R3</font>
    - Umami: <font color="#245bdb">T1R1 + T1R3</font>
    - Bitter: <font color="#245bdb">T2Rs</font>
    
## 2.4 No “Taste Map” 
- All taste qualities are:
    - **Widely distributed across the tongue**
- Classical tongue map = **incorrect**

## 2.5 Cellular Organisation & Signalling 
### **Cell types**
- **Type I**: glial-like support cells
- **Type II**: taste receptor cells → release ATP (non-vesicular) onto sensory afferents
- **Type III**: taste receptor cells → synaptic release 5-HT onto sensory afferents
### Other Cells
- Taste bud acts as a **functional processing unit**
    - Autocrine + paracrine cells signalling within taste bud

## 2.6 Combinatorial coding in taste 
- Miraculin:
    - Acts on sweet receptors (T1R2/T1R3)
    - Converts sour → sweet perception
- → Suggests:
    - Interactions within taste buds
    - Possible combinatorial coding

## 2.7 Non-taste oral sensations 
- Not mediated by taste receptors:
### **Cranial nerves involved**
- CN V (Trigeminal)
- CN IX (Glossopharyngeal)
- CN X (Vagus)
### Functions:
- Temperature
- Pain (spicy, cooling)
- Texture

## **2.8 Central gustatory pathway**

### **Peripheral → central flow**
![[Pasted image 20260412105154.png|400]]

## **3.1 Olfactory Epithelium**

### Cell types:
![[Pasted image 20260412111627.png|450]]
- **ORN = OSN** (olfactory receptor neurons)
	- Lifetime: around **40** days
- OEC (olfactory ensheathing cells)
	- Guide OSN axons from olfactory epithelium to olfactory bulbs<font color="#245bdb"> (fasciculation + reach correct gomeruli)</font>
	
## **4.2 Olfactory receptors 
- Mostly **GPCRs**
- Gene (receptor type) numbers:
    - Mice: ~1300
    - Humans: <500
- NOT 1 receptor : 1 odour 
    → Combinatorial coding
    
## **4.3 OSN organisation 
![[Pasted image 20260412132826.png|225]]
### Fundamental rules:
- Each OSN expresses **ONE receptor**
- OSNs with same receptor:
	- Fasciculate to form olfactory nerves
    - Project to **same glomerulus (1–2)**
- → **Spatial organisation in bulb**

## **4.4 Olfactory bulb structure 

![[Pasted image 20260412133855.png|425]]
![[Pasted image 20260412134457.png|475]]
### Glomeruli
- Dense neuropil capsules outside of olfactory bulb
- No cell bodies
### Afferent
- OSN axons
### Efferent -- to brain
- Mitral cells
- Tufted cells
### Other cells
- Periglomerular dendrites
- Juxtaglomerular dendrites
- Astrocyte
### Quantitative features:
- ~2000 glomeruli
- ~6 million OSNs
- Each glomerulus:
    - Receives **1500–3000 OSNs**
    - Connects to **10–50 mitral cells**
→ **Strong convergence**

## 4.5 Coding of odours 
- Simple odours → **sparse activation**
- Natural odours → **dense patterns**
### Multi-level coding:
1. Receptor level
2. OSN level
3. Glomerulus
4. Mitral/tufted cells
→ **Graded (not binary) responses**
