# 1. Early History of Limbic System

Three interconnected circuits within the limbic system:
- Memory (hippocampus) 
- Emotion (amygdala) 
- Cingulate/Insula: internal state sensation and value attribution?

## 1.1 Early Concepts

### Broca (1878) — “Limbic lobe”

- Linked to olfaction (“primitive / bestial” sense)
- Considered part of a primitive brain (reduced in primates)

### Jakob (1907) — “Visceral brain”

- Cingulate gyrus receives visceral + muscular inputs
- Proposed role in viseceral regulation

## 1.2 Papez Circuit (1937) — First Emotional Circuit

### Core idea:

→ Limbic structures **mediate emotion**

### Circuit flow:

- Emotional stimulus → Thalamus
- → Hypothalamus (mammillary bodies) → **Bodily response**
- → Anterior thalamus → Cingulate cortex → **Feeling (conscious emotion)**
- → Hippocampus → back to thalamus

## 1.3 Kluver–Bucy Syndrome (Temporal Lobectomy)

### Symptoms:

- Docility (↓ aggression)
- Hypo-emotionality
- Hypersexuality
- Visual agnosia
- Hyperorality
- Amnesia

- **Amygdala damage → emotional deficits**
- **Hippocampus damage → memory deficits**

## 1.4 MacLean (Triune Brain Theory)

### Three systems:

1. **Reptilian brain (Basal ganglia)** → innate behaviour
2. **Paleomammalian (Limbic system)** → emotion
3. **Neomammalian (Neocortex)** → cognition

## 1.5 Limitations of Limbic System Concept

- Hippocampus ≠ emotional centre (primarily memory)
-  “Triune brain” is evolutionarily incorrect
	- Brain evolution = **modification, not layering**
- Taking collection of structures without known function OR inventing function for them

## 3.1.1 Septo-Hippocampal Modulation

**Medial septum** 
-  Medial septum acts as a **pacemaker/modulator** that controls hippocampal theta oscillations → limbic memory circuit
- Generate two outputs to **hippocampus**:
    - **GABAergic** → synchronises pyramidal neuron firing in HPC circuits + regulates theta timing
    - **Cholinergic (ACh)** → promotes hippocampal encoding mode ( ↑ hippocampal excitability + ↑ sensory input processing ) during active exploration and attention
- medial septum → fornix → hippocampus → fornix → mamillry bodies

## 3.1.2 Head Direction

- HD cells present in particular limbic areas: 
	- Parahippocampal cortex, lateral mammillary nucleus, anterior dorsal nucleus
- HD signals pathway:
	- Vestibular nuclei → Brainstem nuclei (dorsal tegmental nuclei) → (LMN → mammillothalamic tract → ADN) → Limbic system
- Signals enter via:
    - Lateral mammillary nucleus (LMN)
    - Anterior dorsal nucleus (ADN)

## 3.1.3 Clinical: Diencephalic Amnesia

### Korsakoff Syndrome:

- Cause: Vitamin B1 deficiency (alcoholism)
- Damage:
    - Mammillary bodies
    - Anterior thalamus

### Symptoms:

- Severe **anterograde amnesia**
- Mild retrograde amnesia

# 3.2 Emotional Circuit (Amygdala)

## 3.2.1 Functional Role

- Core structure for:
    - Emotion processing
    - Fear learning
    - Behavioural responses

## 3.2.2 Amygdala Subdivisions

Olfactory amygdala + Amygdala emotional circuit (CeA & BLA)

### 1. Olfactory amygdala

- Less prominent in primates
- <font color="#245bdb">Components: Cortical nuclei + Medial nucleus</font>
- Inputs: olfactory cortex
- Outputs: hypothalamus
- Role: Recognize odours for motivated (social/reproductive/aggressive) behaviours

### 2. Central nucleus (CeA)

- **Role: Output station of amygdala**
	- Mechanism: Disinhibition of downstream areas
- Primarily **GABAergic** output neurons
- Diverse inputs: 
    - Sensory cortex
    - PFC
    - Hippocampus
    - Hypothalamus
    - Brain stem
    - **BLA** (most importantly)
- Outputs:
    - Hypothalamus
    - Brainstem  
    - Autonomic centres

### 3. Basolateral complex (BLA)

- **Role: Associative system for learning emotional value of stimuli**
- Primarily **excitatory pyramidal neurons**
	- The same as neurons in L3/5 neocortex
- Inputs: all cortical regions (inc. hippocampus)
- Outputs:
    - PFC
    - Hippocampus
    - Nucleus accumbens
    - **CeA**  
    
## 3.2.3 Fear Conditioning Circuit

### Mechanism:

- Conditioned + Unconditioned stimuli converge in **BLA** → Associative learning occurs
- → BLA signals sent to **CeA** → Hypothalamus / brain stem → Mediate defensive behaviours

### Outputs:

- PAG → motor pattern generator for defensive & escape behaviours
- Hypothalamus → sympathetic ANS activation
- PVN (paraventricular nucleus) → cortisol (via ACTH)
- Parabrachial nucleus → respiration ↑
- Locus coeruleus → NA ↑ → arousal

## 3.2.4 Beyond Fear

- Importance of attending to eyes in face expression recognition
- Possible role of some amygdala neurons: 
	- Recognizing emotion-relevant stimuli (e.g. face expression)
- Threat detection (e.g. threatening animals)

## 3.2.5 Clinical: Urbah-Wiethe disease

- Genetic defect
	-  → Bilateral amygdala damage
- Deficits:
    - Cannot recognize fear
    - Social vulnerability

# Example Questions

## 1. Overview

The limbic system is a distributed network of cortical and subcortical structures that integrates memory, emotion, and internal physiological states to generate adaptive behaviour. Rather than a single anatomical structure, it consists of interconnected circuits that link cognitive processes with emotional and bodily responses. A central organising principle is that behaviour arises from the integration of three core functional domains: **memory (hippocampus), emotion (amygdala), and internal state/value (cingulate cortex and insula)** .

Historically, the limbic system was first described by Broca as the “limbic lobe,” and later conceptualised as a “visceral brain” by Jakob, reflecting its role in processing internal bodily states. The first formal circuit model was proposed by Papez, who described a loop linking the thalamus, hypothalamus, cingulate cortex, and hippocampus. In this circuit, an emotional stimulus is processed by the thalamus, which sends signals to the hypothalamus to generate bodily responses, and to the anterior thalamus and cingulate cortex to produce conscious emotional experience. The hippocampus then feeds back to the thalamus, linking emotion with memory . This model established the principle that emotional experience involves both physiological responses and cortical processing.

Anatomically, the limbic system includes core cortical structures such as the **insula, cingulate gyrus and parahippocampal gyrus**, as well as subcortical structures including the **hippocampus and amygdala**, with associated regions such as the olfactory cortex and prefrontal areas. These structures form interconnected circuits that support distinct but overlapping functions.

The limbic system can be functionally divided into **three major circuits**. The first is the **memory circuit centred on the hippocampus**. The hippocampus receives highly processed multimodal input via the parahippocampal cortex and is essential for episodic memory formation. A key modulatory system is the **septo-hippocampal pathway**, in which the **medial septum** generates theta rhythms that regulate hippocampal activity. This modulation involves GABAergic inputs that act as a timing pacemaker and cholinergic inputs that place the hippocampus into an encoding mode during exploration . The hippocampus projects via the fornix to other limbic structures, forming part of a wider memory network.

The memory circuit also incorporates spatial processing through **head direction (HD) signals**, which are encoded in regions such as the parahippocampal cortex, lateral mammillary nucleus, and anterior dorsal thalamic nucleus. These signals originate from vestibular inputs in the brainstem and are transmitted through mammillary and thalamic pathways to the limbic system, contributing to spatial navigation. Clinically, damage to this circuit, as seen in **Korsakoff syndrome** due to <font color="#c00000">vitamin B1 deficiency</font>, affects the mammillary bodies and anterior thalamus, resulting in severe anterograde amnesia and mild retrograde amnesia .

The second major circuit is the **emotional circuit centred on the amygdala**. The amygdala consists of several subdivisions with distinct roles. The **basolateral complex (BLA)** acts as an<font color="#c00000"> associative learning system, integrating sensory</font> inputs from the cortex, hippocampus, and prefrontal cortex to <font color="#c00000">assign emotional value to stimuli</font>. The **central nucleus (CeA)** serves as the <font color="#c00000">main output structure</font>, sending <font color="#c00000">GABAergic projections to the hypothalamus and brainstem</font> to generate <font color="#c00000">behavioural and autonomic responses</font>. The amygdala also includes an olfactory component that links odour processing to motivated behaviours such as social interaction and reproduction .

A key mechanism of the amygdala is **fear conditioning**, in which conditioned and unconditioned stimuli converge in the BLA to form associative memories. The BLA then signals the CeA, which activates downstream pathways: the <font color="#c00000">periaqueductal grey </font>generates <font color="#c00000">defensive behaviours</font>, the <font color="#c00000">hypothalamus </font>activates the<font color="#c00000"> sympathetic nervous system</font> and <font color="#c00000">hormonal responses</font> via the paraventricular nucleus, the <font color="#c00000">parabrachial nucleus</font> increases <font color="#c00000">respiration</font>, and the <font color="#c00000">locus coeruleus</font> enhances<font color="#c00000"> arousal</font> through noradrenaline release . Beyond fear, the amygdala is involved in detecting emotionally relevant stimuli, such as facial expressions and threats.

Clinical evidence further highlights limbic system function. For example, **Klüver–Bucy syndrome**, resulting from temporal lobe damage, is characterised by docility, reduced emotionality, hypersexuality, and visual agnosia, demonstrating the importance of limbic structures in emotional and behavioural regulation. Similarly, damage to the amygdala impairs fear recognition and increases social vulnerability.

In summary, the limbic system is a highly integrated network that combines hippocampal memory processing, amygdala-based emotional evaluation, and cortical representation of internal states to guide behaviour. Through its interconnected circuits, it links sensory experience with emotion, memory, and physiological responses, enabling adaptive responses to the environment.
